## In one line

An apprentice in England is a full employee with a written apprenticeship agreement, and gets the same core rights as any other employee — paid holiday, sick pay, paid training time, protection from discrimination — plus specific apprenticeship rights to a training plan and a set number of off-the-job training hours.

## Key facts

- An apprentice in England must have an apprenticeship agreement. It is a contract of employment, and without one the apprenticeship cannot be funded.[^r1][^r5]
- In law, an approved English apprenticeship agreement is treated as a contract of service, not as an old-style common-law contract of apprenticeship.[^r4]
- Apprentices are entitled to at least 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year — 28 days for someone working five days a week, including bank holidays if the employer counts them within the entitlement.[^r8][^r9]
- Statutory Sick Pay is payable from the first day of sickness absence from 6 April 2026, at the lower of £123.25 a week or 80% of average weekly earnings, with no minimum earnings threshold.[^r11][^r22]
- Off-the-job training is paid working time, and since 1 August 2025 the requirement is a minimum number of hours published for each apprenticeship standard rather than a flat "20% of the time" or "6 hours a week" rule.[^r3][^r7]
- Apprentices aged under 18 must not work more than 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week, and get a 30-minute break after 4.5 hours.[^r12][^r13]
- Apprentices are protected by the Equality Act 2010, and employers and training providers must make reasonable adjustments.[^r15][^r21]
- Statutory redundancy pay requires two years' service, but if an apprentice is made redundant the Department for Education will normally keep funding the training for at least 12 weeks while they find a new employer.[^r2][^r14]
- Complaints escalate in a fixed order: employer or provider first, then the Department for Education, and the escalation must reach DfE within 12 months of the issue.[^r18][^r19]

## What is the apprenticeship agreement and what must it contain?

The apprenticeship agreement is the contract between an apprentice and their employer. It is a legal requirement for an English apprenticeship, and the apprenticeship cannot be funded without one. The employer and the apprentice both sign it; the training provider is not a party to it.[^r1][^r5]

The agreement must set out the standard being worked towards, the sector, and the training the apprentice will receive to achieve it.[^r4] Employers can write their own or use the template published through the apprenticeship service.[^r5]

Legally the key point is that an approved English apprenticeship agreement is treated as a contract of service — an ordinary employment contract — rather than a traditional contract of apprenticeship.[^r4] The practical consequence is covered under dismissal below.

## What is the training plan and how is it different from the apprenticeship agreement?

The training plan, previously called the commitment statement, is a separate three-way document signed by the apprentice, the employer and the training provider. It records what training will be delivered, when, by whom, and how much off-the-job training is required.[^r5]

The apprenticeship agreement is about employment. The training plan is about training. An apprentice should hold copies of both. If the training being delivered does not match the training plan, that document is the evidence to raise it with.

**GTOD take:** ask for both documents in writing before you start, and keep them somewhere you can find them. Almost every apprenticeship dispute we hear about turns on whether the training actually delivered matches what was signed.

## How much holiday does an apprentice get?

Apprentices get the same statutory holiday as any other employee: at least 5.6 weeks of paid annual leave a year.[^r8][^r9]

- Working five days a week: 28 days a year (5 × 5.6). Statutory paid holiday is capped at 28 days.[^r9]
- Working three days a week: 16.8 days a year (3 × 5.6).[^r9]
- Leave starts building up from the first day of the job, accruing at one-twelfth of the annual entitlement per month in the first year under an accrual system.[^r9]

Bank holidays are not a separate legal entitlement. An employer may count them within the 5.6 weeks, so check the contract.[^r8]

College or university days are working days, not holiday. An employer cannot require an apprentice to use annual leave to attend required training.[^r3]

## Do apprentices get sick pay?

Yes. Apprentices are employees, so Statutory Sick Pay applies on the same terms as for any other employee.[^r1][^r10]

From 6 April 2026 two changes make SSP substantially more useful to low-paid apprentices:[^r11][^r22]

- SSP is payable from the first full day of sickness absence, rather than from the fourth day.
- The lower earnings limit has been removed, so there is no minimum weekly earnings threshold to qualify.

The amount is the lower of £123.25 a week or 80% of average weekly earnings, calculated over the eight weeks before the absence, and it is payable for up to 28 weeks.[^r11][^r22]

Many employers pay contractual sick pay above SSP. That comes from the contract, not the law, so check the apprenticeship agreement or staff handbook.

## What is the off-the-job training entitlement and what counts towards it?

Off-the-job training is training that takes place within the apprentice's normal paid working hours and teaches new knowledge, skills or behaviours relevant to the apprenticeship standard. It is paid working time and the employer must pay for it, whether it happens at work, at a college, at a university or elsewhere.[^r3][^r6]

The rule changed on 1 August 2025. For apprenticeships starting from that date, each apprenticeship standard has its own published minimum number of off-the-job training hours. This replaced the earlier requirements expressed as a percentage of working time or as a flat minimum of six hours a week.[^r7]

Other current rules from the 2025 to 2026 funding rules:[^r7]

- The minimum practical period for an apprenticeship is 8 months, reduced from 12 months.
- A programme with less than 8 months or fewer than 187 hours of remaining content is not eligible for apprenticeship funding.
- Providers no longer have to automatically extend the duration for apprentices working fewer than 30 hours a week, but must still set a duration that realistically delivers the required training hours.

Things that broadly do not count as off-the-job training include training on things not in the apprenticeship standard, progress reviews and assessment activity, and training delivered outside paid working hours without corresponding paid time.[^r6]

## What are the rules on working hours for apprentices?

Adult apprentices are covered by the ordinary Working Time Regulations, including the 48-hour average weekly limit unless they have opted out, and the standard rest break entitlements.

Apprentices aged under 18 have stronger protections that cannot be opted out of:[^r12][^r13]

- No more than 8 hours a day and no more than 40 hours a week.
- A 30-minute rest break where the working day is longer than 4.5 hours.
- 12 uninterrupted hours of rest in each 24-hour period worked.
- Two days off each week.

Working hours are also relevant to pay. Because minimum wage is tested as an hourly rate, unpaid overtime can drag a salaried apprentice below the legal minimum.[^r20]

## What happens if an apprentice is dismissed or made redundant?

Apprentices have the same protections against unfair dismissal as other employees, and employers should follow a fair process consistent with the Acas Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures.[^r2]

Two apprenticeship-specific points matter:

- Some apprenticeship agreements are fixed-term with no clause allowing early termination. Ending one early in that situation can be a breach of contract in itself, on top of any unfair dismissal question.[^r2]
- Statutory redundancy pay requires at least two years' continuous service, which many apprentices will not have. Where it applies, it is half a week's pay per year of service under age 22, one week's pay per year from 22 to 40, and one and a half weeks' pay per year at 41 and over.[^r2]

If an apprentice is made redundant, government funding for the training normally continues for at least 12 weeks while they look for a new employer, and there is dedicated redundancy support for apprentices reachable through the apprenticeship helpline on 08000 150 400.[^r14]

## What protection do apprentices have against discrimination, and what adjustments can they ask for?

The Equality Act 2010 applies to apprentices at every stage, including application and interview. Employers and training providers must not discriminate on the protected characteristics, and both have a duty to make reasonable adjustments so that a disabled apprentice is not disadvantaged in the job or in the training.[^r15][^r21]

Funding for additional learning support for apprentices with learning difficulties or disabilities is provided to training providers by government, so a provider should not be treating support as something the apprentice pays for.[^r15]

There are also specific English and maths flexibilities. An apprentice with a learning difficulty or disability who can meet the occupational standard but cannot reach the usual English and maths level, even with support and adjustments, may be able to complete Entry Level 3 Functional Skills instead, on the basis of an evidenced assessment by the provider.[^r16]

Separately, since 11 February 2025, apprentices who were aged 19 or over at the start of their training are no longer required to hold or achieve level 2 English and maths qualifications in order to complete their apprenticeship. Apprentices aged 16 to 18 at the start still are.[^r17]

## How does an apprentice complain, and to whom?

There are three distinct routes and they handle different problems. Using the wrong one wastes months.

**1. Employment problems — pay, hours, holiday, bullying, dismissal.** These go to the employer, through its grievance procedure, and then if unresolved to Acas for free confidential advice on 0300 123 1100, and if necessary to an employment tribunal. Minimum wage underpayment can also be reported to HMRC, anonymously.[^r1][^r20] The Department for Education does not investigate employment disputes.[^r19]

**2. Training problems — training not delivered, poor teaching, assessment failures.** These go first to the training provider under its published complaints procedure. If that is exhausted without resolution, escalate to the Department for Education using the complaints route for post-16 provision it funds. The escalation must reach DfE within 12 months of the issue occurring.[^r18][^r19]

**3. General advice and where-do-I-go questions.** The national apprenticeship helpline is 08000 150 400, or nationalhelpdesk@apprenticeships.gov.uk.[^r18]

The GOV.UK complaints route directs escalated complaints about ESFA-funded post-16 provision to the departmental customer service team by email at customer.complaints@education.gov.uk or by post to Coventry.[^r19]
