## In one line

A degree apprenticeship removes tuition debt and pays a wage from day one, but it is now roughly four times harder to get into than it was three years ago and two in five apprentices do not finish — while a degree is worth around £100,000 in extra lifetime earnings on average, with about one in five graduates financially worse off for having done one.

## Key facts

- The maximum tuition fee for a standard full-time undergraduate course in England rises to £9,790 for the 2026/27 academic year, a 2.71% increase on £9,535, and to £10,050 for 2027/28.[^u1]
- Degree apprentices pay no tuition fee. Training is paid for by the employer and government through the apprenticeship levy system.[^u12][^u13]
- Apprentices are not eligible for student loans, including maintenance loans.[^u13]
- Plan 5 student loans are repaid at 9% of income above £25,000 a year in 2026-27, carry interest at RPI only — 4.1% for the year from 1 September 2026 — and are written off after 40 years.[^u2][^u3][^u4][^u5]
- Undergraduates starting in 2024/25 are forecast to enter repayment with an average debt of about £45,600 in cash terms, or about £38,700 in 2024-25 prices.[^u19]
- Institute for Fiscal Studies research for the Department for Education estimates the average net lifetime earnings gain from an undergraduate degree at roughly £130,000 for men and £100,000 for women, about a 20% uplift.[^u6][^u7]
- The same research finds around one in five undergraduates would have been better off financially had they not gone, with returns close to zero for some subjects and above £250,000 for others.[^u6][^u7]
- Competition for level 6 apprenticeships on the government's vacancy service rose to 11.3 applications per position, from 2.8 two years earlier.[^u16]
- The national apprenticeship achievement rate reached 60.5%, and around 38% of apprentices withdrew before completing in 2023-24.[^u10][^u11]

## What does each route actually cost in tuition and debt?

**University in England, starting 2026/27.** The tuition fee cap for a standard full-time undergraduate course is £9,790 a year, up 2.71% from £9,535. Accelerated full-time courses are capped at £11,750 and part-time at £7,335. The cap rises again to £10,050 for 2027/28.[^u1] Tuition is covered by a tuition fee loan, not paid up front.

**Degree apprenticeship.** There is no tuition fee for the apprentice. Training costs are met by the employer and government through the apprenticeship levy and co-investment arrangements, so a degree apprentice can finish a degree with no tuition debt.[^u12][^u13]

The debt difference is real but it is not the same as a cash difference. A Plan 5 student loan is not a commercial debt: repayment is 9% of earnings above the threshold, nothing is payable below it, and the balance is cancelled after 40 years.[^u2][^u3] For many graduates the loan behaves more like a graduate contribution than a mortgage.

## What are the student loan terms currently in force?

These are the Plan 5 terms that apply to English undergraduates who started, or start, courses from the 2023/24 academic year onwards. Figures are those in force as at 2 September 2026.

| Term | Plan 5, 2026-27 |
| --- | --- |
| Annual repayment threshold | £25,000 (£2,083 a month, £480 a week)[^u2][^u5] |
| Repayment rate | 9% of income above the threshold[^u3] |
| Interest rate | RPI only, 4.1% for 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027[^u4][^u5] |
| Write-off | 40 years after entering repayment[^u2][^u5] |
| Threshold uprating | Frozen at £25,000 until April 2027, then intended to rise annually with RPI[^u5] |

**GTOD take:** the £25,000 threshold is the number that matters most in an apprenticeship-versus-university comparison, because it is close to what a good degree apprentice is paid. A degree apprentice earning above £25,000 has no student loan repayment because they have no loan; a graduate on the same salary pays 9% of the excess.

## What does the earnings evidence show?

**For graduates.** Institute for Fiscal Studies research commissioned by the Department for Education estimates that the average undergraduate degree raises net lifetime earnings by roughly £130,000 for men and £100,000 for women in discounted present-value terms, about a 20% gain.[^u6][^u7] That is an average that hides an enormous spread. The same research finds net returns close to zero for women taking creative arts and languages, negative for men taking creative arts, and above £250,000 for law and economics and above half a million for men in medicine or economics.[^u6][^u7]

The headline finding that matters for a 17-year-old is that around one in five undergraduates would have been financially better off not going.[^u6][^u7] Which fifth you are in depends heavily on subject, institution and prior attainment; Department for Education Longitudinal Education Outcomes data tracks that earnings spread by subject and by provider.[^u8]

**For apprentices.** Department for Education further education outcomes data, built on the Longitudinal Education Outcomes study, shows earnings rising with level and with time since completion: median earnings of about £18,050 one year after an intermediate (level 2) apprenticeship rising to about £26,810 at five years, and about £20,700 one year after an advanced (level 3) apprenticeship rising to about £29,330 at five years.[^u9] At every level, apprenticeships showed higher earnings one year after study than classroom-based education and training at the same level.[^u9]

**For neither.** Graduate labour market statistics for 2024 show non-graduates faring materially worse on labour market attachment: around 27.9% of non-graduates were economically inactive, against 9.6% of graduates.[^u14]

## Can apprentices progress to further study?

A degree apprenticeship ends with a full bachelor's degree, so the academic entry route to a master's or a professional qualification is the same as for any other graduate. Level 7 apprenticeships exist at master's level too.[^u13]

The practical constraint is funding rather than eligibility, and it cuts in a specific direction: an apprentice is not eligible for undergraduate student finance while apprenticing, and government funding for level 7 apprenticeships has been narrowed in recent policy changes.[^u12][^u13]

## What are the genuine risks of the apprenticeship route?

Be honest about these before applying.

- **It is very hard to get in.** Applications per level 6 apprenticeship vacancy advertised on the government's Find an Apprenticeship service rose to 11.3, from 2.8 two years earlier, with level 4 to 6 apprenticeships overall at 10.6 applications per position.[^u16] Degree apprenticeship places are a small fraction of the number of undergraduate places.[^u15]
- **A lot of people do not finish.** The national apprenticeship achievement rate stands at about 60.5%, and around 38% of apprentices withdrew before completing in 2023-24 — an improvement on 44% the year before, but still high.[^u10][^u11][^u17]
- **You are tied to one employer.** Losing the job normally ends the apprenticeship, subject to the redundancy protections described in our rights document. There is no equivalent of transferring course.
- **Geography constrains you.** Apprenticeship vacancies are where the employers are. Applicants outside major employment centres have far less choice than a university applicant, who can apply anywhere.
- **Less academic breadth and less slack.** A degree apprenticeship is a full-time job plus a degree. Holiday is annual leave, not university vacations. There is less room to change your mind about the subject.
- **Access is uneven.** Sutton Trust research has questioned how far degree apprenticeships reach disadvantaged young people relative to their promise.[^u15]

## What are the genuine risks of the university route?

- **Debt with a real repayment cost for mid-earners.** Forecast average debt on entering repayment is around £45,600 for the 2024/25 starting cohort.[^u19] With a 40-year term and a £25,000 threshold, many middle earners will repay for most of their working life without clearing the balance.[^u2][^u5]
- **The return is not guaranteed.** Around one in five graduates would have been better off financially without the degree, and subject choice drives that more than almost anything else.[^u6][^u7]
- **Three or four years of forgone earnings.** A degree apprentice is earning throughout. That gap is the single largest financial item in any five-year comparison.
- **Graduate unemployment is not zero.** HESA graduate outcomes data recorded graduate unemployment rising from 5% for 2020/21 leavers to 7% for 2022/23 leavers.[^u18]
- **Not everyone finishes either.** Non-continuation after the first year is a real risk, tracked by the Office for Students and Discover Uni.

## Which route should I choose?

**GTOD take:** the question is usually posed as "apprenticeship or university" when the evidence supports a different framing.

1. **Compare specific things, not categories.** A level 6 software engineering apprenticeship at a large bank and a creative arts degree at a low-tariff provider are not the same decision as a medicine degree versus a level 3 retail apprenticeship. The IFS spread by subject is wider than the average gap between the routes.[^u6]
2. **Do not treat the loan as ordinary debt.** It is an income-contingent contribution with a 40-year cut-off. Fear of the headline balance leads people to overweight it.[^u2][^u3]
3. **Treat the competition data seriously.** At 11.3 applications per level 6 vacancy, a degree apprenticeship cannot be a plan with no backup.[^u16] Apply to both, and keep the UCAS application live.
4. **Ask about completion, not just entry.** With roughly two in five apprentices not finishing, the question to put to an employer is what proportion of their apprentices complete and are retained.[^u10][^u11]
