## In one line

From 1 April 2026 the apprentice National Minimum Wage rate is £8.00 an hour, but it only covers apprentices under 19 and apprentices aged 19 or over who are still in year one — everyone else is legally entitled to the full age-based rate.

## Key facts

- The apprentice rate of the National Minimum Wage rose from £7.55 to £8.00 an hour on 1 April 2026, an increase of 45p or about 6%.[^s1][^s2]
- The apprentice rate applies only to apprentices aged under 19, and to apprentices aged 19 or over who are in the first year of their apprenticeship. Level and sector make no difference.[^s3][^s5]
- The other UK minimum wage rates in force from 1 April 2026 are £8.00 an hour at 16 to 17, £10.85 an hour at 18 to 20, and £12.71 an hour (the National Living Wage) at 21 and over.[^s1][^s2]
- The accommodation offset — the maximum an employer may count towards minimum wage for accommodation it provides — is £11.10 a day or £77.70 a week from 1 April 2026.[^s2]
- Time spent training, whether at the workplace, at a college or at a university, is working time and must be paid.[^s3]
- The apprentice rate is a legal floor, not a going rate. Median apprentice pay is well above it in most sectors, and higher and degree apprenticeships at large employers commonly advertise well above £20,000 a year.[^s11][^s12]
- Underpayment of apprentices is a real and measured problem, concentrated among the youngest apprentices.[^s6]
- HMRC can order an employer to repay up to six years of arrears at current rates and can fine it up to 200% of the underpayment, capped at £20,000 per worker.[^s8][^s9]

## What is the apprentice minimum wage rate right now?

As at 2 September 2026, the rates in force are those that came into effect on 1 April 2026. UK minimum wage rates change every April, so a rate quoted for any other period is out of date.[^s1]

| Rate | Hourly rate from 1 April 2026 | Previous rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apprentice rate | £8.00 | £7.55 |
| Aged 16 to 17 | £8.00 | £7.55 |
| Aged 18 to 20 | £10.85 | £10.00 |
| Aged 21 and over (National Living Wage) | £12.71 | £12.21 |
| Accommodation offset | £11.10 per day / £77.70 per week | £10.66 per day / £74.62 per week |

Source: Low Pay Commission and GOV.UK rate announcements for 2026.[^s1][^s2]

## Who exactly does the apprentice minimum wage rate apply to?

The apprentice rate of the National Minimum Wage covers two groups of apprentices, and nobody else:[^s3][^s5]

1. Apprentices aged under 19, for the whole of their apprenticeship.
2. Apprentices aged 19 or over who are still within the first 12 months of their apprenticeship.

You must be on a recognised apprenticeship — with an apprenticeship agreement and a training plan — for the apprentice rate to be lawful at all. Someone described as an "apprentice" but not enrolled on an actual apprenticeship is entitled to the ordinary age-based rate.[^s3]

## When does an apprentice move onto the standard age-based minimum wage?

An apprentice moves onto the standard age-based rate as soon as both of these are true: they are aged 19 or over, and they have completed the first year of the apprenticeship.[^s3][^s5]

Worked examples using the rates in force from 1 April 2026:

- Age 17, month 14 of the apprenticeship: still £8.00 an hour, because the apprentice is under 19.
- Age 19, month 6: £8.00 an hour, because they are in year one.
- Age 19 and one day past the first anniversary of starting: £10.85 an hour, the 18 to 20 rate.
- Age 22, month 13: £12.71 an hour, the National Living Wage.

The pay rise is not automatic in payroll systems. It is the employer's legal duty to apply it, but in practice apprentices should diary both their nineteenth birthday and their first work anniversary and check the payslip in the following pay period.

**GTOD take:** the single most common apprentice pay mistake we see described is an employer leaving someone on the apprentice rate into year two. Check it yourself; do not assume HR caught it.

## What changes about apprentice pay in year two?

Legally, only one thing changes at the end of year one: apprentices aged 19 or over stop being eligible for the apprentice rate and become entitled to their age-based rate.[^s3][^s5] For a 19 or 20 year old on the floor rate that is a jump from £8.00 to £10.85 an hour — around a 36% rise — under the rates in force from 1 April 2026.[^s1]

Two things do **not** change automatically in year two:

- Apprentices aged under 19 stay on the apprentice rate. A 17 year old in year two is still lawfully paid £8.00 an hour.[^s3]
- There is no statutory annual increment beyond the minimum wage. Many employers run their own banded apprentice pay scales that rise each year, but that comes from the contract, not from the law.

## What do apprenticeships actually pay by level and sector?

Published, comparable pay data by apprenticeship level is thinner than it should be, so treat the following as ranges rather than benchmarks.

Government outcome statistics show earnings rising with level and with time since completion. Department for Education further education outcomes data, drawn from the Longitudinal Education Outcomes study, reports median earnings one year after completion of around £18,050 for intermediate (level 2) apprenticeships and around £20,700 for advanced (level 3) apprenticeships, rising to roughly £26,810 and £29,330 respectively five years after completion.[^s10]

For the intake end of the market, aggregator data for 2026 puts the average advertised apprentice salary in England at about £24,420 a year across all levels, and the average degree apprenticeship salary at a little over £26,000 based on apprentice-submitted reviews, with digital, finance and engineering employers at the top of the range.[^s11][^s12]

## How common is apprentice underpayment?

Underpayment is measurably more common among apprentices than among workers generally, and it is worst among the youngest.[^s6]

Low Pay Commission analysis of Department for Education survey data found that close to one in five apprentices aged 16 to 17, and close to one in ten aged 18, were paid below their legal entitlement. Around half of those underpaid were within 5% of the apprentice rate, which suggests a lot of it is employer error rather than deliberate exploitation — but it is still unlawful.[^s6]

The same analysis found apprentice median pay sits consistently below that of non-apprentices of the same age, and that a large majority of the very youngest apprentices are paid at or very close to the minimum.[^s6][^s7]

## How do I tell if I am being underpaid as an apprentice?

Work through this in order. You need your payslip, your contract or apprenticeship agreement, and an honest count of hours.

1. **Establish which rate you are legally owed.** Under 19, or 19+ and in year one, means £8.00 an hour from 1 April 2026. Otherwise use your age band: £10.85 at 18 to 20, £12.71 at 21 and over.[^s1][^s3]
2. **Count all paid working time, not just time at your desk.** Training, college days, university days and required study count as working time and must be paid.[^s3]
3. **Divide gross pay by total hours worked in the pay period.** If the result is below your entitled rate, you are being underpaid. A salaried apprentice working unpaid overtime can drop below the minimum this way even on a headline salary that looks fine.[^s4]
4. **Check the deductions.** Deductions for things such as uniform, tools or equipment required for the job can push effective pay below the minimum wage, which is unlawful.[^s4][^s9]
5. **Check accommodation.** If your employer provides accommodation, no more than £11.10 a day may be counted towards minimum wage from 1 April 2026.[^s2]
6. **Diary the two trigger dates.** Your nineteenth birthday and your first work anniversary. Check the next payslip after each.[^s3]

## What do I do if I am being underpaid as an apprentice?

There is a working escalation ladder, and the early steps are low-risk.

1. **Ask, in writing.** Email your manager or HR, state the rate you believe you are owed and the dates, and ask them to check. Many cases are payroll error and are fixed at this stage.[^s8]
2. **Get free confidential advice.** Acas gives free confidential advice on minimum wage on 0300 123 1100. Citizens Advice also advises on underpayment.[^s8][^s9]
3. **Report it to HMRC.** HMRC enforces the National Minimum Wage. Complaints can be made online and can be made anonymously; HMRC will not pass your details to your employer without your agreement, and you can report a former employer.[^s5][^s8][^s9]
4. **Know what enforcement produces.** HMRC can require repayment of arrears going back up to six years, calculated at current rather than historical rates, and can impose a penalty of up to 200% of the amount underpaid, capped at £20,000 per worker. Employers can also be publicly named.[^s8][^s9]
5. **Employment tribunal.** An unlawful deduction from wages claim is also possible, normally within three months less one day of the deduction, and normally after Acas early conciliation. Take advice before choosing this route rather than HMRC.[^s8]

You are legally protected from being dismissed or treated badly for asserting your right to the minimum wage.[^s9]
