## In one line

Apprentices in England pay nothing for their training but are excluded from student finance altogether, and the support that does exist — a £3,000 care leavers' bursary, a council tax disregard for low-paid apprentices, and patchy regional travel schemes — has to be claimed, not received automatically.

## Key facts

- Apprentices in England pay no tuition fee for their apprenticeship: the cost of training is met by the employer and government through the apprenticeship levy system.[^f1][^f2]
- Apprentices are **not** eligible for student loans, including tuition fee loans and maintenance loans, so day-to-day living costs come out of the apprentice's wage.[^f1]
- A care-experienced apprentice who was under 25 when they started, on an apprenticeship in England beginning on or after 1 August 2023, can claim a £3,000 bursary, paid in three instalments of £1,000 across the first year.[^f3][^f4][^f5]
- The care leavers' bursary is claimed by the training provider on the apprentice's behalf, and must be paid on to the apprentice within 30 days of the provider receiving it. It can only be received once.[^f3][^f4]
- An apprentice earning no more than £195 a week gross, on training leading to a qualification accredited by a recognised body, is disregarded for council tax. There is no upper age limit for this disregard.[^f6][^f7][^f12]
- There is no national travel discount scheme for apprentices in England. Support is regional and varies by combined authority or council.[^f8]
- Funding for additional learning support for apprentices with a learning difficulty or disability goes to the training provider, not to the apprentice, so it should never be something the apprentice pays for.[^f9]

## What are apprentices NOT eligible for?

This section exists because it is where people get caught out. Read it before you budget.

- **Tuition fee loans and maintenance loans.** Apprentices cannot apply for student finance for the apprenticeship. There is no maintenance loan equivalent, so living costs, rent and travel are paid out of the apprentice's wage.[^f1]
- **Student bank accounts and student overdrafts on student terms.** These require proof of enrolment on a course through student finance, which an apprentice does not have. Some banks do offer apprentice accounts; that is a commercial decision, not an entitlement.
- **University hardship funds and university bursaries tied to student finance status.** A degree apprentice studying at a university is normally not a student-finance-funded student, which is the trigger for most institutional bursaries.
- **The "student" council tax exemption route.** Apprentices qualify, if at all, through the separate apprentice disregard with its £195 a week earnings condition — not through the full-time student exemption.[^f6][^f7]

**GTOD take:** the single most common budgeting error we see is a school leaver assuming a degree apprenticeship comes with something maintenance-loan-shaped. It does not. Your apprentice salary is the whole of your income.

## What is the care leavers' bursary for apprentices?

A £3,000 bursary is available to care-experienced apprentices in England.[^f3][^f5]

| Condition | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Age | Under 25 at the start of the apprenticeship[^f3][^f4] |
| Start date | Apprenticeship started on or after 1 August 2023[^f3] |
| Care status | An eligible child, a relevant child, or a former relevant child[^f4] |
| Location | The apprenticeship must be based in England; the care can have been with a local authority anywhere in the UK[^f4] |
| Amount and payment | £3,000, paid in three instalments of £1,000 across the first year[^f3][^f5] |
| How to claim | Through the training provider, who claims it and must pay it on within 30 days[^f3][^f4] |
| Frequency | Once only[^f4] |

The care status categories are defined as: an eligible child is a 16 or 17 year old currently looked after who has been in care for at least 13 weeks since turning 14; a relevant child is a 16 or 17 year old who left care after their sixteenth birthday; a former relevant child is someone under 25 who was in care before turning 18.[^f4]

To claim, tell the training provider. They will need confirmation from the local authority — typically a short letter or email from a personal adviser. The apprentice does not have to disclose details of their care experience to the employer.[^f3][^f11]

**GTOD take:** this bursary is significantly under-claimed because it depends on the provider knowing about it and acting. If you are care-experienced, raise it in writing with your provider at induction rather than waiting to be asked.

## Can apprentices get a council tax discount?

Yes, through the apprentice disregard, but it is tightly conditioned on low pay.

An apprentice is disregarded for council tax purposes if they are employed to learn a trade, business or profession, the training leads to a qualification accredited by a recognised awarding body, and they are paid no more than £195 a week gross.[^f6][^f7][^f12]

- Being "disregarded" means you are not counted when the council works out how many adults live in the property.
- If everyone in the household is disregarded, the bill is reduced by 50%.[^f12]
- If you are the only adult who counts once others are disregarded, the usual single-person discount of 25% applies.
- There is no maximum age for the apprentice disregard.[^f12]

To apply, contact the local council and supply a contract of employment or a letter from the employer confirming the apprenticeship and the pay.[^f12]

The £195 a week threshold is the binding constraint. At the apprentice minimum wage of £8.00 an hour from 1 April 2026, a 37.5-hour week is £300 gross, well above it — so most full-time degree apprentices will not qualify.

## What travel support can apprentices get?

There is no national apprentice travel discount in England. The Trades Union Congress has campaigned for one precisely because provision is fragmented.[^f8]

What exists is regional and changes often. Examples encountered during this research, all of which need checking locally before being relied on:

- Greater Manchester's Our Pass, offering free bus travel to 16 to 18 year olds.
- Merseytravel's My Ticket for young people up to 18.
- A 16-18 photocard in the West Midlands Combined Authority area giving half-price travel.
- Gloucestershire's Thinksmart scheme, a free bus pass for apprentices travelling to placement.
- The national 16-17 Saver railcard, giving 50% off many rail fares for 16 and 17 year olds.

**GTOD take:** treat travel as a real line in the offer you accept, not an afterthought. Ask the employer directly at offer stage whether they contribute to travel or to a season ticket loan — many large employers do, and it is rarely advertised.

## What support must the employer and training provider provide?

- **The cost of training.** Employers with an annual pay bill above £3 million pay the apprenticeship levy at 0.5% and draw down funds from an apprenticeship service account; smaller employers pay a small share of the training cost with government paying the rest, subject to the funding band maximum.[^f2][^f10] Either way the apprentice pays nothing.
- **Paid time for training.** Off-the-job training is working time and must be paid.
- **Additional learning support.** Funding for support for apprentices with learning difficulties or disabilities is provided to the training provider by government. An apprentice should never be asked to fund their own reasonable adjustments or learning support.[^f9]
