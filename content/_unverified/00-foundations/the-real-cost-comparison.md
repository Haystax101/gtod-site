## In one line

Over five years from September 2026, on the assumptions set out below, a degree apprentice living at home ends up with roughly £94,000 of accumulated net cash and no student debt, and an undergraduate on maximum maintenance support ends up with roughly £19,000 of cash and about £72,500 of Plan 5 debt — but that five-year snapshot is not the lifetime answer.

## Key facts

- Tuition fee cap for a standard full-time undergraduate course: £9,790 for 2026/27, £10,050 for 2027/28.[^c1]
- Maximum maintenance loan for 2026/27, full-time undergraduate: £9,118 living at the parental home, £10,830 living away from home outside London, £14,135 living away from home in London — a 2.71% uplift on 2025/26.[^c2][^c11]
- Plan 5 loans: repay 9% of income above £25,000; interest at RPI only, 4.1% for the year from 1 September 2026; written off after 40 years.[^c3][^c4][^c5][^c14]
- Degree apprentices pay no tuition fee and are not eligible for student loans.[^c12]
- Income tax personal allowance is £12,570 in 2026/27 and is frozen; basic rate 20%; employee National Insurance 8% above the primary threshold.[^c7][^c8]
- Average student living costs are reported at about £1,142 a month, with average student rent of £575 a month and London highest at about £1,269 a month.[^c9][^c10]
- The apprentice minimum wage from 1 April 2026 is £8.00 an hour, rising to £10.85 at 18 to 20 and £12.71 at 21 and over once the apprentice rate no longer applies.[^c6]

**GTOD take on the salary assumptions:** the apprentice path assumes a competitive level 6 apprenticeship, not a level 2 one. £22,000 in year one is well above the legal floor: at 37.5 hours a week the apprentice rate of £8.00 an hour produces about £15,600 a year.[^c6] The step to £34,000 in year five reflects moving onto a qualified salary after completing. The graduate figures assume a solid but not exceptional graduate scheme. Change these numbers and the answer changes; that is the point of showing the arithmetic.

## What does the apprentice's money look like over five years?

The apprentice works from September 2026. Tax is calculated at the 2026/27 personal allowance of £12,570, basic rate 20% and employee National Insurance 8%, held constant across all five years.[^c7][^c8]

| Year | Gross salary | Income tax (20% above £12,570) | NI (8% above £12,570) | Net pay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | £22,000 | £1,886 | £754 | £19,360 |
| 2 | £24,000 | £2,286 | £914 | £20,800 |
| 3 | £26,000 | £2,686 | £1,074 | £22,240 |
| 4 | £28,000 | £3,086 | £1,234 | £23,680 |
| 5 | £34,000 | £4,286 | £1,714 | £28,000 |
| **Total** | **£134,000** | **£14,230** | **£5,690** | **£114,080** |

Worked example for year 1: £22,000 minus £12,570 leaves £9,430 of taxable income. Income tax is 20% of £9,430, which is £1,886. National Insurance is 8% of £9,430, which is £754. Net pay is £22,000 minus £1,886 minus £754, which is £19,360.

Living at the parental home, costs over five years are board at £250 a month (£3,000 a year, £15,000 over five) and travel at £80 a month (£960 a year, £4,800 over five). Total £19,800.

**Accumulated net cash after five years: £114,080 minus £19,800 = £94,280.**
**Student debt after five years: £0.**[^c12]

## What does the same comparison look like if the apprentice rents?

The parental-home assumption does a lot of work, so here is the alternative. Replace board and travel with graduate-equivalent living costs of £14,400 a year plus £960 a year of travel.

Costs over five years: (£14,400 plus £960) multiplied by 5 = £76,800.

**Accumulated net cash after five years: £114,080 minus £76,800 = £37,280.**
**Student debt after five years: £0.**

## What does the student's money look like over five years?

Years 1 to 3 are a three-year degree outside London, on the maximum maintenance loan — which in practice means a household income low enough to qualify for full support.[^c2][^c11] Years 4 and 5 are graduate employment.

**Borrowing during study**

| Year | Tuition loan | Maintenance loan | Drawn that year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | £9,790 | £10,830 | £20,620 |
| 2 | £10,050 | £10,830 | £20,880 |
| 3 | £10,050 | £10,830 | £20,880 |
| **Total principal** | **£29,890** | **£32,490** | **£62,380** |

**Interest, at 4.1% a year (Plan 5, RPI only)**[^c4][^c5]

- End of year 1: £20,620 multiplied by 1.041 = £21,465
- End of year 2: (£21,465 plus £20,880) multiplied by 1.041 = £44,081
- End of year 3: (£44,081 plus £20,880) multiplied by 1.041 = £67,625
- End of year 4: £67,625 multiplied by 1.041 = £70,398
- End of year 5, less repayments: about **£72,500**

Repayments only begin the April after graduation, and only on income above £25,000. On a £30,000 salary that is 9% of £5,000, which is £450 a year; on £32,000 it is 9% of £7,000, which is £630 a year.[^c3][^c14] Roughly £820 of repayments across years 4 and 5 against roughly £5,700 of interest accruing in the same period — the balance is still growing.

**Cash during study, years 1 to 3**

- Money in: maintenance loan £10,830 plus part-time work £2,000 = £12,830 a year, £38,490 over three years.
- Money out: living costs £13,704 a year, £41,112 over three years.[^c9]
- Shortfall: about **£2,622**, covered from savings, family or a university bursary.

**Cash in work, years 4 and 5**

| Year | Gross | Tax | NI | Student loan | Net after loan | Living costs | Left over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | £30,000 | £3,486 | £1,394 | about £190 | £24,930 | £14,400 | £10,530 |
| 5 | £32,000 | £3,886 | £1,554 | £630 | £25,930 | £14,400 | £11,530 |

Year 4 repayment is small because repayments start only in the April part-way through that year.

**Accumulated net cash after five years: £10,530 plus £11,530 minus £2,622 = about £19,438.**
**Student debt after five years: about £72,500.**

## What is the five-year bottom line?

| | Degree apprentice, living at home | Degree apprentice, renting | Graduate, 3-year degree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gross earnings over 5 years | £134,000 | £134,000 | £62,000 |
| Years spent earning | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Accumulated net cash at year 5 | about £94,300 | about £37,300 | about £19,400 |
| Student debt at year 5 | £0 | £0 | about £72,500 |
| Qualification at year 5 | Bachelor's degree | Bachelor's degree | Bachelor's degree |

On these assumptions the apprentice living at home is about £75,000 ahead in cash and about £72,500 ahead on debt.

## Why does that five-year gap not settle the question?

Four honest caveats. All four are material.

1. **A Plan 5 loan is not a normal debt.** Nothing is repayable below £25,000 of income, repayment is capped at 9% of the excess, and the balance is cancelled after 40 years.[^c3][^c14] A £72,500 balance is not equivalent to £72,500 of cash owed. For a graduate who never earns much, a large part of it is never repaid.
2. **The degree's earnings premium arrives later.** Institute for Fiscal Studies work for the Department for Education estimates an average net lifetime earnings gain from an undergraduate degree of roughly £130,000 for men and £100,000 for women — an amount of the same order as the five-year gap above, but earned over a whole career.[^c13]
3. **Both salary paths are assumptions, and they are the biggest lever.** Change the apprentice's year 5 salary from £34,000 to £26,000, or the graduate's from £32,000 to £45,000, and the picture shifts substantially. Model your own numbers.
4. **Living at home is doing enormous work.** Comparing the renting apprentice with the graduate narrows the cash gap to about £18,000 across five years — still in the apprentice's favour, plus no debt, but a very different headline.

**GTOD take:** the five-year comparison is genuinely and unambiguously in the apprentice's favour on cash. The lifetime comparison is not settled by it, and anyone who tells a 17-year-old that a degree apprenticeship is worth "£100,000 more" without saying over what period and on what salary assumptions is selling something.
