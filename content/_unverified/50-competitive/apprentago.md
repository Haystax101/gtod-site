## What it actually is

Apprentago is a UK **early-careers marketplace and workflow tool**, not a content brand and not a
training provider. It sits in the same structural position GTOD is aiming at: the layer between a
young person and an apprenticeship vacancy. Its distinguishing bet is that **AI does the CV work**,
and that the same database serves three paying-or-not-paying constituencies at once:

- **Students (free)** — search, apply, build a CV, track applications.
- **Schools (paid, ~£1,000/yr, or free in funded regions)** — oversight and statutory reporting.
- **Employers (paid or regionally sponsored)** — post roles, track applicants, buy creator marketing.

The three-sided design matters strategically: the student side is the acquisition funnel and is
deliberately loss-leading, while schools and employers pay. That is the standard UK careers-tech
model (see `landscape-overview.md`) — Apprentago's twist is that it also sells **TikTok-native
creator marketing** as a line item, which is unusual and directly overlaps GTOD's native strength.

Positioning is aimed at **age 14+**, i.e. deliberately earlier than the Year 13 decision point.

## Features, verified as far as possible

### Student side (free)

| Feature | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vacancy search | Browse and filter apprenticeships and work experience by job role, industry, location | Local/proximity discovery is emphasised |
| Direct apply | Submit applications through the platform rather than bouncing to employer sites | This is the key moat — it makes them the system of record |
| AI CV builder | Generates an ATS-compatible CV, marketed as a ~2-minute job | Core differentiator |
| Per-application CV tailoring | Store multiple CV versions, tailor one to each vacancy on the fly | More sophisticated than most rivals |
| Application tracker | Track every application, with note-taking against each | Student-facing, launched ~Nov 2024 |
| Work-experience logging | Students record work experience hours, visible to their school | Feeds the school reporting product |
| Role/industry explainers | Content explaining what industries and roles actually involve | Modest — not their strength |
| School auto-linking | Signing up with a school email address links the account to the school with no MIS integration | Clever, low-friction growth loop |

### School side (~£1,000/yr)

Marketed as a **careers productivity platform** — the buyer is the careers leader, and the job to be
done is Ofsted evidence, not student inspiration.

- Single view of every student's CV, work-experience hours and live applications.
- **Instant Gatsby Benchmark report download**, described as Ofsted-ready.
- Push opportunities out to named students.
- **No MIS integration required** — a genuine competitive advantage; SIMS/Arbor integration is the
  usual reason careers tools take a term to deploy.

### Employer side (paid / sponsored)

- Employer profile pages with rich content.
- Job posting with an approval step before the vacancy goes live.
- **Kanban-style applicant tracker** — move candidates through stages in one click.
- Dashboard covering live pipeline, performance metrics and **diversity impact**.
- **Open API** to sync vacancies automatically and push candidate data into Workday, HubSpot etc.
- **Early-careers creator marketing**: hand-picked "StudyTok / ApprenticeshipTok" creators script,
  shoot and post sector-explainer videos; employers get a dashboard covering reach, watch time,
  click-through, resulting Apprentago sign-ups and CVs generated.

That last product is the one GTOD should study hardest — it is an **audience-arbitrage business**
sold to employers, and GTOD already owns the audience Apprentago has to rent.

## Funding, ownership and who is behind it

**Founder-led positioning.** Apprentago markets heavily on its founder's own
apprenticeship background, and that personal origin story is a working part of its
distribution rather than incidental colour. The competitively relevant point is the
*mechanism*: a credible first-person account of finding an apprenticeship converts
attention into sign-ups, and GTOD already has that in Charlie and George.

- **Angel investment**, including Richard Waddington, described as an exited nine-figure founder.

## Distribution — how they get students

1. **Founder-led TikTok.** `@carlo_weaver` posts product and apprenticeship content; the application
   tracker was pre-launched via a TikTok waitlist. This is the same playbook as GTOD.
2. **Creator network.** They have recruited StudyTok/ApprenticeshipTok creators — and monetise them
   via the employer marketing product.
3. **Schools as a distribution channel.** The school-email auto-link means one careers leader can
   deliver an entire year group. At £1,000/yr this is cheap customer acquisition disguised as revenue.
4. **Regional public funding.** The Youth Guarantee deal makes them free-at-point-of-use across a
   whole combined authority, which buys near-monopoly regional coverage.
5. **Earned media** on the founder's biography.

## Strengths

- **The AI CV builder is a genuinely good wedge.** It solves the single most-avoided task for a
  16-19 year old, it produces an artefact the student keeps, and it creates a reason to return.
- **Three-sided model with real revenue** — they are not dependent on advertising alone.
- **Public-sector revenue is sticky and defensible.** A combined-authority contract is very hard for
  a competitor to dislodge mid-term, and it comes with regional exclusivity in practice.
- **The school hook is well-chosen.** Gatsby/Ofsted reporting is a statutory pain; solving it with no
  MIS integration removes the main objection.
- **Founder story is a distribution asset**, not just PR — it earns trust with exactly the
  non-traditional students the sector fails.
- **API-first employer integration** is more serious engineering than most UK careers sites offer.

## Weaknesses and where GTOD can attack

1. **The engagement gap.** 450k reached vs ~5k daily. They are a transactional tool people use once
   at deadline time. There is no evident reason to open Apprentago on a Tuesday in March. GTOD's
   community and podcast are exactly the retention layer they lack.
2. **Rented audience.** Their creator marketing depends on hiring creators. GTOD *is* the creator.
   Apprentago's cost line is GTOD's owned asset — a structural margin advantage.
3. **Geographic concentration risk.** The school and employer traction is heavily
   Cambridgeshire & Peterborough because that is where the money is. Outside the funded region the
   value proposition is unproven and the £1,000 has to be argued for on merit.
4. **Funding-cliff exposure.** Youth Guarantee Trailblazers are time-limited pilots. If the policy is
   not extended, a large share of revenue and the "free" positioning disappear at once. **Track the
   CPCA funding end date — that is the date Apprentago becomes vulnerable.**
5. **Serving three masters.** Features that please careers leaders (surveillance-flavoured
   dashboards showing every student's applications) are not features 17-year-olds want. A student who
   knows their school can see their applications behaves differently — and often stops using the tool.
   **This is a real, exploitable trust gap: GTOD can credibly be the student's side only.**
6. **AI CV output is commoditising fast.** Any student with free ChatGPT can produce a CV. The moat
   is the apprenticeship-specific context, not the generation — and that moat is thin.
7. **Thin community, thin guidance.** Explainers exist but are not the product. No verified evidence
   of peer community, alumni stories at depth, interview practice, or assessment-centre preparation.
8. **Employer approval bottleneck.** Vacancies need approving before going live, which means the
   board can lag GOV.UK Find an Apprenticeship, the actual source of truth.

## Bottom line for GTOD

Apprentago is the **most direct competitor in the market and the one to benchmark against**. They
have beaten GTOD to the transactional layer (search, apply, CV, track) and have real revenue.

But they have bought distribution rather than built an audience, and they have a one-visit product.
GTOD's asymmetry is the reverse: an owned, engaged audience and no transactional layer. **The
competitive question is not whether GTOD can out-build Apprentago's feature list — it can't and
shouldn't try — but whether GTOD can add just enough transactional utility to its community faster
than Apprentago can manufacture a community.** Community is much harder to buy than a CV builder is
to build. That asymmetry favours GTOD.
