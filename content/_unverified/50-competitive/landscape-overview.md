## The one thing to understand about this market

**Almost nothing here is funded by students, because students have no money.** Every consumer-facing
site below is free to the young person. The bill is paid by one of four parties:

| Who pays | Mechanism | Consequence for the student |
|---|---|---|
| **Employers** | Job ad slots, profile pages, branding campaigns, events | Coverage skews to employers with early-careers marketing budgets — large corporates. SMEs, where most apprenticeships actually are, are under-represented. |
| **Training providers** | Lead generation — paying for candidate contact details | The student becomes the product. Expect outbound contact from providers. |
| **The DfE / public purse** | Grant-funded resources and the statutory vacancy service | Genuinely impartial, but slow, dry and built for careers leaders rather than teenagers. |
| **Schools** | Subscriptions and premium memberships | Features optimise for Ofsted evidence, not student experience. |

**Nobody in this market is paid to get one specific 17-year-old an offer.** They are paid for reach,
leads and inventory. That misalignment is the structural gap GTOD can exploit — and it is why
outcome metrics are almost never published (the coach-led brands in
`the-apprenticeship-candidate.md` are the sole exception).

## 1. RateMyApprenticeship — now rebranding to **Higherin**

**Funding model:** employer-advertiser. **Free to students.**

The reviews play — "TripAdvisor for school and college leavers". **16,000+ apprentice-written
reviews** of employers, plus vacancy advertising and careers advice. Sister site to RateMyPlacement;
both founded 2007 by three Loughborough undergraduates (Ali Lindsay, Oliver Sidwell, Chris Wickson).
Claims a community of **over 1 million** and support for **4 million+** people finding apprenticeships.
Also runs the **Top 100 Apprenticeship Employers** ranking — a strong employer-side commercial hook.

> [!GAP]
> **Live rebrand.** `ratemyapprenticeship.co.uk` URLs now return Higherin-titled pages and
> `higherin.com` company profiles. The transition state and whether the RMA brand is being retired
> could not be confirmed. **Worth watching: rebrands create SEO and trust turbulence, and this is
> the moment a challenger can take share.**

**Strength:** the review corpus is a genuine moat — a decade of user-generated content that cannot be
bought or scraped legally. **Weakness:** reviews are employer-flattering by selection (apprentices
who post are those still in post), and the site is ad-dense.

## 2. GetMyFirstJob

**Funding model:** employers, colleges and training providers pay for recruitment and marketing
services. **Free to candidates.**

The most institutionally embedded of the private players. Founded by **David Allison** (CEO),
platform launched **2014**; three founders from training and L&D backgrounds. Matches young people
to vacancies via **approved training providers**, who can contact registered candidates proactively.

- **800+** employer, college and training-provider clients
- **1.1 million+** registered users; **2.24 million+** applications submitted
- Milestone claim of **300,000 apprentices** helped into work
- Has an **iOS app**
- Investors include **Nesta, City & Guilds and UnLtd** — impact investors, not pure VC

**Strength:** real matching infrastructure and provider relationships; app presence is rare here.
**Weakness:** the provider-contact model means students get marketed to. Interface and brand feel
built for the system, not for a teenager.

## 3. Notgoingtouni (NGTU)

**Funding model:** jobs-board advertising to employers, training providers, FE colleges, gap-year
providers and SMEs. **Free to students.**

Founded by **Tom Mursell** (notable enough for a Wikipedia entry; 'Best Young Persons Website' award,
Future 100 Young Entrepreneurs). Positions around alternatives to university — apprenticeships,
sponsored degrees, diplomas, gap years, distance learning, jobs.

- **150-200 employers**; **2,000+ live vacancies** at any time

**Strength:** the name is the positioning — unbeatable clarity for the anti-university audience, and
strong brand recall. **Weakness:** small vacancy inventory versus GOV.UK's thousands; essentially a
job board with a great name.

> [!GAP]
> Current ownership unverified — a Trustpilot profile exists but its contents and rating were not
> retrieved, and founder-still-in-charge status is unconfirmed.

## 4. Springpod

**Funding model:** employer and university partnerships, plus **school and college premium
memberships**. **Free student profiles.** Sells employers **credits — 1 credit = 1 learner supported
from enrolment to certification**, explicitly framed like a mobile data plan.

The **virtual work experience** specialist, and the best-funded player here. Each experience is an
on-demand programme of **8-10 hours**: pre-recorded expert talks, interactive tasks, projects and
assignments, built with employers.

- **700,000+** students served
- Published diversity data: **22% eligible for free school meals, 55% from minority backgrounds, 60% female**
- Raised **$2.5m** to expand virtual work-based learning
- **Partnership with UCAS**, which surfaces virtual work experience to its audience

**Strength:** solves a real, scarce problem — work experience is hard to get and required for
applications. The UCAS partnership is enormous distribution. The credit model is clean and scalable.
**Weakness:** 8-10 hours is a big ask; completion rates are unpublished. Content is employer-produced
and therefore promotional.

## 5. Success at School

**Funding model:** employer-advertiser. **Free to students.** Claims to be the most-visited
independent careers website in the UK for ages 13-19.

Careers advice, job listings, "career zones", and employer connection. Sells employers access to
**4,000+ schools and colleges**; posters, guides and lesson plans used by **2,000+ schools**.

**Strength:** content depth, school distribution and reach at the younger end (13+). **Weakness:**
advice-and-listings only — no tools, no tracking, no application support.

> [!GAP]
> **Ownership and funding not established.** Not disclosed in any retrieved extract despite a
> Crunchbase profile existing. Given the sector, assume commercial ownership funded by employer
> advertising until proven otherwise.

## 6. UCAS — apprenticeships

**Funding model:** UCAS is a registered charity funded largely by applicant fees on the
undergraduate side and by **employer/university services** commercially. Apprenticeship **search**
appears free to use.

UCAS now carries apprenticeship listings alongside degree courses, filterable by level, location,
industry and salary, with deadlines and start dates. Also runs **virtual work experience** as a
business line (linked to Springpod).

**Critical limitation, and it is the whole story:** UCAS coverage skews to **higher and degree
apprenticeships** and **does not list every Level 2 and Level 3 role**.

**Strength:** unmatched trust and reach — effectively every Year 13 in England already has a UCAS
account and habit. If UCAS ever fully commits to apprenticeships, it can take the market. **Weakness:**
apprenticeships are a bolt-on to a university-first product, and the incomplete inventory quietly
reinforces the idea that apprenticeships are the lesser path.

## 7. GOV.UK — Find an apprenticeship

**Funding model:** **the taxpayer.** Free, statutory, impartial. Run by DfE.

The legal system of record. Employers advertising English apprenticeships list here, which makes it
the **most complete vacancy database in the market** — everything else is a subset or a re-listing.

- **~3,700 live vacancies** observed in search results (Sept 2026)
- Register a profile, search, and **apply directly**
- **Search alerts** — save a search and be notified
- Must be **16 or over**
- Separate "My apprenticeship" service for people already on a programme

**Strength:** completeness, neutrality, free forever, and it cannot be disintermediated — it is
where the vacancies legally live. **Weakness:** it is a government transaction service. No
inspiration, no explanation of what a job actually *is*, no help writing anything, no community, no
sense of whether you have a chance. **Every commercial player in this market exists because GOV.UK
is complete but cold.** That includes GTOD.

> [!GAP]
> The ~3,700 figure is a point-in-time observation from a search result snippet and fluctuates
> seasonally and sharply — it peaks in spring for September starts.

## 8. AllAboutSchoolLeavers (AllAboutGroup)

**Funding model:** employer-advertiser — "hundreds of employers running digital campaigns".
**Free to students.**

Owned by **AllAboutGroup Holdings Ltd**, company number **06527117**, registered at Lytchett House,
13 Freeland Park, Wareham Road, Poole, Dorset BH16 6FA. A **portfolio publisher**, not a single
site: AllAboutSchoolLeavers, AllAboutApprenticeships, AllAboutLaw, AllAboutFinanceCareers,
AllAboutCareers and MyCareerSpringboard. 10+ years in market. Also runs **School Leaver Awards** with
a ceremony — an events revenue line.

- **600,000+ visitors a year** to AllAboutSchoolLeavers
- Content includes apprenticeship interview practice questions and interview tips

**Strength:** portfolio SEO across many niches; awards and events diversify revenue. **Weakness:**
classic content-plus-jobs-board publishing. No product, no personalisation, no reason to return
between applications.

## 9. Careermap

**Funding model:** employer-advertiser job board. **Free to candidates.**

Operated by **Careermap Limited** (Manchester), part of **Careermap Media Group**. A decade-plus in
market covering apprenticeships, college, university and graduate routes. Runs a **Career Advice
Hub** listed in the Careers & Enterprise Company resource directory, and is a **CDI affiliate member**
— a credibility marker with careers advisers.

> [!GAP]
> **Careermag** — a companion publication — was referenced in the query but not confirmed in
> retrieved extracts. Audience size and traffic unverified.

**Strength:** careers-adviser credibility, CEC directory presence. **Weakness:** the least
differentiated player here; a job board with an advice section.

## 10. Amazing Apprenticeships

**Funding model:** **DfE-funded partner**, since 2015, plus partnership work with **The Careers &
Enterprise Company**. **Free to schools and students.** Not a job board — it does not compete for
vacancies.

Creates, tests and distributes resources for **educators**: posters and display packs, films
featuring apprentices, subject-led materials, guidance documents, and adaptable
Apprenticeships & Technical Education resource suites **with delivery notes so a teacher can run a
session with minimal preparation**. Also produces a "Find an apprenticeship" guide.

**Strength:** deep trust with careers leaders and quasi-official status; a resource pipeline into
schools that no commercial player can match. **Weakness:** the audience is teachers, not teenagers.
Materials are designed to be printed and pinned to a wall.

**Read for GTOD:** this is a **partner, not a competitor.** They need engaging youth-facing content
and creator reach; GTOD has both. A content partnership is plausible and cheap.

## What the map shows

1. **Vacancy data is not a moat.** GOV.UK has everything, free, by law. Every commercial listings
   site is a re-presentation of a public dataset. **Do not compete on inventory.**
2. **Reviews and work experience are moats.** RateMyApprenticeship's 16,000 reviews and Springpod's
   employer-built simulations are the only genuinely defensible assets, because both took years of
   accumulated supply-side relationships.
3. **The market is over-served at discovery and under-served at selection.** Eleven organisations
   help you *find* a vacancy. Almost none help you *pass* an assessment centre. The only ones that do
   are three solo operators.
4. **Nobody has a community.** Not one player in this landscape has peer-to-peer connection between
   applicants. It is eleven broadcast channels and three coaches.
5. **Nobody publishes outcomes.** Because nobody is paid for them.
6. **Trust is fragmenting.** RateMyApprenticeship is mid-rebrand, and the fastest-growing brands are
   individual people on TikTok, not institutions. **Advantage: GTOD.**
