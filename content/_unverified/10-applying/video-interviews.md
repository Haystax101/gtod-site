## In one line

An asynchronous video interview is a recorded, one-way interview with no interviewer present: a question appears, you get a short thinking window and a fixed recording window, and the recording is scored later by a human, an algorithm, or both.[^v4][^v5]

## Key facts

- HireVue's published candidate guidance describes a typical on-demand format of 30 seconds to prepare each question and up to 3 minutes to answer.[^v2]
- HireVue tells candidates most video assessments take under 20 to 30 minutes but to set aside about 45 minutes to be safe.[^v2]
- HireVue's documentation describes a typical AI-scored assessment as 4 to 6 video interview questions matched to the competencies of the role.[^v5][^v8]
- HireVue states its AI scores only the transcript of what you say, and does not use facial expressions, body language, background or tone of voice.[^v5]
- HireVue removed facial analysis from its screening assessments; SHRM reported the feature was discontinued in March 2020 and announced in January 2021, with the company saying visual data no longer added meaningful predictive value.[^v9]
- The ICO audited AI recruitment tool providers and issued nearly 300 recommendations in its November 2024 outcomes report, including that candidates must get clear privacy information and that tools should not infer characteristics such as ethnicity from a name.[^v10][^v11]
- The ICO's guidance for jobseekers states you have a right to be told when automated decision-making is used, to ask how a decision was reached, and to request human review of a decision you believe is wrong.[^v12][^v13]
- Practice recordings are normally private. HireVue tells candidates only they see their practice attempts.[^v2]

> [!GAP]
> The machine that wrote this document could not open vendor pages directly because outbound web access was restricted to indexed search retrieval. Timings such as "30 seconds to prepare, 3 minutes to answer" are HireVue's published defaults; individual employers configure their own timings, and the timing shown in your own invitation always overrides this document.

## How does an asynchronous video interview actually work, step by step?

An asynchronous video interview, also called a one-way, on-demand, pre-recorded or digital interview, works like this:[^v2][^v4][^v17]

1. You get an emailed link with a deadline, typically several days out.
2. You open it in a browser and grant camera and microphone permission.
3. You do a technical check and one or more practice questions. Practice recordings are for you only and are not submitted.[^v2]
4. The real interview starts. A question appears as on-screen text or as a pre-recorded video of a person asking it.
5. A thinking or preparation timer runs. On HireVue's default this is 30 seconds.[^v2]
6. Recording starts automatically and runs to a maximum answer length, on HireVue's default up to 3 minutes.[^v2]
7. Depending on how the employer configured it, you may get a limited number of retakes per question, or none at all.[^v4]
8. When you finish the last question the whole set is submitted at once and cannot be changed.

Willo, another widely used platform, describes "thinking time" as a configurable window before recording begins, set per question, and a separate configurable "time to answer".[^v16] The core point is that the employer chooses these numbers; the platform only supplies the dials.

## How long is a one-way video interview and how many questions is it?

There is no single answer, but the published defaults cluster tightly:

| Element | Published figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation time per question | 30 seconds (HireVue default) | HireVue candidate guidance[^v2] |
| Recording time per question | Up to 3 minutes (HireVue default) | HireVue candidate guidance[^v2] |
| Number of questions in an AI-scored assessment | Typically 4 to 6 | HireVue explainability material[^v5][^v8] |
| Total sitting | Usually under 20 to 30 minutes; allow 45 | HireVue candidate guidance[^v2] |
| Deadline to complete | Typically about a week | Willo platform guidance[^v17] |

Unilever's UK and Ireland Future Leaders Programme page describes its digital interview as three short questions followed by a business case in which candidates solve problems using Unilever scenarios — an example of an employer publishing its own structure rather than using the vendor default.[^v14]

## What is publicly known about AI scoring of video interviews?

This is the part candidates most want to know and where the honest answer is "less than you would like, but more than nothing".

**What HireVue publishes about itself.** HireVue published an AI Explainability Statement, which it describes as an industry first, and has issued updated versions in subsequent years.[^v6][^v7] Its documented pipeline has three stages: transcribe speech to text, interpret the meaning of the text, and score the answer against models trained on how expert human raters scored answers to similar competency questions.[^v5][^v8] HireVue states the scoring uses only the transcript, explicitly excluding facial expressions, body language, surroundings and tone of voice.[^v5] Its technical material describes both word-level features and a neural embedding of each answer.[^v5][^v8]

**The facial analysis history.** HireVue's earlier assessments did analyse video. SHRM reported that the company discontinued facial analysis in March 2020 and announced it in January 2021, on the stated basis that improvements in language analysis meant visual data no longer added meaningful predictive value, with non-verbal data contributing a fraction of a percent to predictive power.[^v9]

**What the UK regulator found across the market.** The ICO ran consensual audits of AI recruitment tool providers and developers and published an outcomes report in November 2024 with almost 300 recommendations. Findings included tools collecting more personal data than necessary and keeping it indefinitely, tools allowing filtering on protected characteristics, and tools inferring gender or ethnicity from an application or even from a name instead of asking. All the audited companies accepted or partially accepted the recommendations.[^v10][^v11]

**What employers are told to do about it.** Amberjack, a UK early-careers specialist, publishes material on quality assurance across both human and AI screening, and trains assessors on video interview assessment aligned to British Psychological Society best practice.[^v19] Amberjack also reports from its 2024/25 data that video interviews did not increase candidate drop-out and could improve candidate experience.[^v20]

> [!GAP]
> No UK employer we could find publishes the actual weighting between AI score and human review for its own early-careers video interview stage, or the score threshold at which an application is rejected. The ICO's own jobseeker guidance acknowledges that a candidate can be rejected on a below-threshold score before a human recruiter sees the application, but the specific thresholds are not published by any employer. Treat any claim about "what the algorithm wants" from a prep site as unverified.

## What are my rights if a video interview is scored by an algorithm?

Under UK data protection law you have rights specific to automated decision-making, and the ICO has published guidance aimed directly at jobseekers:[^v12][^v13]

- You have the right to be told that automated decision-making is being used and to have the employer explain how the tool works and how it affects your application.[^v12]
- You have the right to ask how a decision about you was reached.[^v12]
- Where a decision is made without meaningful human involvement and has a legal or similarly significant effect, you have the right to challenge it and to ask for human review.[^v12][^v13]
- Organisations are expected to test for bias and mitigate it, not simply assume the tool is fair.[^v12]

**GTOD take:** exercising these rights will rarely reverse a rejection from a graduate or apprenticeship scheme, and asking will not put you on a blacklist. What it does reliably do is tell you whether the stage was scored by a person or a model, which is worth knowing before you apply to that employer again.

## What technical setup stops a video interview going wrong?

The most common way to lose a video interview stage is mechanical, not intellectual.

- **Use a laptop or desktop on a wired or strong Wi-Fi connection.** Vendors support browsers directly with no download, but a dropped connection mid-recording is the classic failure.[^v17]
- **Do the technical check the platform offers and do not skip the practice question.** Practice attempts are private and exist precisely so the interface is not new to you.[^v2]
- **Close everything else.** Video conferencing apps, screen recorders and virtual camera software can seize the camera and block the interview.
- **Light your face from in front, not behind.** A window behind you turns you into a silhouette.
- **Use a plain, quiet space and a headset microphone if you have one.** Since HireVue's stated scoring works from a transcript, audio clarity is what actually feeds the model: a mis-transcribed answer is a worse answer.[^v5]
- **Start well before the deadline.** If something fails technically, you need time left to contact the recruiter.
- **Have water and a written prompt sheet out of shot.** Nothing forbids notes in a one-way interview, but reading a script aloud reads as a script.

## How do I request an adjustment for a video interview?

The Equality Act 2010 duty to make reasonable adjustments applies to recruitment assessments, and a video interview is an assessment.[^v22] Practically:

- HireVue's candidate guidance tells candidates they can ask the recruiter for the interview questions in advance as an accommodation, noting HireVue encourages employers to provide them but does not require it.[^v1][^v3]
- Other adjustments to ask for include extra preparation time per question, longer recording windows, additional retakes, an extended deadline, a transcript or captioned questions, or a live interview instead of a recorded one.
- In Civil Service recruitment, additional time on timed elements is granted in bands of 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% depending on assessed need — a useful benchmark for what "extra time" means in practice when you are negotiating with any employer.[^v21]
- Ask at application or as soon as you are invited, not on the day. Once you have submitted a recording it cannot be re-done.

**GTOD take:** asking for the questions in advance is the single highest-value request in this whole stage and almost nobody makes it. HireVue itself tells candidates to ask. The worst outcome is that the employer says no.

## How should I prepare for a one-way video interview?

- **Find out the format first.** The invitation usually states the number of questions, the prep time and the answer time. If it does not, ask the recruiter — that is a fair question, not a favour.
- **Prepare stories, not scripts.** With 30 seconds to think you cannot compose anything new, so what you need in memory is a small set of concrete examples you can attach to whatever gets asked.
- **Structure out loud.** Signposting ("there were three things I did") is how a listener, and a transcript-based model, follows an answer with no interviewer to prompt you.
- **Fill the time but do not pad.** If the window is 3 minutes, a 40-second answer usually means you did not evidence anything. A 2-minute answer with a specific example is generally the shape that works.
- **Answer the motivation question properly.** Unilever, for instance, describes its digital interview stage as designed to learn about candidates' motivation and experience, and publishes its own interview tips page for applicants.[^v14][^v15]
- **Look at the camera lens, not at your own face on screen.** This is the one presentation habit that is genuinely hard and genuinely noticeable to a human reviewer.
- **Record yourself once and watch it back before the real thing.** Practice recordings inside the platform are private.[^v2]

**GTOD take:** the biggest difference between candidates at this stage is not polish, it is whether they had specific examples ready. Everyone is stiff on camera. Not everyone can name a real thing they did last March and say what changed because of it.
