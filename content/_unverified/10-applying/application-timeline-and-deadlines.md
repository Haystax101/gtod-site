## In one line

Apprenticeships have no national deadline — each employer sets its own dates,
most big schemes for a September start open between September and February of
the preceding academic year, and many close the moment they have enough
applications, so the practical deadline is almost always earlier than the
advertised one.

## Key facts

- **There is no UCAS-style master deadline for apprenticeships.** UCAS is
  explicit that apprenticeship vacancies each carry their own closing date and
  that roles can start at any point in the year.[^s1]
- The Office for Students tells prospective degree apprentices that employers
  can advertise at any time of year and that there is no single application
  cycle.[^s10]
- **Applying for an apprenticeship does not use up a UCAS choice.** UCAS
  describes apprenticeship applications as a separate recruitment process that
  runs alongside a university application.[^s1][^s2]
- **Popular schemes close early.** BAE Systems said its 2026 apprenticeship
  applications would close once enough applications had been received and no
  later than 28 February 2026, with some roles expected to close on 20 February
  2026 (dates as reported for the 2026 intake, accessed 2 September
  2026).[^s5][^s6]
- **Some big schemes do not open until the new year.** Rolls-Royce Motor Cars
  ran its 2026 apprenticeship application window through to 28 February 2026,
  with assessment centres in April 2026 and most apprenticeships starting in
  August 2026 (dates as published for the 2026 intake, accessed 2 September
  2026).[^s8]
- **A level results day 2027 is Thursday 12 August 2027; GCSE results day is
  Thursday 19 August 2027** (as reported against the JCQ schedule, accessed 2
  September 2026).[^s18]
- The UCAS equal consideration deadline for 2027 entry is 18:00 UK time on **13
  January 2027**.[^s3]

## Why do these dates change every year?

**Apprenticeship application windows are not fixed and are not regulated.**
Each employer chooses when to open, when to close, how many people to take, and
whether to close early. Careers guidance sources consistently make the same
point: the dates move year to year and some schemes recruit on a rolling basis
until they are full.[^s14][^s16]

That has three practical consequences:

1. **Last year's dates are a hint, not a plan.** A scheme that opened on 1
   September last year may open on 15 September this year, or in January.
2. **A named date can be wrong within weeks.** Every specific date in this
   document is tagged with the intake it belongs to and the date we recorded
   it. Check the employer's page.
3. **You cannot batch your applications.** Because windows are staggered across
   six months, there is no single weekend in which you can do all of them.

**GTOD take:** treat published closing dates as the *latest* the scheme could
possibly close, never as the date to work back from. If an advert says February
and does not say "we may close early", assume it might anyway.

## What is the typical shape of the apprenticeship application year?

For a **September start**, the recruitment year runs roughly from the September
thirteen months before, to the spring five months before. Careers sources
describe most large employers opening between September and November of the
preceding year, with closing dates spread from late October through to early
May, and a distinct later cluster — automotive and some manufacturing
employers — opening in January and February.[^s14][^s15]

| Period | What typically happens | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| September to November (year 13 autumn) | The main opening wave for large degree apprenticeship schemes; finance and professional services tend to open earliest | [^s14][^s15] |
| December to January | Many autumn-opened schemes reach their closing dates; UCAS equal consideration deadline falls 13 January for 2027 entry | [^s3][^s14] |
| January to February | A second opening wave, including engineering, manufacturing and automotive schemes | [^s8][^s14] |
| February to March | Late closing dates cluster here; BAE Systems' 2026 window closed 28 February 2026 | [^s5][^s6] |
| March to June | Assessment centres and interviews; Rolls-Royce Motor Cars held 2026 assessment centres in April 2026 | [^s8] |
| Late June onwards | Remaining and newly posted vacancies on Find an apprenticeship; smaller employers recruit continuously | [^s13] |
| August (results day) | Conditional offers confirmed; late vacancies advertised | [^s11][^s12] |
| September | Most large schemes start; Rolls-Royce Motor Cars' 2026 apprenticeships mostly started in August 2026 | [^s8] |

## Why does applying early genuinely matter?

Because for a large scheme, the advertised closing date is often not the real
one.

- BAE Systems' 2026 apprenticeship recruitment was reported as closing "once
  sufficient applications have been received", with a backstop of 28 February
  2026 and some roles expected to close on 20 February 2026.[^s5][^s6] That is
  an employer publicly telling applicants the date can move forward.
- Careers guidance describes popular schemes closing when they have enough
  applications rather than on the advertised date, and recommends applying
  within the first weeks of a window.[^s14][^s19]

There is a second, less obvious reason. Where an employer processes
applications as they arrive — rolling recruitment — the assessment centre
places fill up over the season. An identical application submitted in October
and in February is competing against a different-sized remaining pool.

**GTOD take:** aim to submit within the first three weeks of a window opening,
but not on day one at the cost of quality. A careless application submitted
early is worse than a good one submitted in week three. The right way to buy
that time is to have your CV, your worked examples and your standard answers
already drafted in the summer before, so that when a window opens you are
editing, not writing.

> [!GAP]
> We could not verify any published data showing that early applicants to a
> given UK apprenticeship scheme have a higher success rate than late ones.
> The early-application advice above rests on employers' own statements that
> they close when full,[^s5][^s6] which is a different and weaker claim. An
> employer publishing offer rates by application week would settle it. Do not
> tell a student that applying early raises their odds as a matter of
> evidenced fact.

## Should I apply in year 12 or year 13?

**Apply in year 13 for a scheme starting the September after you finish sixth
form.** Year 12 is preparation, not application.

**What year 12 is actually for:**

- Building the target employer list and registering for their alerts.
- Getting work experience or virtual work experience that gives you concrete
  examples to write about.
- Sitting any early insight days or spring weeks employers run for year 12s.
- Drafting the reusable parts of your application over the summer, which
  careers guidance specifically recommends as year 12 summer work.[^s21]

**Why not apply in year 12?** Almost all school-leaver degree apprenticeship
schemes are recruiting for a start date after your A levels, so a year 12
applicant is applying a year early and will normally be screened out on
availability. The exception is a **September start straight after year 12** —
possible if you are leaving education at 17 with the entry requirements met,
but rare for degree-level schemes.

**GTOD take:** the genuinely valuable year 12 activity is the employer list and
the insight days, because insight days are the one thing that gets easier to
convert into an offer and that you cannot do retrospectively in year 13.

> [!GAP]
> We could not verify a general rule about minimum age or year-group
> eligibility across degree apprenticeship schemes. Some employers state a
> minimum age of 18 at start date for safety or regulatory reasons; we have
> not confirmed which. Checking three or four named employers' eligibility
> pages would settle this.

## Can I apply for apprenticeships and university at the same time?

Yes, and you should. UCAS treats them as separate processes, so an
apprenticeship application does not consume one of your five UCAS
choices.[^s1][^s2]

The two calendars interact at three points:

1. **13 January 2027, 18:00 UK time** — the UCAS equal consideration deadline
   for 2027 entry.[^s3] This falls in the middle of apprenticeship closing
   season, so December is a genuinely heavy month.
2. **Spring** — you may be holding a UCAS firm and insurance choice while still
   in an apprenticeship assessment process.
3. **Results day** — you decide between them, if you are lucky enough to hold
   both.

**GTOD take:** submit the UCAS application even if the apprenticeship is your
first choice. It costs one application fee and a personal statement you have
largely written anyway, and it removes the pressure that makes people accept a
mediocre apprenticeship offer out of fear.

> [!GAP]
> PwC's Flying Start degree programmes are an apparent exception to the
> "apprenticeships are not applied for through UCAS" rule — search results
> indicate most Flying Start programmes are applied for through UCAS with a
> course code, alongside an application to PwC.[^s17] We could not confirm in
> this session which Flying Start routes are apprenticeships, which are
> sponsored degrees, and how the two applications interlock. Anyone advising
> on Flying Start should read PwC's own entry-level page before answering.

## What should I do in each term?

### Year 12, summer term and summer holiday

- Build the list of 15 to 30 target employers and register for their alerts.
- Do at least one piece of work experience, virtual or physical.
- Draft your CV and a bank of four to six worked examples (a time you led, a
  time you solved a problem, a time you worked in a team, a time something went
  wrong). Careers guidance specifically frames the year 12 summer as the
  preparation window.[^s21]

### Year 13, September to October

- Watch the autumn opening wave. Finance and professional services schemes tend
  to open first.[^s14][^s15]
- Submit to schemes as they open, targeting the first three weeks.
- Check the Amazing Apprenticeships higher and degree vacancy listing, which is
  curated to degree level.[^s20]

### Year 13, November to December

- Continue applying; complete online assessments quickly, because many are
  time-limited once issued.
- Finish and submit the UCAS application ahead of the 13 January
  deadline.[^s3]

### Year 13, January to February

- The second opening wave. Do not assume the season is over in
  January — BAE Systems' and Rolls-Royce Motor Cars' 2026 windows both ran to
  28 February 2026.[^s5][^s6][^s8]
- Watch for early closures announced mid-window.[^s5]

### Year 13, March to June

- Assessment centres and final interviews.[^s8]
- Keep applying to anything still open; smaller employers advertise on Find an
  apprenticeship all year.[^s13]
- Sit your exams. A levels for 2027 run from Monday 10 May 2027, with most
  finishing by Friday 18 June 2027 and a JCQ contingency day of Wednesday 23
  June 2027 (as reported, accessed 2 September 2026).[^s18]

### Year 13, July and August

- If you hold a conditional apprenticeship offer, confirm with the employer
  exactly what they need from you on results day and how to send it.
- If you hold nothing, this is not the end of the road — see results day below.

## What happens on results day if I have an apprenticeship offer?

**An apprenticeship offer is almost always conditional on your results, but
there is no Clearing equivalent and no same-day deadline.**

- Degree apprenticeship offers can be made conditionally while you are still
  studying and waiting for results.[^s16]
- Unlike university, there is no national system that automatically reallocates
  you if you miss the conditions. You deal directly with the employer.
- DfE's own results-day guidance points school leavers towards apprenticeships
  as a next step and towards Find an apprenticeship for live
  vacancies.[^s11][^s12]

**What to actually do on the day:**

1. Get your results and read your offer letter's exact conditions.
2. If you met them, contact the employer or their recruitment inbox the same
   day to confirm, and expect an onboarding and university-registration process
   to follow if it is a degree apprenticeship.
3. **If you narrowly missed**, contact the employer immediately and ask
   directly whether they will still take you. Employers have discretion here in
   a way universities largely do not, and some will accept a near miss,
   especially if the shortfall is in a subject not central to the role. Do not
   assume the offer is dead until they say so.
4. If the offer is withdrawn, search Find an apprenticeship the same
   week — vacancies are advertised year-round and results season generates
   fresh ones.[^s13][^s12]

> [!GAP]
> We could not verify any published employer policy on how a narrowly missed
> grade is handled for an apprenticeship offer, or any data on how often
> apprenticeship offers are honoured after a missed condition. This is the
> single most useful thing to add to this document. Named employers publishing
> a "what if I miss my grades" line on their early careers FAQ would settle
> it.

## What if I miss the whole cycle?

Careers guidance notes that degree apprenticeship employers and their
university partners generally run a single annual intake, so missing a cycle
usually means waiting for the next academic year.[^s16] That is a real cost and
worth planning around.

It is not, however, the same as missing out entirely: Find an apprenticeship
carries vacancies at all levels throughout the year, and smaller employers
recruit continuously rather than in an annual wave.[^s13][^s10] The annual-wave
pattern is specific to large branded schemes.

See `./reapplying-and-rejection.md` for what to do with a missed or failed
cycle.
