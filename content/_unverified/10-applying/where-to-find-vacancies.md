## In one line

There is no single complete list of UK apprenticeship vacancies, so a serious
search runs the government service for your nation, one or two school-leaver
job boards, and — most importantly — the careers pages of the specific
employers you actually want to work for.

## Key facts

- The government's vacancy service for England is **Find an apprenticeship**,
  at `findapprenticeship.service.gov.uk`, reached from GOV.UK. It is free, and
  you register a profile, search, and apply through it.[^s1][^s2]
- Find an apprenticeship covers **England only**. Scotland, Wales and Northern
  Ireland each have a separate service, and GOV.UK says so explicitly.[^s1]
- Between August 2025 and June 2026 there were 46,640 adverts covering 69,820
  vacancies on Find an apprenticeship (figures as at the 2025/26 DfE
  apprenticeships statistics release, accessed 2 September 2026).[^s4]
- The Office for Students, writing for prospective degree apprentices, says
  employers can advertise a degree apprenticeship at any point in the year and
  that there is no single application cycle the way there is for university
  entry.[^s22]
- Every site listed in this document is **free to the applicant**. The job
  boards make their money from employers and training providers, not from
  students.[^s7][^s9][^s11]
- RateMyApprenticeship and RateMyPlacement merged into a single brand,
  **Higherin**, in August 2025; `higherin.com` is the current home, and old
  RateMyApprenticeship URLs still resolve (as at 2 September 2026).[^s8][^s9]

## Which service covers which UK nation?

Apprenticeship policy is devolved, so the official vacancy service depends on
where the *job* is, not where you live.

| Nation | Official service | URL | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| England | Find an apprenticeship | https://www.findapprenticeship.service.gov.uk/ | [^s1][^s2] |
| Scotland | apprenticeships.scot (Skills Development Scotland) | https://www.apprenticeships.scot/find-a-vacancy/ | [^s17] |
| Wales | Find an apprenticeship (Welsh Government) / Careers Wales | https://www.gov.wales/find-apprenticeship | [^s19][^s20] |
| Northern Ireland | nidirect, pointing to JobApplyNI | https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/campaigns/apprenticeships | [^s21] |

**GTOD take:** if you live near a border — north Wales into Cheshire, the
central belt into Cumbria — search both nations' services. The vacancy is
listed where the employer is registered, not where you commute from.

## What does GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship actually cover?

**Find an apprenticeship** is the Department for Education's own vacancy
service for England.[^s1][^s2]

- **Covers:** apprenticeship vacancies in England at every level, from
  intermediate through to degree apprenticeships. The service lists adverts
  placed by employers and by training providers on employers' behalf.[^s2][^s4]
- **Cost:** free.[^s1]
- **Account:** you register a profile, search vacancies and apply through the
  service. GOV.UK states you must be 16 or over.[^s1] Creating an account
  requires a GOV.UK One Login.[^s3] An account lets you save vacancies and set
  up alerts for new ones.[^s3]
- **Application cap:** the service limits how many live vacancies you can hold
  applications for at one time.

> [!GAP]
> Two numbers here need re-checking against the live pages. First, guidance
> pages give different minimum ages — GOV.UK says 16 or over to register and
> apply,[^s1] while apprenticeships.gov.uk describes account creation from age
> 14.[^s3] The likely reconciliation is that a younger student can hold an
> account but not submit an application, but neither page states that
> outright. Second, the "apply for up to ten live vacancies at any one time"
> cap is widely repeated and appears in guidance about the service, but we
> could not confirm the current number on the service's own page in this
> session. Do not quote a specific cap to a student without re-checking.

**Real weaknesses of Find an apprenticeship:**

- It is England-only, so it is the wrong tool entirely for a job in Scotland,
  Wales or Northern Ireland.[^s1]
- Its coverage is skewed towards vacancies placed through training providers.
  Large employers running competitive, branded degree apprenticeship schemes
  often recruit through their own applicant tracking system, and university and
  careers-service guidance routinely tells applicants to check employer careers
  pages directly as well as the government service.[^s22][^s25]
- Search quality is only as good as the advert. Employers write their own job
  titles, so a "Level 6 Digital and Technology Solutions Professional" advert
  may not surface on a search for "software degree apprenticeship".

## What does UCAS offer for apprenticeships?

UCAS runs apprenticeship content and a vacancy search alongside its
university admissions service.[^s5][^s6]

- **Covers:** apprenticeship and school-leaver vacancies across the UK, listed
  by employers on **UCAS Career Finder**. UCAS lets you filter by
  apprenticeship type, including degree apprenticeships, and by
  region.[^s6][^s7]
- **Cost:** free to students. Career Finder is a paid product for the employers
  who advertise on it.[^s7]
- **Alerts:** UCAS describes a service that lets prospective apprentices sign
  up for personalised vacancy alerts matched to their preferences.[^s7]
- **You do not apply through UCAS Apply.** UCAS's undergraduate application
  system is for university courses. Apprenticeship vacancies found via UCAS are
  applied for with the employer.

**Real weaknesses of UCAS for apprenticeships:** coverage depends on which
employers have paid to advertise, so it is a curated slice rather than a
census. Its strength is the opposite of Find an apprenticeship's — UCAS is
weighted towards larger, brand-name employers running structured schemes.

## What is Higherin (formerly RateMyApprenticeship)?

RateMyApprenticeship, RateMyPlacement and On-Campus Promotions merged into one
brand, **Higherin**, in August 2025.[^s8]

- **Covers:** live apprenticeship, degree apprenticeship and school-leaver
  vacancies, plus its distinctive asset — thousands of reviews of schemes
  written by people who actually did them, rated out of ten and covering
  responsibilities, skills and company culture.[^s9][^s10]
- **Cost:** free. A free account gets you matched opportunities by email and
  deadline reminders, and a dashboard for managing applications.[^s10]
- **Age range:** Higherin describes supporting students aged 14 to 24 and
  above.[^s8]

**What Higherin is genuinely the best source for:** the reviews. Aggregator
review scores are one of the few things where the aggregator *is* the primary
source, because it collected the data.[^s10] Use it to find out what a scheme
is like day to day before you spend four hours on an application.

**Real weaknesses of Higherin:** reviews are self-selected — people with strong
opinions write them — and a scheme's reviews may be several years old and
describe a programme that has since changed. Employer rankings and "top 100"
style lists on any of these sites reflect that site's own methodology and
voting base, not an independent audit.[^s26] The rebrand also means older
school and college careers pages still link to `ratemyapprenticeship.co.uk`.

## What is GetMyFirstJob?

**GetMyFirstJob** is a school-leaver careers platform covering apprenticeships,
degree apprenticeships, graduate roles and work experience.[^s11]

- **Covers:** vacancies from employers and from accredited training providers.
  Its own description references working with 800+ companies and training
  providers.[^s11]
- **Cost:** free to register.
- **Distinctive feature:** it is training-provider-heavy. When you register,
  providers can contact you about vacancies, which is genuinely useful for
  non-degree-level and local roles that never get a glossy advert.[^s11]

**Real weakness of GetMyFirstJob:** because providers can contact you, expect
outbound contact from organisations you did not specifically apply to. That is
the trade for the coverage.

## What is Notgoingtouni?

**Notgoingtouni** is a job board aimed squarely at people choosing an
alternative to the university route.[^s12]

- **Covers:** apprenticeships plus school-leaver programmes, gap year
  providers, FE colleges and training providers. Its own description references
  working with 150 to 200 employers and holding over 2,000 live vacancies at a
  time, spanning accounting and finance, engineering, HR and other fields.[^s12]
- **Cost:** free to applicants.

**Real weakness of Notgoingtouni:** the listing mix includes gap year and
provider content alongside actual employed apprenticeships, so read what a
listing actually is before you invest time in it.

## What is Success at School?

**Success at School** is a careers information site with a jobs section for
school leavers.[^s13]

- **Covers:** apprenticeships, school-leaver training programmes and
  internships across a wide range of fields.[^s13]
- **Cost:** free.
- **Distinctive feature:** it is primarily an advice and careers-education
  publisher, and the jobs board sits alongside that content.

**Real weakness of Success at School:** its vacancy volume is smaller than the
dedicated boards. Treat it as a supplement, not a primary search.

## What is Springpod, and is it a vacancy site?

**Springpod is mainly a virtual work experience platform, not a vacancy
board.**[^s14] It matters for apprenticeship applications for a different
reason.

- **Covers:** free virtual work experience programmes, run with employers and
  universities, plus apprenticeship insight content. Programmes typically run
  around 6 to 10 hours, can be paused and resumed, and issue a completion
  certificate.[^s14]
- **Cost:** free to students. Springpod also runs work experience in
  collaboration with UCAS.[^s14]

**GTOD take:** the reason to use Springpod is that it manufactures evidence.
The single hardest question in a 17-year-old's application is "give an example
of your interest in this industry", and a completed virtual work experience is
a concrete, dated, verifiable answer. It is not a substitute for applying.

**Real weakness of Springpod:** completing a virtual work experience is not the
same as an offer, a placement, or a reference, and no employer is obliged to
weight it. Do not let it displace time spent on actual applications.

## What are AllAboutSchoolLeavers and Amazing Apprenticeships?

- **AllAboutSchoolLeavers** lists apprenticeships at every level plus
  school-leaver programmes and sponsored degrees, filterable by employer and
  location, with accompanying advice content.[^s15] Free to applicants.
- **Amazing Apprenticeships** publishes a regularly updated **higher and degree
  apprenticeship vacancy listing** — a downloadable PDF of live higher and
  degree-level vacancies with direct application links — alongside a search and
  apply hub.[^s16][^s28] Free.

**GTOD take:** the Amazing Apprenticeships higher and degree listing is the
single most underused resource in this document for anyone specifically after a
degree apprenticeship. It is curated to the level you want, which no general
search is. Its weakness is that it is a periodic snapshot: a PDF published in
one month will contain vacancies that have since closed, so always click
through and confirm the vacancy is still live before you write anything.

> Amazing Apprenticeships previously circulated a monthly "Vacancy Snapshot"

> Employer" zone. We could not confirm in this session which of the two is

## Should I use LinkedIn and Indeed for apprenticeships?

Yes, as a secondary net, not a primary one.

- **Indeed UK** and **LinkedIn** both carry large volumes of UK apprenticeship
  and apprentice-titled roles and both support saved searches and email
  alerts.[^s23][^s24]
- **Cost:** free to job seekers on both.

**Real weaknesses of LinkedIn and Indeed for apprenticeships:**

- Neither distinguishes a statutory apprenticeship (with off-the-job training
  and a qualification) from a job that merely uses the word "apprentice" or
  "trainee" in its title. You have to read the advert to tell.
- Aggregated listings are often scraped from elsewhere, so you get duplicates
  and occasionally listings that have already closed.
- Coverage of the big structured degree apprenticeship schemes is patchy,
  because those employers usually drive traffic to their own careers site.

**GTOD take:** LinkedIn's real value for an apprenticeship applicant is not the
job search. It is following the early careers or future talent accounts of the
five or ten employers you actually want, so you see the announcement the day
applications open. That is a materially different use of the platform.

## Why do I still have to check employer careers pages?

Because the most competitive schemes are frequently recruited there first, and
sometimes only there. University degree apprenticeship guidance and the Office
for Students both direct applicants to employer careers pages alongside the
official service.[^s22][^s25]

**How to run the employer-direct search properly:**

1. Build a list of 15 to 30 target employers, using the review sites and
   employer ranking lists to find names you did not already know.[^s10][^s26]
2. Find each one's "Early Careers", "Future Talent", "Emerging Talent" or
   "Students and Graduates" page — the apprenticeship route is almost always
   filed under one of those four labels, not under the main jobs page.[^s25]
3. Register for that employer's own talent alerts where offered.
4. Diarise a manual re-check for employers whose scheme opens on a fixed date.

**GTOD take:** this is the step most applicants skip and it is the one that
decides outcomes. An hour spent building the employer list once, in September,
is worth more than an hour a week spent refreshing a generic job search all
year.

## What about colleges, training providers and universities?

- **Training providers** advertise vacancies they are recruiting for on behalf
  of employers, and many appear on GetMyFirstJob and the government
  service.[^s11][^s2]
- **Universities that deliver degree apprenticeships** publish lists of the
  employer partners they work with and pages explaining how to find and apply
  for one — Loughborough's is an example of the genre.[^s25]
- **Local colleges** run their own vacancy lists for the apprenticeships they
  deliver, which are often the best route to smaller local employers who never
  advertise nationally.

**Important distinction:** for a degree apprenticeship you apply to the
**employer**, not to the university. The university is the training provider
the employer sends you to. Some employers then require you to complete a
separate university registration once you have the job, but the offer comes
from the employer.[^s22]

> [!GAP]
> We have not verified a single authoritative national directory of colleges
> and training providers advertising apprenticeship vacancies. If one exists it
> would be a useful addition here; checking whether Skills England or the
> apprenticeship service publishes a provider directory would settle it.

## What is the honest ranking of these sources?

**GTOD take**, in the order we would actually use them for a degree
apprenticeship search:

1. **Target employers' own careers pages** — highest signal, lowest coverage.
2. **Amazing Apprenticeships higher and degree listing** — curated to level.[^s16]
3. **Find an apprenticeship** (or your nation's equivalent) — the widest legal
   coverage.[^s2][^s17][^s19][^s21]
4. **UCAS Career Finder** and **Higherin** — good for discovering employers you
   had not heard of, and Higherin for reviews before you commit time.[^s6][^s9]
5. **GetMyFirstJob, Notgoingtouni, AllAboutSchoolLeavers, Success at
   School** — useful breadth, especially for non-degree-level and local roles.
6. **LinkedIn and Indeed** — a backstop, and a following tool.

Set up email alerts on at least three of them. The failure mode is not
searching badly, it is searching once in November and again in March.
