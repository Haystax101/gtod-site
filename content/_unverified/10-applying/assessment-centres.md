## In one line

A UK early-careers assessment centre is a half or full day of standardised exercises — usually some combination of group exercise, case study, presentation, role play, written exercise and interview — scored by trained assessors against a published framework rather than against the other candidates in the room.[^a24][^a25]

## Key facts

- The Civil Service scores candidates against Success Profiles, a published five-element framework of Behaviours, Strengths, Ability, Experience and Technical, with only the relevant elements used for any given role.[^a1][^a5]
- The Civil Service Fast Stream Assessment Centre is delivered virtually on a laptop and consists of a written advice exercise, a stakeholder communication exercise and a personal development conversation.[^a7]
- The FSAC personal development conversation is a 30-minute one-to-one role play in which an assessor plays your line manager.[^a7]
- The FSAC written advice exercise suggests a working shape of roughly 20 minutes reading the brief, 60 minutes answering and 5 minutes checking.[^a8]
- The Civil Service's now-closed Fast Track Apprenticeship assessment centre was a half day, and its group exercise put candidates in groups of three to five with a shared brief plus an individual brief, with no appointed chair.[^a10][^a11]
- KPMG's apprentice Launch Pad is an in-person half-day event combining two assessed activities, an interview and a group assessment, with five non-assessed group activities.[^a15]
- PwC's early-careers final stage is an in-person interview with a senior leader from the business area you have been matched to, preceded by a virtual immersive assessment centre.[^a12]
- Deloitte's early-careers final stage assessment has two exercises: a discussion of a topic you prepared in advance, around 30 to 40 minutes, and a skills and motivation interview, around 50 minutes.[^a17][^a18]
- The NHS assesses candidate values against the NHS Constitution through values-based recruitment, delivered through methods including pre-screening, values-based interviews, role play, written scenario responses and assessment centres.[^a22][^a23]
- CIPD guidance is that assessment centre selectors must be trained to observe, record, classify and rate behaviour against the job description and person specification, and that centres should be overseen by experienced selectors for consistency.[^a24][^a25]

## What is an assessment centre and why do apprenticeship employers run one?

An assessment centre is a selection event where several candidates are assessed at the same time, across several different exercises, by several trained assessors. The point of the design is that no single exercise and no single assessor decides your outcome: each behaviour is meant to be observed more than once, in more than one situation.[^a24][^a25]

Assessment centres combine behavioural ratings from observed exercises with evidence from other sources such as cognitive and personality assessment.[^a25] For apprenticeships this is usually the final stage, after the application form, the online test and the video interview.[^a12][^a15]

**GTOD take:** the single most useful mental shift is to stop treating it as a competition against the other candidates. Assessors are scoring you against a fixed benchmark. In principle everyone in your group can pass, and in practice everyone in your group can fail.

## What exercises does a UK assessment centre actually contain?

| Exercise | What happens | What it is used to score |
|---|---|---|
| Group exercise | Three to six candidates get a shared scenario and often an individual brief, and must reach a decision together in a set time[^a10] | Working with others, influencing, listening, contribution quality[^a10][^a24] |
| Case study or analysis exercise | You get a pack of business information and must recommend a course of action | Analysis, judgement, prioritisation under time pressure[^a8] |
| Presentation | You present a recommendation, sometimes prepared in advance, sometimes on the day | Communication, structure, ability to handle questions[^a17][^a18] |
| Role play | A one-to-one scenario with an assessor playing a colleague, client or manager[^a7] | Interpersonal skill, handling difficult conversations, managing a relationship[^a7] |
| Written exercise | You draft a brief, an email or advice from source material against the clock[^a8] | Written communication, structuring an argument for a specific reader[^a8] |
| Interview | Competency, strengths or values-based, sometimes with a senior leader[^a12][^a17] | Behaviours, strengths, motivation and fit[^a1][^a3] |
| Informal social elements | Lunch, office tour, sessions with current apprentices, non-assessed group activities[^a15] | Usually not scored, but see below |

### What does a group exercise involve?

The Civil Service Fast Track Apprenticeship guide gives one of the clearest published descriptions of a UK early-careers group exercise. Candidates worked in groups of three to five. Everyone received the same brief describing the scenario and the main options, and everyone also got an individual brief setting out different options with supporting material. Groups were explicitly told not to appoint a chair.[^a10]

That last detail matters. A group exercise designed with no chair is designed so that self-appointing as chair is not a shortcut to a good score.

**GTOD take:** the reliable way to score in a group exercise is to be the person who makes the group's decision better: bring your unique information out early, ask a quiet person a direct question, keep an eye on the clock, and summarise where the group has got to. Talking most is not scoring most.

### What does a written exercise involve?

The Fast Stream written advice exercise asks candidates to give written advice and recommendations to a senior team in a government department about the implications of a proposal. The published guidance suggests spending about 20 minutes reading and watching briefing material, about 60 minutes writing the answer and about 5 minutes reviewing.[^a8] At FSAC the written advice exercise and the stakeholder communication exercise share a single overarching policy theme, so the material carries across.[^a7]

### What does a role play involve?

The Fast Stream's personal development conversation is a 30-minute one-to-one role play in which the assessor plays your line manager and questions you about assessing or implementing a government policy.[^a7] The general pattern across employers is the same: a named fictional counterpart, a short brief given in advance, and an assessor who will push back on whatever you say to see how you respond.

### Do the informal parts count?

Employers usually distinguish assessed from non-assessed activities. KPMG's apprentice Launch Pad publicly describes two assessed activities and five non-assessed group activities in the same half-day event.[^a15]

**GTOD take:** treat "non-assessed" as meaning "not scored on a form", not "invisible". You are in a building full of people who will work with whoever gets hired. Being rude to a receptionist has ended more applications than a weak case study ever has. Equally, do not perform through lunch — the non-assessed sessions are also your only real chance to find out whether you want the job.

## What are assessors actually scoring, and how?

Assessors do not score a general impression. They score evidence of specific behaviours against a defined framework.

CIPD's guidance is that selectors must be trained to observe, actively listen, record, classify and rate behaviour, and to seek evidence objectively against the job description and person specification, with experienced selectors overseeing the centre for consistency.[^a24][^a25] CIPD also flags that assessor subjectivity is a real source of error, citing research where interviewer differences accounted for a substantial share of score variance — which is exactly why multiple assessors and multiple exercises exist.[^a25]

### The Civil Service Success Profiles framework

Success Profiles is the published recruitment framework used across the Civil Service, and it is the most transparent competency framework any large UK employer publishes. It has five elements:[^a1][^a5]

- **Behaviours** — the actions and activities that result in effective performance, defined separately for each grade.[^a2][^a6]
- **Strengths** — the things you do regularly, do well, and are motivated by.[^a3]
- **Ability** — aptitude or potential to perform to the required standard.[^a1]
- **Experience** — knowledge or mastery gained through exposure to an activity or subject.[^a1]
- **Technical** — specific professional skills, knowledge or qualifications.[^a1]

Not every element is used for every role; the job advert says which apply.[^a1][^a5] Behaviours are grade-specific, so the standard expected of an apprentice-level role is defined differently from a senior one.[^a2][^a6]

Scoring is against benchmarks rather than a ranking. A Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service page describing its use of Success Profiles explains a benchmark as a score a candidate must reach across a single element or a combination — for example a minimum score on lead behaviours, or a required total across four behaviours.[^a26]

**GTOD take:** if you are applying anywhere public sector, read the Success Profiles behaviour definitions for the grade you are applying to before you write a word. The scoring language is published. Almost nobody uses it.

### The NHS values-based recruitment framework

NHS recruitment adds a values dimension. Values-based recruitment attracts and selects people whose individual values and behaviours align with the values of the NHS Constitution, alongside the usual assessment of aptitude and skills.[^a22]

The national framework, first published in 2014 and refreshed in 2016, states that values-based recruitment can be delivered through pre-screening assessments, values-based interview techniques, role play, written responses to scenarios and assessment centre approaches.[^a22][^a23] NHS Employers also publishes a behaviour framework that can be used as interview scoring criteria.[^a22]

## What do specific UK early-careers assessment centres look like?

| Employer | Final-stage format as published | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Civil Service Fast Stream | Virtual assessment centre on a laptop: written advice exercise, stakeholder communication exercise, 30-minute personal development conversation role play | Civil Service Careers[^a7] |
| Civil Service Fast Track Apprenticeship (now closed) | Half-day centre including a group exercise in groups of 3 to 5, shared brief plus individual briefs, no appointed chair | FTAC Guide 2018[^a10][^a11] |
| PwC (early careers) | Virtual immersive assessment centre, then an in-person interview with a senior leader in the matched business area | PwC UK[^a12] |
| KPMG (apprentice) | In-person half-day Launch Pad: two assessed activities (interview and group assessment) plus five non-assessed group activities | KPMG UK[^a15][^a16] |
| Deloitte (early careers) | Final Stage Assessment: prepared-topic discussion of about 30 to 40 minutes, plus a skills and motivation interview of about 50 minutes | Deloitte UK[^a17][^a18] |
| EY (early careers, UK) | Online assessments combining rank-order, written and recorded video answers, plus a timed numerical reasoning test where applicable | EY UK[^a20][^a21] |

Two employer details are worth calling out because they are unusual and published:

- **Deloitte tells candidates to use generative AI for the preparation.** Its early-careers integrity guidance asks candidates to use GenAI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot alongside other research methods when researching the final assessment topic, while prohibiting AI use in other stages.[^a19] Read the employer's own integrity guidance before assuming AI use is banned or allowed.
- **PwC opens its process with a non-assessed values activity** designed to let you decide whether its culture suits you, before any scored assessment starts.[^a12]

## How does a virtual assessment centre differ from an in-person one?

Both formats are common and some employers run both in the same process — PwC's published sequence is a virtual immersive assessment centre followed by an in-person final interview.[^a12] KPMG's Launch Pad is in person.[^a15] The Fast Stream assessment centre is delivered virtually via a platform accessed on a laptop.[^a7]

Practical differences that matter:

- **Virtual centres are usually shorter and more modular.** Exercises are scheduled as separate slots rather than a continuous day.
- **Virtual group exercises are harder to contribute to.** Turn-taking has no body language, so you must verbally signal that you want to speak and explicitly bring quieter people in.
- **Technical failure is a real risk in virtual centres.** Test the platform, use a wired or strong connection, and know who to phone if you drop out.
- **In-person centres carry travel and cost.** See expenses below.
- **In-person centres give you more information about the employer.** You see the office, the people and the culture, which is the half of the day that is for your benefit.

## Does an employer pay my expenses for an assessment centre?

There is no legal requirement in the UK for an employer to reimburse a candidate's travel to an assessment centre, and practice varies widely.

The clearest published example is Civil Service external recruitment expenses guidance, which sets out an entitlement to claim travel to an assessment centre, tests or interview on stated terms: travel limited to the cost of the direct journey from where you currently stay to the venue; public transport used wherever possible; taxis normally only where no suitable public transport exists; a mileage rate for car travel; and one night's accommodation where you live too far to travel on the day. Claims had to be submitted with original receipts within four weeks of attending.[^a27] The same guidance notes that external candidate interview expenses are paid in specified circumstances, including where a candidate is unemployed, a school leaver or graduate, or must travel a significant distance.[^a27]

> [!GAP]
> That Civil Service expenses guidance is an old published document and its mileage rate and claim deadline may have changed. We could not verify a current, centrally published UK-wide expenses rule for private-sector apprenticeship assessment centres, and most large employers do not publish their expenses policy at all. Ask the recruitment contact directly, in writing, before you book travel.

**GTOD take:** ask. Every recruiter has been asked this before and it does not count against you. Ask three specific things: whether travel is reimbursed, whether an overnight stay is covered if you are travelling far, and whether they can pay or book in advance rather than reimburse — because "we will reimburse you in six weeks" is not the same offer to a 17-year-old as it is to a graduate with a credit card.

## What adjustments can I ask for at an assessment centre?

The Equality Act 2010 duty to make reasonable adjustments applies to recruitment assessments, not just to the job.[^a28] Employers publish what they will do.

PwC's early-careers adjustments page states that it will make reasonable adjustments for disabled and neurodivergent applicants, that you can request adjustments within the online application form, and that requests are kept confidential. Its published examples of adjustments already provided include extra time on online assessments, hard copy materials, office orientation before the day, and use of laptops with assistive technology for in-person assessments.[^a13][^a14]

Adjustments worth asking for at an assessment centre stage specifically:

- The exercise briefs or the case study material in advance, or in a different format.
- Extra time on written and analysis exercises. The Civil Service benchmark for timed elements is extra time in bands of 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% depending on assessed need.[^a29]
- A quiet room, a rest break between exercises, or a longer gap between exercises.
- An orientation of the building before the assessed day starts.[^a13]
- Assistive technology on the machine you will use.[^a13]
- A one-to-one alternative where a group exercise would create a substantial disadvantage.

Ask at application, or as soon as you are invited. Adjustments that need a room booking, a printed pack or a hardware change cannot be arranged on the morning.[^a13]

## How should I prepare for an assessment centre?

- **Find the framework and use its words.** For public sector, that is Success Profiles behaviours for your grade.[^a2][^a6] For the NHS, it is the NHS Constitution values and the values-based behaviour framework.[^a22][^a23] For most private employers it is a published list of skills or capabilities on the careers site.
- **Read the employer's own assessment page and its integrity guidance.** Employers differ on whether AI use in preparation is expected or banned.[^a19]
- **Prepare specific examples, not adjectives.** Every framework is scored on evidence of behaviour.[^a24]
- **Practise the group exercise mechanics, not the content.** Timekeeping, summarising, bringing others in, and stating your unique information early are the transferable moves.[^a10]
- **Prepare commercial context for the employer.** What they do, who their clients are, what changed in their market this year.
- **Ask for adjustments and expenses in advance, in writing.**[^a13][^a27]
- **Prepare your own questions.** The informal parts of the day are the only chance you get to find out whether the apprenticeship is any good.
