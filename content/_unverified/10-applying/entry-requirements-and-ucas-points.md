## In one line

Two separate things govern whether you can do an apprenticeship: the
**employer's own entry bar**, which they set themselves and which varies
enormously between schemes, and the **statutory English and maths rule**, which
is a condition of *completing* an apprenticeship rather than of starting one.

## Key facts

- **Apprenticeship entry requirements are set by the employer, not by
  government.** There is no national minimum grade for a degree
  apprenticeship. Two employers offering the same apprenticeship standard can
  demand entirely different grades.[^s20]
- **Apprentices aged 16 to 18 at the start of training must achieve English and
  maths qualifications to complete their apprenticeship** if they do not
  already hold a suitable equivalent — normally up to an approved level 2
  qualification.[^s1]
- **For apprentices aged 19 and over, the mandatory English and maths pass
  requirement was removed from 11 February 2025.** Adult apprentices instead
  demonstrate occupationally relevant English and maths skills within the
  apprenticeship (change confirmed by DfE, as reported February 2025, accessed
  2 September 2026).[^s3]
- **UCAS tariff points for A levels:** A* = 56, A = 48, B = 40, C = 32, D = 24,
  E = 16. You add your subjects together.[^s5]
- **From September 2026, level 3 apprenticeships in England, Wales and Northern
  Ireland and SCQF level 6 apprenticeships in Scotland carry UCAS tariff points
  themselves**, banded by expected duration: 12 months = 48 points, 18 months =
  64, 24 months = 96, up to a maximum of 112 points.[^s11][^s15]
- The UCAS tariff calculator is indicative only; **each provider decides which
  qualifications it accepts and how many points it counts**.[^s6]

## Who actually sets apprenticeship entry requirements?

**The employer does.** This is the single most important thing to understand,
and it is the opposite of how university admissions works.

For a university course, the provider publishes a requirement and it is broadly
consistent for everyone applying to that course. For an apprenticeship, the
employer is hiring an employee. The Office for Students describes degree
apprentices as employees with the same rights as other employees.[^s20] An
employer setting a hiring bar can set it wherever it likes.

That produces three practical realities:

1. **The same apprenticeship standard has different bars at different
   employers.** Two companies both recruiting to the Level 6 Digital and
   Technology Solutions Professional standard may ask for very different
   grades.
2. **Requirements are sometimes expressed in grades and sometimes in UCAS
   points.** "BBB" and "120 UCAS points" are not identical: the points version
   is usually more flexible about how you get there.
3. **A published requirement can be a floor, not a threshold.** Meeting it gets
   your application read. On a heavily oversubscribed scheme, being at the
   floor is not the same as being competitive.

## What is the GCSE English and maths rule, exactly?

There are **two different things** both called "the English and maths
requirement", and confusing them is the most common mistake in this area.

### 1. The employer's entry requirement (set by the employer)

Most employers ask for GCSE English and maths at a stated grade as part of
their own hiring criteria. This is not a government rule. It is the employer's
choice, it varies, and it is the one that decides whether your application gets
anywhere.

### 2. The statutory completion requirement (set by DfE, England)

This is a condition of *finishing* the apprenticeship, applied at gateway to
end-point assessment — not a condition of being hired.

- **Aged 16 to 18 at the start of training:** you must achieve English and
  maths qualifications to complete the apprenticeship if you do not already
  hold a suitable equivalent. Funding is available up to an approved level 2
  qualification in those subjects.[^s1] For 16 to 18 apprentices this must be
  met in order to pass gateway.[^s1]
- **Aged 19 and over at the start of training:** the mandatory requirement to
  hold or achieve English and maths qualifications to pass the apprenticeship
  was **removed from 11 February 2025**. Adult apprentices instead develop and
  are assessed on occupationally relevant English and maths skills as part of
  the apprenticeship.[^s3]
- Employers and apprentices affected by the change had to agree by 1 April 2025
  whether the apprentice would continue studying English and maths, with the
  decision recorded in the training plan.[^s3]

**What counts as "a suitable equivalent"?** GOV.UK publishes lists of
acceptable English and maths qualifications for apprenticeships, including
qualifications from other UK frameworks — for example, English or maths at
SCQF level 4 or above is treated as acceptable for a level 2
apprenticeship.[^s1][^s2]

**GTOD take:** if you are 16 to 18 and do not have GCSE English or maths at
grade 4, this is a real hurdle but not a wall. You can normally do the level 2
functional skills qualification alongside the apprenticeship. What it does mean
is that competitive degree apprenticeship employers, who screen on their own
entry criteria long before any of this applies, will usually screen you out.
Resitting before you apply is the stronger move.

> [!GAP]
> These rules are for **England**. We did not verify the equivalent English and
> maths rules for Modern and Graduate Apprenticeships in Scotland, Wales or
> Northern Ireland in this session. Anyone advising outside England should
> check their nation's apprenticeship authority directly.

## How is the UCAS tariff calculated?

The UCAS tariff allocates points to post-16 qualifications using a model based
on the **size** of the qualification and its **grade**.[^s7] You add the points
from each qualification together.

**A levels:**[^s5]

| Grade | Points |
| --- | --- |
| A* | 56 |
| A | 48 |
| B | 40 |
| C | 32 |
| D | 24 |
| E | 16 |

Three A levels at A*, A*, B is 56 + 56 + 40 = **152 points**.[^s5]

**Two things the tariff does not do:**

- It does not tell you whether a provider will accept a qualification. UCAS is
  explicit that the calculator is indicative and each provider decides what it
  accepts and counts.[^s6]
- It does not replace subject requirements. An employer asking for A level maths
  will not accept 120 points made up of other subjects.

## What are BTECs, T Levels and Scottish Highers worth?

### BTEC

Pearson publishes the tariff values for BTEC qualifications.[^s8] The
**Extended Diploma** is the three-A-level-sized BTEC and awards three grades:

| Extended Diploma grade | Points |
| --- | --- |
| D*D*D* | 168 |
| DDD | 144 |
| DDM | 128 |
| DMM | 112 |

The single-A-level-sized **Extended Certificate** awards one grade: D* = 56,
D = 48, M = 32, P = 16 — the same values as A level A*, A, C and E
respectively.[^s8]

### T Levels

A full T Level is sized as three A levels and carries a single overall grade.
Reported tariff values are Distinction* = 168, Distinction = 144, Merit = 120,
Pass = 96.

> [!GAP]
> The T Level tariff values above come from secondary summaries of the UCAS
> tariff tables, not from a UCAS page we confirmed in this session. In
> particular we could not verify whether the Pass grade is split into more than
> one band. Check the official UCAS tariff tables for the relevant entry year
> before quoting T Level points.

### Scottish Highers and Advanced Highers

UCAS publishes tariff values for Scottish qualifications.[^s9][^s10] Reported
values are:

| Qualification | A | B | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Higher | 33 | 27 | 21 | 15 |
| Advanced Higher | 56 | 48 | 40 | 32 |

There is an important **replacement rule**: if you take an Advanced Higher in a
subject you already passed at Higher, only the Advanced Higher counts for that
subject — you do not add both.

> [!GAP]
> The specific Higher and Advanced Higher point values and the replacement rule
> above come from secondary summaries of the UCAS tariff, not from the UCAS
> pages themselves in this session. The UCAS Scottish Advanced Highers tariff
> page[^s9] and the UCAS Scottish qualifications document[^s10] would settle
> them.

### The rough equivalence to remember

An Extended Diploma at DDD, three A levels at AAA, and a full T Level at
Distinction all land on **144 points**. That is the tariff working as designed:
same size, same grade band, same points.[^s5][^s8]

**GTOD take:** the tariff makes vocational and academic routes *arithmetically*
equal, but it does not make them equal in the eyes of every employer.
Engineering and finance schemes that name specific A level subjects are
effectively filtering by subject, not by points, and a BTEC in a different
field will not clear that filter however many points it carries. Read what the
employer asks for, not what the points table says.

## Do apprenticeships themselves carry UCAS points?

Yes, from September 2026 — this is new and worth knowing.

- **Level 3 apprenticeships in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and SCQF
  level 6 apprenticeships in Scotland, are being included in the UCAS
  tariff.**[^s11][^s12]
- Points are banded by the expected time in study and work, with a competency
  requirement (achieving a pass): 12 months = 48 points, 18 months = 64, 24
  months = 96, and up to a maximum of 112 points for a level 3
  apprenticeship.[^s11][^s15]
- Apprentices must meet the conditions of their offer, including passing
  end-point assessment, by the stated date in the relevant academic year.[^s15]
- UCAS has said it has no current plans to extend tariff points beyond level
  3.[^s15]

**Why this matters:** it means completing a level 3 apprenticeship is now a
recognised route into higher education with a tariff value attached, rather
than something admissions tutors have to translate by hand. It does **not**
mean a level 3 apprenticeship is equivalent to three A levels — 112 points is
below the 144 that AAA or DDD reaches.[^s5][^s8][^s11]

## How do employers use predicted grades?

**Almost all school-leaver apprenticeship offers are conditional on your final
results, and predicted grades are what get you through the door in the
meantime.**

- If you are still studying, you enter predicted grades on the application.
- The offer is then conditional. BAE Systems, for example, tells apprenticeship
  applicants that an offer is conditional on achieving certain grades or
  qualifications.[^s19]
- Predicted grades are used as a screening filter, not as the decision.
  Employers run online assessments, video interviews and assessment centres
  precisely because grades alone do not predict who they want.[^s19]

**GTOD take:** the practical consequence is that your predicted grades decide
whether a human ever reads your application, and everything after that decides
whether you get the job. If your predictions are borderline for a target
employer, the highest-value thing you can do in autumn term is get them raised
by your teachers — that is a bigger lever than another draft of your cover
letter.

## What do I do if I narrowly miss my grades?

**Do not assume the offer is gone.** An apprenticeship offer sits with an
employer who has discretion, and for a degree apprenticeship there is also a
university partner with its own process. Ulster University's apprenticeships
results day guidance describes several routes that exist when results fall
short:[^s16]

- Depending on how many funded places are available, the provider may be able
  to offer a place with reduced entry requirements, in line with any grade
  reductions applied to the equivalent full-time course.[^s16]
- If there is capacity and the job role is suitable, you may be offered a place
  on a **different** degree apprenticeship with different entry requirements —
  but the employer has to approve that before the place is confirmed.[^s16]
- If you have secured the employer but the university cannot make you an offer
  on your level 3 results, the guidance is to discuss next steps with the
  employer, including other higher level apprenticeship pathways at levels 4
  and 5.[^s16]

**What to actually do, in order:**

1. **Contact the employer the same day.** Not the university, not your school.
   The employer holds the job.
2. **Ask a specific question**, not an open one: "I achieved X, my offer
   required Y. Are you able to confirm my place, and if not, is there an
   alternative level 4 or 5 route with you?"
3. **Ask about a remark** if you are close on a single unit and the deadline is
   still open.
4. **Search live vacancies in parallel** — apprenticeship vacancies are
   advertised year-round, unlike university places.[^s18][^s20]

**GTOD take:** the asymmetry here works in your favour and almost nobody uses
it. A university has a fixed number of places and an algorithm. An employer has
a hiring manager who has already met you at an assessment centre, has decided
they want you, and does not want to run the process again. Ask. The worst case
is a no you already had.

> [!GAP]
> We could not verify any published, named employer policy stating what happens
