## In one line

The online assessment stage of a UK apprenticeship application is almost always built by one of about eight test publishers, each of which puts free official practice on its own website, and every one of them is legally required to adjust the test if you are disabled.[^s1][^s28]

## Key facts

- SHL, Talogy, Cappfinity, Arctic Shores, Amberjack, Test Partnership, Aon and Korn Ferry are the publishers whose names you will most often see in a UK early-careers assessment invitation; the employer's logo is on the screen but the science underneath is the publisher's.[^s1][^s8][^s11][^s14][^s17][^s19][^s22][^s25]
- SHL, Talogy, Cappfinity, Test Partnership, Aon, Korn Ferry and the Civil Service all publish their own free practice material. Practising on the publisher's own material is free and is the closest match to the real thing.[^s1][^s20][^s22][^s25][^s30]
- SHL's Verify Interactive G+ is a general cognitive ability test with 30 questions, ten each of deductive, inductive and numerical reasoning, taking 36 minutes plus about 10 minutes of instructions and practice.[^s3]
- SHL's Verify Interactive tests are computer adaptive: the difficulty of the next question depends on whether you got the last one right.[^s3][^s4]
- Arctic Shores' task-based assessment has no time limit and most people take roughly 30 to 50 minutes; it is a behavioural measure, not a right-or-wrong test.[^s13][^s14]
- Civil Service verbal and numerical tests are not timed, and most candidates take between 15 and 45 minutes for each.[^s30]
- Under the Equality Act 2010 an employer must make reasonable adjustments for a disabled applicant, and that duty applies at the online-test stage, not just at interview.[^s32][^s33]
- In Civil Service recruitment, extra time on a timed test is offered in bands of 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% depending on assessed need.[^s28]

## Why do apprenticeship employers use online assessments at all?

Apprenticeship employers use online assessments because early-careers applicant volumes are far larger than the number of people any human sifter could read. The assessments are designed as a first filter: cheap per candidate, identical for everyone, and scored automatically.

Publishers argue this also helps candidates with no work history. Amberjack, which specialises in UK early careers, says early-careers assessment should focus on future potential, transferable skills and realistic job scenarios rather than prior experience, and recommends blending cognitive ability, situational judgement and behavioural measures.[^s17]

**GTOD take:** this is genuinely good news if you are 17 with no internships. An aptitude test does not know which school you went to. It is the one stage of the process where preparation converts almost directly into score.

## What are the main formats of online assessment, and what does each measure?

| Format | What it actually measures | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical reasoning | Reading tables and charts and drawing correct conclusions from them | Data on screen, multiple-choice questions, timed[^s2][^s30] |
| Verbal reasoning | Whether a written statement follows from a passage | Passage plus a statement to mark true / false / cannot say[^s30] |
| Inductive or abstract reasoning | Spotting the underlying rule in a sequence of shapes and predicting what comes next | Pattern series, no words or numbers[^s2] |
| Deductive or logical reasoning | Working out what must be true given a set of stated rules | Rule puzzles, seating and scheduling problems[^s3][^s23] |
| Checking | Accuracy and speed at comparing information | Side-by-side data to compare against the clock[^s26] |
| Situational judgement (SJT) | Judgement and decision-making in realistic workplace scenarios | Scenario plus four or five possible responses to rank or choose[^s5][^s10][^s31] |
| Personality or behavioural questionnaire | Stable work preferences and motivations, not ability | Statements to agree with or rank; usually untimed[^s9][^s24] |
| Strengths assessment | What you are both good at and energised by | Task-based or scenario-based, often untimed[^s11] |
| Game-based or task-based | Cognitive ability and behaviour inferred from how you play, not what you claim | Mini-tasks, often untimed, no obvious right answer[^s14][^s21] |

## What is SHL and what do SHL tests involve?

SHL is the most widely used publisher in UK graduate and apprenticeship recruitment. Its main early-careers products are the Verify range of ability tests and its situational judgement tests.[^s1][^s5]

SHL Verify Interactive G+ measures general cognitive ability and produces separate scores for deductive, inductive and numerical reasoning. SHL's factsheet gives 30 questions, ten per ability, a 36-minute test time, and around 10 minutes on top for instructions and a practice section. It is computer adaptive, so question difficulty moves with your performance.[^s3][^s4]

The "Interactive" versions replace multiple-choice clicking with drag-and-drop interactions and can be taken on a phone or tablet as well as a laptop.[^s1]

### Where does SHL offer free practice?

SHL runs a candidate site, SHL Direct, with free practice tests and example questions covering numerical, inductive, verbal, checking and situational judgement formats. SHL states that it does not sell practice tests to candidates and that not every practice test produces a feedback report.[^s1][^s2]

- Practice tests: https://www.shl.com/shldirect/en/practice-tests/ [^s1]
- Example questions by test type: https://www.shl.com/shldirect/en/example-questions/ [^s2]

## What is Talogy (formerly Cubiks) and what do its tests involve?

Talogy is the publisher formed after Cubiks was absorbed into the group, and its early-careers products are the Logiks ability tests, the P.A.P.I. personality questionnaire and the Dilemmas situational judgement range.[^s8][^s9][^s10]

Logiks gives a general cognitive ability score alongside verbal, numerical and abstract sub-measures. Talogy says the Logiks item bank is large enough that assessments differ between sittings, which is a deliberate anti-cheating design.[^s8]

P.A.P.I. is a personality questionnaire rather than an ability test. Talogy describes it as covering seven personality factors split into 26 need and role scales.[^s9]

Dilemmas is Talogy's situational judgement format, used to test suitability against the behaviours a specific role needs.[^s10]

> [!GAP]
> Talogy's own site did not surface a candidate-facing free practice page during this research. Commercial prep sites publish Logiks timings, but those are not Talogy's own figures and are not cited here. If a Talogy test is in your process, ask the employer's recruitment contact for the official practice link rather than relying on a prep vendor's numbers.

## What is Cappfinity and what does a Cappfinity assessment involve?

Cappfinity, formerly Capp, is the publisher most associated with strengths-based assessment in UK early careers. Its stated approach is to measure both capability and motivation, meaning what you can do and what energises you, rather than aptitude alone.[^s11]

Cappfinity assessments are typically built as branded, scenario-driven experiences in which you work through a realistic piece of work for that employer, with numerical, verbal and judgement elements embedded inside the scenario rather than presented as separate tests. Cappfinity also publishes a separate range of cognitive tests.[^s11][^s12]

Cappfinity's own materials describe situational responses as having no single right or wrong answer, but an optimal answer determined by the context of the scenario.[^s11]

**GTOD take:** the practical consequence of a strengths design is that trying to reverse-engineer the "corporate" answer tends to score worse than answering as yourself, because strengths scoring looks for consistency across many items. You cannot hold a fake persona steady for 40 minutes.

## What is Arctic Shores and how does its assessment work?

Arctic Shores publishes a task-based assessment: instead of asking you to describe your behaviour, it puts you through a series of short interactive tasks and infers behaviour from what you actually do.[^s14]

- There is no time limit and most candidates take roughly 30 to 50 minutes.[^s13]
- The tasks are drawn from cognitive-neuroscience style measures, and each task measures something different; together they build a behavioural profile.[^s13]
- Arctic Shores states there is no right or wrong way to respond and that candidates do not need to practise, because the design depends on instinctive responses.[^s13]

**GTOD take:** "no practice needed" is the publisher's position and it is broadly honest about the design, but it is still worth doing the practice or demo task the employer links to, purely so the interface is not a surprise. Familiarity is not the same as gaming the test.

## What is Amberjack and what does an Amberjack process look like?

Amberjack is a UK early-careers specialist that runs both assessment and the wider recruitment process for large apprenticeship and graduate schemes. Its recommended early-careers design blends cognitive ability testing, situational judgement and behavioural assessment, often followed by an integrated video interview in the same sitting.[^s17]

Amberjack publishes guidance on assessing neurodiverse candidates, including advice that employers should drop the word "reasonable" when talking to candidates about adjustments so the conversation is about what can actually be done, and that test instructions should be written in short sentences and offered in more than one format.[^s18]

## What is Test Partnership and what does it publish?

Test Partnership is a UK publisher whose early-careers range includes the Concepts family of ability tests and the MindmetriQ gamified assessments.[^s19][^s21]

- Concepts Critical Thinking measures the ability to interpret, apply, analyse and evaluate complex written information.[^s19]
- Concepts Data Analysis measures the ability to work with complex quantitative data.[^s19]
- MindmetriQ is Test Partnership's game-style measure of general cognitive ability, marketed as shorter and more resistant to AI-assisted cheating than a conventional text test.[^s21]

Test Partnership publishes short candidate practice tests so applicants can get used to the style of its ability tests before the real thing: https://www.testpartnership.com/preparation-frs.html [^s20]

## What is Aon's assessment suite and where is its free practice?

Aon's assessment business, which absorbed cut-e, supplies both ability tests and the ADEPT-15 personality questionnaire.[^s22][^s24]

- ADEPT-15 is Aon's personality assessment, built on 15 behavioural dimensions, and it produces a picture of the kind of work you fit rather than an ability score.[^s24]
- Aon's ability tasks are named as challenges, for example gapChallenge and switchChallenge for deductive reasoning and gridChallenge.[^s23]
- Aon's own candidate guidance says you get instructions plus a practice task you may repeat as many times as you like before the real assessment starts, and that the real assessment is timed with an open-ended number of items, so working quickly and accurately matters more than finishing.[^s22]

Aon publishes downloadable practice-task PDFs for individual challenges, and a candidate preparation page: https://www.aon.com/en/capabilities/talent-and-rewards/prepare-for-your-online-assessment [^s22][^s23]

**GTOD take:** the "indefinite number of questions" design is the single most misunderstood format in early careers. You are not supposed to finish. Candidates who panic at the unanswered items score worse than candidates who keep a steady accurate rhythm.

## What is Korn Ferry / Talent Q and how do its tests work?

Korn Ferry owns the Talent Q assessment range, which appears in UK graduate and apprenticeship processes as Aspects, Elements and Dimensions.[^s25][^s26][^s27]

Talent Q Aspects Ability covers verbal reasoning, numerical reasoning, logical reasoning and checking, and all three Aspects Ability tests are adaptive. Because adaptive testing takes you quickly to questions at your own level, Korn Ferry states the process can take around half the time of a conventional fixed test.[^s26]

Korn Ferry publishes a candidate guide to psychometric assessments with practice assessments across verbal, numerical, logical and checking: https://www.kornferry.com/careers/candidates/candidate-guide-assessments [^s25]

## What are game-based assessments and do they actually work?

Game-based assessment means a test delivered as interactive tasks rather than questions, where scores come from how you behave in the task. Test Partnership's MindmetriQ and Arctic Shores' task-based assessment are the two you are most likely to meet in UK early careers.[^s14][^s21]

The published evidence is reasonably encouraging but not overwhelming. A peer-reviewed study of game-based cognitive ability assessment in recruitment reported convergent validity with conventional cognitive tests of around r = 0.5 and test-retest reliability of around r = 0.68 in a sample of 3,107 job applicants, found no significant gender difference in test-taking anxiety, and reported a Net Promoter Score of 58 from 4,778 applicants on candidate experience.[^s34]

**GTOD take:** a correlation of 0.5 with a conventional ability test means a game-based assessment is measuring something related but not identical. Treat a game-based result as a real assessment with real consequences, not as a bit of fun the employer bolted on.

## What does the Civil Service use, and why does it matter to apprentices?

The Civil Service is worth studying even if you are not applying to it, because it publishes more about its own tests than any private employer does. Civil Service apprenticeship and Fast Track applications can include verbal, numerical and judgement tests, plus strengths and styles assessments depending on the role.[^s29]

- The verbal test gives you a passage and a statement and asks you to decide whether it is true, false, or cannot be determined from the passage.[^s30]
- The numerical test gives you graphs or tables and asks which of several answers is correct.[^s30]
- The Civil Service Judgement Test is a situational judgement test scored against the published Civil Service Behaviours.[^s31]
- Civil Service verbal and numerical tests are untimed, and most people take 15 to 45 minutes on each.[^s30]
- The Civil Service publishes free official practice tests and a downloadable practice exercise of numerical and verbal questions.[^s30][^s36]

## How do I request a reasonable adjustment for an online assessment?

Under the Equality Act 2010 an employer has a legal duty to make reasonable adjustments so that a disabled applicant is not put at a substantial disadvantage, and that duty covers assessments and tests during recruitment, not only the job itself.[^s32][^s33] Failing to make an adjustment when the duty applies is treated as discrimination.[^s33]

Practical steps:

1. **Declare early, at application, not on the day.** SHL's own guidance is blunt about this: raising it in advance lets the organisation put an adjustment in place, and waiting until assessment day limits what is possible.[^s6][^s7]
2. **Ask the employer's named recruitment contact, not the test publisher first.** The employer commissions the test and controls the settings. SHL directs candidates to the recruiter who invited them, with its own candidate support form as a backup.[^s6]
3. **Some assessments let you self-declare in the platform.** Arctic Shores states that some employers ask you to contact them first while others let you declare an adjustment inside the assessment before you start, without contacting anyone.[^s16]
4. **Know what you can ask for.** Published examples include extra time on timed tests for dyslexia or motor impairments, screen-reader support for visual impairments, and changes to how the test is presented.[^s7]
5. **In the Civil Service, extra time comes in fixed bands.** A recruiter can add 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% to the time available depending on assessed need, and you request it in the application itself; if you miss it there you can go back to the named contact in the advert.[^s28][^s35]
6. **You must meet the Equality Act definition of disability** — a physical or mental impairment with a substantial, long-term negative effect on normal daily activities — for the duty to be engaged.[^s28]

Arctic Shores also states that its research led it to adjust the scoring key itself for some neurodivergent candidates, rather than only adjusting time.[^s15][^s16] Amberjack's published position is that employers should talk about "adjustments" rather than "reasonable adjustments" when speaking to candidates, to encourage people to ask.[^s18]

**GTOD take:** declaring an adjustment does not go to the hiring manager as "this person needed help". It goes to the recruitment operations team as a test setting. The number of apprentices who sit a timed test without the extra time they are entitled to, because they did not want to make a fuss, is the most avoidable loss in this whole process.

## How should I prepare for an online assessment stage?

- Find out which publisher the employer uses. The invitation email, the test URL and the login page usually name it.
- Do that publisher's own free practice first, because format familiarity is most of what practice buys you.[^s1][^s20][^s22][^s25][^s30]
- Sit the real test on a laptop with a wired or stable connection, not on a phone on mobile data, unless the publisher explicitly supports mobile.[^s1]
- For adaptive tests, do not rush: an early wrong answer moves you down the difficulty ladder.[^s3][^s26]
- For open-ended timed tests such as Aon's, accept that you will not finish and keep accuracy up.[^s22]
- For personality, strengths and behavioural assessments, answer consistently and honestly; the design assumes instinctive responses and consistency across many items.[^s13][^s11]
- Request any adjustment at the point of application.[^s28]
