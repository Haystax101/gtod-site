## In one line

Most big UK apprenticeship and graduate employers use online assessments from a handful of publishers (SHL, Aon, Arctic Shores, pymetrics, HireVue, Sova, Cappfinity, Saville, Korn Ferry, TestGorilla) before anyone interviews you, several of those publishers offer free official practice, and under the Equality Act 2010 you can ask the employer for adjustments such as extra time before you start.[^s1][^s7][^s31][^s38]

## Key facts

- SHL runs a free official practice site with over 20 practice assessments, including verbal, numerical, deductive and inductive reasoning, situational judgement, personality, video interview and job simulations, but it does not publish time limits or question counts for them.[^s1]
- Aon (formerly cut-e) publishes exact timings for its tests: for example, as at September 2026 its "scales numerical" reasoning test allows 12 minutes for 37 tasks and its "scales verbal" test allows 12 minutes for 49 tasks.[^s7]
- Arctic Shores' game-based (task-based) assessment measures behaviour and cognition rather than knowledge; its glossary says assessments take between 10 and 45 minutes, while its candidate page says 30 to 45 minutes for up to nine levels.[^s8][^s9]
- pymetrics (now owned by Harver) says its games take about 30 minutes in total, each game lasting 1 to 3 minutes, with no way to pause once a game starts.[^s13]
- HireVue says most of its on-demand video interviews have between five and eight questions and usually take under 20 to 30 minutes, but it advises setting aside 45 minutes.[^s15]
- Sova, Saville Assessment and Korn Ferry all offer free practice through their own candidate pages, but none of the three publishes typical timings on those pages.[^s18][^s21][^s22]
- TestGorilla gives 50% extra time to candidates who declare a disability or condition such as ADHD, dyslexia or autism, and 20% for non-native speakers of the test language, requested on the set-up screen before the test starts.[^s23]
- The Civil Service publishes free practice tests and a candidate guide to reasonable adjustments; for its timed tests (Customer Service Skills and Casework Skills only) recruiters can add 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% extra time.[^s25][^s26]
- Section 20 of the Equality Act 2010 sets out the duty to make reasonable adjustments, and section 39 applies that duty to employers, including in the arrangements they make for deciding who to offer a job to.[^s31][^s32]
- Disability Confident Level 2 (Employer) and Level 3 (Leader) organisations commit to offering an interview to applicants who declare a disability and meet the minimum criteria for the job.[^s35][^s36]
- Employer examples: PwC UK and KPMG UK both use SHL for their early careers online assessments; KPMG's cognitive test is timed at 36 minutes, and its skills assessment takes about 20 minutes untimed.[^s38][^s39]

## What are online assessments and why do apprenticeship employers use them?

Online assessments are tests you complete on your own computer or phone, usually right after you submit an application form and before any human interview. Big employers get thousands of applications, so they buy tests from specialist publishers to sift candidates and to check skills the application form cannot show.[^s2][^s38]

SHL, the most widely used publisher, groups its assessments into ability tests (can you do the tasks), personality questionnaires (how you prefer to work), situational judgement tests (what you would do in a work scenario), and assessment centre exercises.[^s2]

Employers describe them as a stage, not the whole decision. PwC UK, for example, follows its online assessment with a video interview, a virtual assessment centre and a business interview.[^s38] KPMG UK follows its online assessments with a video interview and an in-person "Launch Pad" half day.[^s39]

**GTOD take:** if you are applying to a large employer for a degree apprenticeship, assume you will hit at least one of these tests. Knowing the publisher tells you which free practice to do.

## Which companies make the tests, and what does each one measure?

Below is each major publisher, what its own pages say it measures, and the typical timing if the publisher states one. Where the publisher does not state a timing, the answer is "Not stated". Real test questions are not reproduced here.

**SHL.** Verbal reasoning (understanding written information), numerical reasoning (interpreting data), deductive reasoning (drawing logical conclusions), inductive reasoning (spotting patterns), plus situational judgement, personality, a video interview, coding and Excel simulations and a contact centre simulation. Timing: Not stated on the practice page.[^s1]

**Aon (formerly cut-e).** Aon's "scales" tests and game-style challenges. As at September 2026 Aon states: scales numerical 12 minutes for 37 tasks; scales verbal 12 minutes for 49 tasks; scales lt (language) 10 minutes; scales mtu (mechanical) 15 minutes for 24 tasks; scales verbal instruct 8 minutes for 24 tasks; scales eql (basic numeracy) 5 minutes; scales clx and scales ix (inductive logic) 6 and 5 minutes; scales cls 12 minutes; gridChallenge (working memory) 9 minutes; motionChallenge (planning) 6 minutes; switchChallenge and gapChallenge (deductive logic) 6 minutes each; digitChallenge 6 minutes; scales e3+ (concentration) 2 minutes; chatAssess (a chat-style situational judgement test) 20 minutes; ADEPT-15 personality questionnaire around 30 minutes to rate 100 statements.[^s7]

Aon also says the introduction and practice section of each test is untimed, and the clock only starts on the real test; personality questionnaires are usually untimed.[^s7]

**Arctic Shores.** A task-based (game-based) assessment. Arctic Shores says the tasks measure behaviour and cognition, built around the five main personality dimensions plus cognitive ability, and that one task can measure many traits at once.[^s8] Its candidate page says it measures how you naturally tackle problems, respond to changing requirements and handle risk and uncertainty.[^s9] Timing: the glossary says 10 to 45 minutes; the candidate page says 30 to 45 minutes for up to nine levels, with possible extra aptitude sections.[^s8][^s9] The employer chooses which tasks you get.[^s10]

**pymetrics (Harver).** Game-based behavioural assessment. pymetrics says the games measure cognitive, social and emotional attributes and how they show up at work.[^s13] Harver's product page lists attributes including effort, risk tolerance, decision-making, attention, learning, fairness, generosity and emotional intelligence across "12+" games.[^s14] Timing: about 30 minutes in total with each game 1 to 3 minutes (pymetrics candidate page); about 25 minutes (Harver product page). These two Harver-owned pages disagree slightly.[^s13][^s14]

**HireVue.** On-demand (recorded) video interviews and assessments. HireVue says its assessments look at job-specific hard skills and competencies such as communication, conscientiousness, problem-solving, team orientation and initiative, and not at appearance, age, race, gender or grades.[^s15] Timing: most interviews have five to eight questions with a few minutes per answer, usually under 20 to 30 minutes, and HireVue suggests allowing 45 minutes.[^s15] You get a practice question before the real interview starts.[^s16]

**Sova.** A single online platform that can include personality, motivation and competency questionnaires, situational judgement, logical, numerical and verbal reasoning, checking and accuracy, learnability, skills tests, video interviews and virtual assessment centres.[^s18] Timing: Not stated on the candidate hub.[^s18]

**Cappfinity.** Strengths-based assessments. Cappfinity says its skills and strengths assessment measures 80 behavioural skills benchmarked against workplace performance.[^s19] It runs candidate preparation hubs (a general one and employer-branded versions, for example for EY).[^s20][^s42] Timing: Not stated by Cappfinity.[^s19]

**Saville Assessment.** Aptitude tests (Analysis, Comprehension, Technical and Swift Aptitude ranges) and the Wave personality questionnaires (Professional Styles, Focus Styles, Match 6.5, Wave Strengths), which measure motives, talents, skills potential and preferred culture.[^s21] Timing: Not stated on the candidate page.[^s21] Most Saville aptitude tests cannot be taken on a mobile phone, so use a laptop or desktop.[^s21]

**Korn Ferry (Talent Q).** Korn Ferry's candidate guide describes four ability test types: checking (comparing information between tables quickly and accurately), numerical, verbal and logical (patterns in sequences of symbols).[^s22] Timing: Not stated.[^s22]

**TestGorilla.** A skills-testing platform where an employer builds an assessment from up to five tests and up to 20 custom questions, which can include multiple choice, essays, file uploads, video answers, typing and coding.[^s24] Timing: the welcome screen shows the estimated total time for your specific assessment; TestGorilla times each test and each question individually.[^s24]

## What is the difference between numerical, verbal, logical, situational judgement, game-based and personality tests?

Numerical reasoning tests ask you to read charts or tables and work out answers from the data.[^s1][^s22] The Civil Service describes its numerical test as evaluating numerical information and spotting patterns and trends, and it allows a calculator.[^s29]

Verbal reasoning tests give you a short passage and ask what follows from it.[^s1] The Civil Service describes its verbal test as using written information to reason to a conclusion.[^s29]

Logical tests come in two flavours: inductive (find the pattern in a sequence of shapes) and deductive (draw a conclusion from given rules).[^s1][^s7]

Situational judgement tests show a work scenario and ask which response is best or worst; SHL says they are designed to simulate real job situations.[^s2] Aon's chatAssess does the same thing in a messaging-app style.[^s7]

Game-based assessments replace questions with interactive tasks. Arctic Shores says there is no right or wrong way to approach its tasks and asks you to do them the way that feels natural.[^s10] pymetrics says the same: no right or wrong answers, and no preparation needed.[^s13]

Personality questionnaires ask about how you prefer to work. SHL says they measure behavioural preferences, not abilities, so there is no pass mark in the usual sense.[^s2] Aon's ADEPT-15 asks you to rate 100 statements about your work style.[^s7]

Video interviews record you answering questions on camera with no interviewer present. KPMG UK says its recordings are scored by its own recruitment team, not by software.[^s39]

Work-sample or job simulation assessments put you in a realistic task. EY UK's "Job Simulation" includes timed video sections, and SHL offers coding, email-writing and Excel simulations.[^s41][^s1]

## Where can I practise online assessments for free using official sites?

Practise on the publisher's own site first. These are the official free options, each confirmed on the publisher's own page on the access date given in the sources:

- **SHL Direct practice tests**: over 20 free practice assessments covering reasoning, situational judgement, personality, video interview and simulations. SHL warns the practice versions do not exactly match the difficulty or format of the real test an employer sends you.[^s1]
- **Aon "Prepare for your online assessment"**: screencast videos explaining each test plus downloadable practice task sets.[^s7]
- **Saville Assessment candidate area**: free online practice aptitude tests under timed conditions.[^s21]
- **Korn Ferry candidate guide**: links to practice versions of its checking, numerical, verbal and logical tests, plus a downloadable PDF guide.[^s22]
- **Sova candidate preparation hub**: free practice questions, and a practice question at the start of each section of the real assessment.[^s18]
- **Cappfinity preparation hub**: a Cappfinity-powered hub; EY runs its own branded version for student applicants.[^s20][^s42]
- **HireVue**: a practice question inside the interview itself before the scored questions begin.[^s16]
- **Arctic Shores and pymetrics**: no separate practice site. Arctic Shores gives you a practice task during onboarding, and its candidate page mentions a trial app after you submit application questions.[^s10][^s9] pymetrics says no practice is needed.[^s13]
- **TestGorilla**: most tests start with unscored practice questions that the employer never sees.[^s24]

Government and public sources:

- **Civil Service Jobs**: the Civil Service blog (September 2024) says its guidance site carries instructions for each of its seven test types (Verbal, Numerical, Judgement, Management Judgement, Work Strengths, Customer Service Skills, Casework Skills) and a suite of practice tests.[^s25] Its candidate guides say practice questions also appear at the start of each real test.[^s29][^s30]
- **National Careers Service**: a "prepare and practise" page describing verbal, numerical, abstract, personality and situational judgement tests, with links to SHL practice tests, the British Psychological Society and the Psychometric Test Project.[^s37]

**GTOD take:** paid prep sites sell "SHL-style" or "Aon-style" tests. The publisher's own practice is free, closer to the real thing, and enough for most people. Do the free official practice before paying for anything.

## How long do online assessments take?

Only trust a timing if the publisher or employer states it. Here is what each publishes, as read on the access date in the sources.

Publisher timings: Aon gives exact limits per test, for example 12 minutes for its numerical test and 12 minutes for its verbal test.[^s7] pymetrics says about 30 minutes for the full game set.[^s13] HireVue says usually under 20 to 30 minutes for a five-to-eight-question interview.[^s15] Arctic Shores says between 10 and 45 minutes on one page and 30 to 45 minutes on another.[^s8][^s9] SHL, Sova, Saville, Cappfinity and Korn Ferry: Not stated.[^s1][^s18][^s21][^s19][^s22]

Employer timings: KPMG UK's apprentice process has a Skills Assessment of about 20 minutes (untimed) and a Cognitive Assessment of 36 minutes (timed, numerical and logical reasoning), then a video interview of six questions taking about 40 minutes including preparation time.[^s39] EY UK says its "EY One" assessment averages 60 minutes, its Numerical Reasoning Test about 20 minutes (time-recorded), and its Job Simulation has timed video sections.[^s41] PwC UK says its behavioural assessment is untimed and its cognitive assessment (numerical, inductive and deductive reasoning) is timed, without giving minutes.[^s38]

Civil Service timings: the Verbal and Numerical tests are untimed and adaptive; the guide says allow up to an hour for each but you should not need that long.[^s29] The Judgement Test is untimed and takes around an hour.[^s30] The Fast Stream's two online tests (Work-based Scenarios with 12 situations, and a Case Study with 24 situations) are untimed, take roughly 90 minutes together, and must be finished within 7 days of becoming available.[^s27]

Separate from the test clock, employers set a deadline for completing the assessment. EY UK says you must complete assessments within the timeframe given or you may be withdrawn.[^s41]

## How do employers like PwC, KPMG and EY use online assessments?

**PwC UK (early careers, including apprenticeships).** Stage 1 is the online application. Stage 2 is an online assessment on SHL's platform with three parts: a non-assessed "Bringing our values to life" activity, an untimed behavioural assessment, and a timed cognitive assessment covering numerical, inductive and deductive reasoning. Stage 3 is an on-demand video interview, also on SHL, where you record answers for assessors to review later. Then a virtual assessment centre and a business interview.[^s38] PwC only allows one application per year and bans using AI tools or third-party help in its assessments unless explicitly authorised.[^s38]

**KPMG UK (apprentice).** Stage 1: a non-assessed job preview quiz and an application form, with contextual recruitment. Stage 2: online assessments via SHL: a Skills Assessment (rank responses to work scenarios, about 20 minutes, untimed) and a Cognitive Assessment (numerical and logical reasoning, 36 minutes, timed); practice tests are offered beforehand and you get a personal feedback report. Stage 3: a video interview with six pre-recorded questions from an AI avatar, scored by KPMG's own recruiters. Stage 4: "Launch Pad", a half-day in-person assessment.[^s39]

**EY UK (apprenticeships).** Stage 1: application form. Stage 2: online assessments built on the EY Strengths framework, using rank-order questions, written and recorded video answers, and a time-recorded numerical reasoning test, with practice tests included.[^s40] Stage 3: an EY Experience Day (in person for apprenticeships). Stage 4: a final interview with a senior team member.[^s40] EY's practice hub is powered by Cappfinity.[^s42]

**Civil Service (Fast Stream and other roles).** Civil Service Jobs uses seven online test types, chosen to fit the role.[^s25] The Fast Stream uses two untimed tests, Work-based Scenarios and a Case Study, and gives every candidate feedback whatever the result.[^s27]

**GTOD take:** the same SHL platform sits behind PwC and KPMG, so the SHL Direct practice site is the best single free resource if you are applying to the big accountancy and consulting firms.

## Can I get extra time or a different format on an online test if I have a disability, dyslexia, ADHD or autism?

Yes. The Equality Act 2010 puts a duty on employers to make reasonable adjustments for disabled people, and that duty covers the arrangements an employer makes for deciding who to offer a job to, which includes online tests.[^s31][^s32] GOV.UK lists changing the recruitment process so a candidate can be considered as an example of a reasonable adjustment, and says the rules cover apprentices and trainees.[^s33]

The Act's definition of disability is a physical or mental impairment with a substantial, long-term adverse effect on normal daily activities.[^s26][^s6] That includes conditions such as dyslexia, ADHD and autism when they meet that test; TestGorilla names those three as examples for its 50% extra time.[^s23]

Section 20 of the Act sets three requirements: change a practice that puts a disabled person at a substantial disadvantage, deal with physical barriers, and provide auxiliary aids, including providing information in an accessible format. The employer cannot charge you for the adjustment.[^s31]

SHL's UK guidance states plainly that failing to make reasonable adjustments is unlawful discrimination, and that adjustments are there to remove barriers, not to lower the bar.[^s5] SHL's FAQ adds that the Act can still require an adjustment even if you declare late, as long as the employer could reasonably have known about your disability.[^s6]

What adjustments typically exist: SHL lists extra time on timed tests, screen reader support, changes to presentation such as font size and contrast, large print on screen or paper, and text scripts for voice or video sections.[^s3][^s4] SHL's FAQ also mentions audio versions, a reader, and British Sign Language interpreters through a third party.[^s6] Aon mentions screen magnifiers for visual impairment and extended time for dyslexia.[^s7] Sova mentions extra time, a scribe or a screen reader.[^s18] HireVue offers extended interview time, screen reader compatibility and previews of interview questions.[^s17][^s15]

## How do I actually request a reasonable adjustment before an online assessment?

The single most consistent instruction across every publisher is: contact the employer, before you start the test.

- **SHL**: tell the recruiting organisation early; it makes the final decision. You can also fill in a form on SHL's Candidate Help site to discuss options with SHL's support team before you disclose anything to the employer.[^s3][^s4]
- **Aon**: contact the company or the test provider before the assessment.[^s7]
- **Arctic Shores**: your invitation email tells you which of two routes applies. Either you email the employer's support address before starting, or you tick the adjustments you need from a list on screen before the assessment begins. Once you start without adjustments they cannot be added afterwards.[^s11][^s12]
- **pymetrics**: built-in settings for ADHD, dyslexia and colour blindness that are not shared with the employer; for anything else, contact the employer or pymetrics support.[^s13]
- **HireVue**: ask the recruiter for extra time or a question preview.[^s15]
- **Sova**: contact the organisation you are applying to.[^s18]
- **TestGorilla**: declare on the environment set-up page before the test; 20% extra time for non-native speakers (the employer is told) and 50% for a disability or condition (the employer is not told), or 70% for both.[^s23]
- **Civil Service Jobs**: the application asks whether you need test adjustments; give the reason, what would help, and any adjustments you have had before, and a recruiter will contact you. Evidence such as a diagnostic report may be requested but is not compulsory. Options include display changes, paper versions of the verbal and numerical tests in a supervised session, BSL and subtitles for videos, and 25% to 100% extra time on the timed Customer Service and Casework tests.[^s26][^s28]

Employer examples: PwC UK says to discuss adjustments while completing the application.[^s38] KPMG UK says declare on the application form, or email or phone its student recruitment team.[^s39] EY UK says use the adjustments section of the application form and contact its Early Careers team before doing any assessment, because it cannot arrange a re-sit afterwards.[^s41]

The National Careers Service gives the same advice: contact the employer as soon as you are sent a test, and ask for things like screen-reading software or extra time.[^s37]

**GTOD take:** if you had extra time or a reader at GCSE or A level, say so when you request an adjustment. Publishers and the Civil Service all ask what has helped you before, and that is the quickest way to get a similar arrangement.

## What is the Disability Confident scheme and does it help with assessments?

Disability Confident is a UK government scheme that encourages employers to improve how they recruit, keep and develop disabled people.[^s34] It has three levels: Level 1 Committed, Level 2 Employer and Level 3 Leader.[^s35]

At Level 2, as at September 2026, employers commit to offering an interview to an applicant who declares a disability and meets the minimum criteria for the job, and to proactively offering and making reasonable adjustments as required under the Equality Act 2010, including flexibility in assessment methods.[^s36]

The Civil Service runs the scheme and says it will still support candidates who need adjustments even if they do not think they qualify for Disability Confident.[^s28]

**GTOD take:** the Disability Confident badge on a job advert is a signal that the employer has a process for adjustments, so asking should be straightforward. It does not exempt you from online tests; it means you should get an interview if you meet the minimum criteria and declare a disability.

## Can an employer tell I practised, or use AI to score me?

Publishers differ, so check the specific test. Arctic Shores says its assessment detects attempts to game it in real time and marks such candidates as unsuitable, and advises simply doing your best within the instructions.[^s8] TestGorilla makes you accept an honesty agreement that bans AI tools and retakes, takes periodic webcam snapshots, and requires full-screen mode.[^s24] PwC UK says use of AI tools or third-party help in its application is not permitted unless explicitly authorised, and serious cases may be reported to your school or college.[^s38]

On scoring: HireVue says the idea that a robot decides whether you get the job is inaccurate, and that its interviews are a first screening step followed by human interviews.[^s15] KPMG UK says its video interview answers are reviewed and scored by its recruitment team, not automatically.[^s39]
