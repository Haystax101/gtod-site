## In one line

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each run their own apprenticeship system with different names, levels, funders and vacancy sites, so if you live or work outside England you should search apprenticeships.scot, Wales's Find an Apprenticeship service, or nidirect rather than GOV.UK's England-only Find an apprenticeship service.[^s1][^s28][^s36]

## Key facts

- Apprenticeships.scot is Scotland's official apprenticeship site, run by Skills Development Scotland, and lists Foundation, Modern and Graduate Apprenticeship vacancies.[^s1]
- To do a Modern or Graduate Apprenticeship in Scotland you must live in Scotland as your main home for the whole apprenticeship, have the right to work in the UK, and work for a business based in Scotland.[^s7]
- Scottish levels use the SCQF: SCQF 5 maps to RQF Level 2 (GCSE 9 to 4), SCQF 6 to RQF Level 3 (A level), SCQF 7 to RQF Level 4 (HNC) and SCQF 12 to RQF Level 8 (doctorate).[^s12]
- Graduate Apprenticeships in Scotland run from SCQF Level 9 (ordinary degree) to SCQF Level 11 (master's), take 2 to 5 years, and there are 15 types.[^s2][^s6]
- Scottish Graduate Apprenticeship tuition is paid by SAAS direct to the university; apprentices must be at least 16 and ordinarily resident in Scotland, with an employer based in Scotland and a contract of at least 21 hours a week.[^s13]
- Foundation Apprenticeships in Scotland are done at school, mostly chosen at the end of S4 or S5, and a SCQF Level 6 one is equal to a Higher; they are unpaid and you are not an employee.[^s4][^s21]
- In Wales, Medr (the Commission for Tertiary Education and Research) has been responsible for funding apprenticeships since it became operational on 1 August 2024.[^s26][^s27]
- Wales has four apprenticeship levels: Foundation (Level 2), Apprenticeship (Level 3), Higher (Levels 4 to 5) and Degree (Level 6).[^s27][^s28]
- Welsh degree apprenticeships are fully funded by Medr but only in four subject areas as at September 2026: digital, engineering, advanced manufacturing and construction, and you normally need to be 18 or over.[^s27]
- To be funded in Wales you must work in Wales for at least 51% of your time; where you live does not decide it, so someone living in England but working mostly in Wales counts as Welsh-funded.[^s32]
- Welsh apprentices must be employed for at least 16 hours a week and sit Essential Skills Wales tests in Communication, Application of Number and (where the framework includes it) Digital Literacy within 61 days of starting, unless they hold a proxy such as a relevant GCSE.[^s32]
- Wales's official vacancy site is findanapprenticeship.service.gov.wales, run by the Welsh Government; Careers Wales also runs an apprenticeship search at careerswales.gov.wales/apprenticeship-search.[^s28][^s24]
- Northern Ireland's Higher Level Apprenticeships run from Level 4 to Level 7 (master's), most are at Level 5, last at least two years, and require employment with a Northern Ireland-based company for at least 21 hours a week including off-the-job training.[^s35]
- Northern Ireland apprenticeship vacancies are listed on nidirect's "Search for apprenticeship opportunities" service (JobApplyNI).[^s36]
- The apprentice minimum wage is £8.00 an hour from 1 April 2026 for apprentices under 19, or 19 and over in their first year; this is a UK-wide rate and applies in all four nations.[^s18][^s21]
- The apprenticeship levy is charged at 0.5% of pay bills over £3 million with a £15,000 allowance, and applies to employers across the UK including Scottish health boards and Northern Ireland trusts; Scotland's share is spent by the Scottish Government on skills, training and employment.[^s19][^s16]

## Why are apprenticeships different in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

Skills and education are devolved, so each nation designs and funds its own apprenticeship programme. Scotland's are administered by Skills Development Scotland (SDS) for the Scottish Government.[^s8] Wales's are funded by Medr, which took over from the Welsh Government (Levels 2 to 5) and from HEFCW (degree apprenticeships).[^s27] Northern Ireland's are run by the Department for the Economy.[^s37]

The practical result: the names of the apprenticeships, the level system, the age and residency rules, the English and maths requirements and the vacancy websites are all different from England. The wage floor and the levy tax are the two things that stay the same across the UK.[^s18][^s19]

**GTOD take:** treat everything in the GTOD corpus about "apprenticeship standards", "Find an apprenticeship", the DfE funding rules and Level 7 funding changes as England-only unless a document says otherwise. This document is the place to check before you apply outside England.

## What are Foundation, Modern and Graduate Apprenticeships in Scotland?

Scotland has three apprenticeship families, all administered by SDS.[^s8]

Foundation Apprenticeships (FAs) are done while you are still at school. Most people pick one at the end of S4 or S5 and it takes one or two school years.[^s4] There are 15 types, including Business Skills, Engineering, Software Development, Construction and Hospitality; 12 are at SCQF Level 6 (equal to a Higher) and 3 pilots are at SCQF Level 4 or 5 (equal to National 4 or 5).[^s4] FAs are unpaid and you are not an employee.[^s21] They cost the school nothing because the pupil is still at school.[^s5]

Modern Apprenticeships (MAs) are paid jobs with training, available from SCQF Level 4 or 5 up to SCQF Level 11, across more than 100 types.[^s3] They usually take between 1 and 4 years.[^s3]

Graduate Apprenticeships (GAs) are degree-level jobs where you are employed by a company and spend part of your week at university, taking 2 to 5 years.[^s2] There are 15 GA types, including Accounting, Construction and the Built Environment, Data Science, Civil Engineering, Business Management (Project Management) and Early Learning and Childcare.[^s2][^s6] The qualification is the same degree a full-time student would get.[^s2]

## What SCQF level is a Scottish apprenticeship, and what is that in English levels?

Scotland uses the Scottish Credit and Qualifications Framework (SCQF), which runs from 1 to 12, while England uses the RQF, which runs from Entry Level to Level 8. A Scottish level number always looks higher than its English equivalent.[^s12]

The SCQF Partnership's official comparison gives these matches:[^s12]

| SCQF level | Scottish qualification | English (RQF) level | English equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | National 5 | Level 2 | GCSE grades 9 to 4 (A* to C), NVQ 2 |
| 6 | Higher | Level 3 | A level, NVQ 3 |
| 7 | HNC, Advanced Higher | Level 4 | first year of degree, NVQ 4 |
| 12 | Doctorate | Level 8 | Doctorate |

So a Modern Apprenticeship at SCQF 5 is roughly an English Level 2 (intermediate) apprenticeship, and SCQF 6 is roughly an English Level 3 (advanced).[^s12] A Foundation Apprenticeship at SCQF 6 counts as a Higher.[^s4]

Graduate Apprenticeship levels are stated inconsistently by official sources. Apprenticeships.scot says GAs run from SCQF Level 9 (ordinary degree) to SCQF Level 11 (master's).[^s2][^s6] The SDS corporate site says SCQF 8 (HND) to SCQF 11, quoting 2019 figures.[^s9] UCAS says GAs are offered at SCQF 8 (HND or SVQ 4), 10 (honours degree) and 11 (master's).[^s14] **GTOD take:** trust apprenticeships.scot, which is the live SDS vacancy site; the SDS corporate page reads as older material. Check the level on the individual vacancy.

## Who can do a Modern or Graduate Apprenticeship in Scotland?

Age: you must be over the legal school-leaving age in Scotland, and you can apply before you leave school as long as you have left by the time you start. There is no maximum age for a Modern Apprenticeship, but SDS funding differs for people aged 25 and over.[^s7][^s3] For Graduate Apprenticeships the SFC rule is that you must be at least 16 when the course starts.[^s13]

Residency: the apprenticeships.scot FAQ says Scottish residency means living in Scotland as your main residence for the whole apprenticeship, you must have the right to work in the UK, and you must work for a business based in Scotland.[^s7] UCAS states the same: aged 16 or over, living in Scotland and not in full-time education.[^s15] Family-visa holders can apply if the visa allows work, and "no recourse to public funds" visas are not a bar because SDS funding is not classed as public funds, but the visa must last long enough.[^s7] Citizens Advice Scotland adds that overseas nationals with time restrictions on their stay cannot normally apply, while refugees and asylum seekers may be eligible.[^s21]

Graduate Apprenticeships have an extra SFC test: ordinarily resident in the UK, Channel Islands or Isle of Man for the three years before the relevant date, ordinarily resident in Scotland on that date and on the day the course starts, employed by an employer with a base in Scotland, spending the majority of working time in Scotland, on a contract of at least 21 hours a week.[^s13] Glasgow Caledonian University puts it simply: you must be living and working in Scotland in the same field as the GA you apply for.[^s20]

Entry requirements: each Modern Apprenticeship listing states any entry requirements for that framework, and work experience is considered alongside qualifications.[^s7] UCAS notes some GA roles require you to be 18 or over.[^s14]

> [!GAP]
> No SDS or Scottish Government source opened in this research states a compulsory English or maths (National 5 or equivalent) requirement for Modern Apprenticeships comparable to England's functional skills rule. The MA programme specification section on eligibility (4.2) could not be read in full from the PDF. Settle it by reading sections 4.2.2 and 4.2.5 of the SDS Modern Apprenticeship Programme Specification 26/27 and the individual framework documents on apprenticeships.scot.

## Who pays for a Scottish apprenticeship, and does the levy apply?

Modern Apprenticeships: SDS contributes to training and assessment costs, usually paying the learning provider directly; the amount depends on the apprentice's age, the qualification level and the sector, and providers may ask the employer for a top-up.[^s5] The SDS contribution table uses three age bands (16 to 19, 20 to 24, 25 and over) and, in the version opened on 3 September 2026, ranged from £300 to £10,300 per apprentice depending on framework and age.[^s11] The 2026/27 programme specification (effective 6 May 2026) prioritises 16 to 24 year olds across all sectors, funds 25 and over only in designated key sectors, and gives disabled and care-experienced apprentices aged 20 to 29 the highest (16 to 19) rate.[^s10]

Graduate Apprenticeships: funded by the Scottish Funding Council through university places, with SAAS paying the tuition fee direct to the university for eligible starters; the SFC expected at least 1,378 new GA opportunities in academic year 2025-26 with no upper cap.[^s13][^s5]

Overall budget: on 7 March 2025 the Scottish Government announced roughly £102.5 million for SDS in 2025-26, supporting 25,500 Modern Apprenticeship starts and 2,500 Foundation Apprenticeship starts, with a further 2,500 FAs and about 1,200 GAs funded through the SFC.[^s17] The 1,200 GA figure and the SFC's 1,378 target are different measures from different documents and should not be treated as contradictory.[^s17][^s13]

Levy: the apprenticeship levy is a UK-wide HMRC tax of 0.5% on annual pay bills over £3 million, with a £15,000 allowance, and applies to Scottish employers.[^s19] There is no Scottish digital levy account; the Scottish Government says Scotland's share of levy money supports skills, training and employment programmes.[^s16] Apprenticeships.scot lists the levy as a UK employment tax alongside other employer funding.[^s5]

Wages: Modern and Graduate Apprentices must be paid at least the National Minimum Wage for an apprentice, and SDS encourages employers to pay the Living Wage.[^s3][^s5]

## Which universities run Graduate Apprenticeships in Scotland?

Official pages opened for this document do not publish a single current list of GA universities. The SDS corporate site said that by the end of 2019, 14 Scottish universities and colleges offered GAs across 14 subjects including ICT/Digital, Cyber Security, Data Science, Civil Engineering, Engineering, Construction and Business.[^s9]

As one verified example, Glasgow Caledonian University offers GAs in AI and Data Science, Business Management, Business Management with HR Management, Civil Engineering, Construction and the Built Environment (Quantity Surveying), Cyber Security, Engineering (Design and Manufacture) and a Master of Accountancy, plus a postgraduate Cyber Security MSc, working with over 300 employers across Scotland.[^s20]

> [!GAP]
> The apprenticeships.scot "browse frameworks" and "universities" pages did not render a list when fetched, and the SAAS Graduate Apprenticeship page returned a 403. A current list of GA universities and the subjects each offers should be confirmed from apprenticeships.scot's vacancy search filtered by Graduate Apprenticeship.

## How do I find a Scottish apprenticeship vacancy?

Search vacancies on apprenticeships.scot; each listing explains how to apply.[^s1][^s3] For a Foundation Apprenticeship you check what your school offers and complete a short form, after which a careers adviser contacts you.[^s7] The SDS helpline is 0800 917 8000.[^s2] Scottish Apprenticeship Week runs from 2 to 6 March.[^s1]

## What are the apprenticeship levels in Wales?

Wales uses the same level numbers as England (Level 2 upwards) but its own names:[^s27][^s25]

| Welsh name | Level | Equivalent | 
|---|---|---|
| Foundation Apprenticeship | 2 | five good GCSEs / NVQ 2 |
| Apprenticeship | 3 | two A levels / NVQ 3 |
| Higher Apprenticeship | 4 to 5 | HNC, HND or foundation degree |
| Degree Apprenticeship | 6 | bachelor's degree |

Note that a Welsh "Foundation Apprenticeship" is a Level 2 job, nothing like Scotland's school-based Foundation Apprenticeship.[^s27][^s4] Careers Wales says Higher Apprenticeships can reach Levels 6 to 7 and Degree Apprenticeships can be Level 6 or 7, but Medr, the funder, lists degree apprenticeships at Level 6 only.[^s25][^s27] **GTOD take:** Medr's Level 6 figure is the one that determines what is funded; treat Level 7 apprenticeships in Wales as not generally available unless a specific listing says otherwise.

Apprenticeships in Wales normally take 1 to 4 years, with study usually one day a week.[^s27][^s23]

## Who can do an apprenticeship in Wales?

Age: apprenticeships are open to anyone over 16 who has legally left school; degree apprenticeships require you to be 18 or over, with no upper age limit.[^s27][^s32]

Where you work matters more than where you live. The Medr programme framework (V8, August 2025) says you must work in Wales for at least 51% of your time, and the deciding factor is the country where you work.[^s32] The Medr residency guide (August 2026) adds that you must be resident in and eligible to work in the UK and hold any required visa; it bases eligibility categories on the Education (Fees and Awards) (Wales) Regulations 2007 and does not itself set a "live in Wales" rule.[^s29] The older gov.wales page says "living in Wales" and not in full-time education, but it was last updated in July 2020.[^s23] **GTOD take:** follow the Medr 51% working-time rule; the gov.wales wording is out of date.

Employment: you must be employed (including self-employed) for at least 16 hours a week and paid at least the National Minimum Wage.[^s32] You cannot start if you are still of school age, in full-time education, in custody, or on a competing government-funded programme.[^s32]

English and maths: Wales uses Essential Skills Wales (ESW) rather than GCSE resits or functional skills. Providers must test Communication, Application of Number and, where the framework includes it, Digital Literacy within 61 days before or after your start date; if you hold valid proxies for the relevant ESW qualification you take a WEST screener instead.[^s32] Medr describes the requirement as application of number and communication, plus digital literacy if applicable to the sector.[^s27]

Off-the-job training: the framework says apprentices receive both off-the-job and on-the-job training in line with the Specification of Apprenticeship Standards for Wales (SASW), but it does not quote a minimum number of hours.[^s32]

Welsh language: your Individual Learning Plan must specify opportunities to maintain or develop your Welsh language skills, and Welsh-medium and bilingual apprenticeships are available.[^s32][^s27]

> [!GAP]
> The SASW minimum off-the-job training figure for Wales was not verified, as the SASW document itself was not opened. Settle it by reading the current Specification of Apprenticeship Standards for Wales on gov.wales.

## Which degree apprenticeships can you do in Wales?

Degree apprenticeships in Wales are limited to four priority areas as at September 2026: digital, engineering, advanced manufacturing and construction.[^s27] They last a minimum of three years and usually up to five. Medr fully funds the degree for apprentices who work in Wales, and the employer pays the wages. You normally need Level 3 qualifications or relevant industry experience.[^s27]

Medr's 2024/25 provision list named Bangor, Cardiff, Cardiff Metropolitan, the Open University, Swansea, the University of South Wales, University of Wales Trinity Saint David and Wrexham as providers, with Aberystwyth's programmes under development; typical programmes were BSc Applied Software Engineering, Applied Cyber Security, Applied Data Science and BEng engineering degrees.[^s30] Medr's June 2026 update added new pathways including BSc Civil Engineering and Digital User Experience at UWTSD and eight engineering degrees at UWTSD and the University of South Wales.[^s31]

## What is Medr and what is changing in Wales?

Medr, the Commission for Tertiary Education and Research, became operational on 1 August 2024 and funds and regulates further education, higher education, apprenticeships, adult community learning and school sixth forms in Wales.[^s26] It inherited the Welsh Government's arrangements for Levels 2 to 5 and HEFCW's for degree apprenticeships, and says future commissioning is under review.[^s27]

On 1 September 2026 Medr announced a redesigned apprenticeship programme in which providers apply for grants (stage 1 deadline 2 October 2026, stage 2 deadline 31 December 2026) with delivery starting in August 2027, covering Levels 2 to 6 in three strands: core apprenticeships (Levels 2 to 5), degree apprenticeships (Level 6, higher education institutions only) and supported apprenticeships.[^s33][^s34] The programme is described as all-age (16 and above) and promises more Welsh-language apprenticeships.[^s34][^s33]

For a 16 year old applying in the 2026/27 academic year, the current rules above still apply; the new programme affects who delivers training from August 2027.[^s33] Medr also pays a £2,000 per learner incentive (up to 10 per business) to employers of disabled apprentices at Levels 2 to 5 until 31 July 2027.[^s27]

The apprenticeship levy applies to Welsh employers with pay bills of £3 million or more at 0.5%; Medr states this on its own page, and there is no Welsh levy account to draw from.[^s27] Where a Welsh-based employer's apprentice works in England 50% or more of the time, the Medr framework says the UK Government voucher (English levy) arrangements apply instead, and on the border the office where business rates are paid decides which government funds.[^s32]

## How do I find an apprenticeship vacancy in Wales?

The Welsh Government's Find an Apprenticeship service at findanapprenticeship.service.gov.wales lists vacancies across sectors and lets you filter by job, postcode, sector, level (Foundation, Apprenticeship, Higher or Degree), vacancy ID and Disability Confident employer.[^s28] It is a separate site from GOV.UK's Find an apprenticeship for England.[^s28]

Careers Wales runs its own search and apply page at https://careerswales.gov.wales/apprenticeship-search, a list of larger employers offering apprenticeships, and a helpline on 0800 028 4844.[^s24] The gov.wales apprenticeships page also points to the Welsh Find an Apprenticeship service and to Apprenticeship Week Wales 2026.[^s22]

## What are ApprenticeshipsNI and Higher Level Apprenticeships in Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland's apprenticeships are run by the Department for the Economy, whose hub page links to Higher Level Apprenticeship bulletins, statistics for all apprenticeships in 2024/25, Apprenticeship Week and a page on degree apprenticeships in law.[^s37]

Higher Level Apprenticeships (HLAs) offer qualifications from Level 4 to Level 7 (master's), with most at Level 5 (foundation degree). The minimum duration is two years.[^s35] They are delivered by the six further education colleges (Belfast Metropolitan, Northern Regional, North West Regional, Southern Regional, South Eastern Regional and South West), Ulster University, Queen's University Belfast and the Open University, across more than 20 sectors including ICT, Engineering, Accounting, Business Services, Construction, Health Sciences, Hospitality and Childcare.[^s35] The nidirect page cites achieving professional qualifications without paying higher education tuition fees as a benefit.[^s35]

## Who can do a Higher Level Apprenticeship in Northern Ireland?

Age: you must be at least the minimum school leaving age in Northern Ireland; entry requirements vary by sector and may include qualifications or entry tests.[^s35]

Employment: you must be employed, or about to take up permanent paid employment as an apprentice, or be an existing employee moving to a new role, with a Northern Ireland-based company, for at least 21 hours a week including off-the-job training time.[^s35]

Residency and right to work: UK citizens and EU/EEA nationals need settled status or indefinite leave to remain; Republic of Ireland nationals are eligible under the Common Travel Area; other nationals must show a legal right to work through a visa, settled status, refugee status or equivalent. The employer carries the legal responsibility for checking the right to live and work in the UK.[^s35]

The nidirect HLA page shows a last-updated date of 20 August 2026.[^s35]

## Can I live in England and do an apprenticeship in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland?

Scotland: no, not while living in England. SDS requires Scottish residency, meaning Scotland is your main home for the whole apprenticeship, plus a Scotland-based employer.[^s7] Graduate Apprenticeships additionally require you to be ordinarily resident in Scotland on the day the course starts and to work mostly in Scotland.[^s13] If you move to Scotland for the job, you can meet the residency test; the FAQ frames the rule around where you live during the apprenticeship, not where you grew up.[^s7]

Wales: yes, if the job is in Wales. Funding follows the country where you work, and you qualify if you work in Wales for at least 51% of your time, even if your employer is registered in England.[^s32] The reverse also applies: if you are employed in Wales but work in England 50% or more of the time, the English (UK Government) system funds you.[^s32]

Northern Ireland: the Higher Level Apprenticeship rules require an NI-based employer and at least 21 hours a week, but do not state a residence test for the apprentice.[^s35]

Scottish, Welsh or NI resident applying in England: the rest of the GTOD corpus covers English rules. **GTOD take:** the Medr framework's principle that funding follows the workplace is how the English system works too, so a Scottish or Welsh resident working for an English employer at an English site is normally funded under English rules, but confirm with the employer and the English funding rules document before relying on it.

## What is UK-wide and what is England-only?

UK-wide (applies in all four nations):

- The apprentice National Minimum Wage rate: £8.00 an hour from 1 April 2026 for apprentices under 19, or 19 and over in the first year of the apprenticeship; after that the age rate applies (£10.85 for 18 to 20, £12.71 for 21 and over).[^s18] Scottish, Welsh and NI pages all require at least this rate.[^s3][^s32][^s21]
- The apprenticeship levy tax itself: 0.5% of pay bills over £3 million, £15,000 allowance, collected by HMRC from employers everywhere in the UK.[^s19]

Devolved (different in each nation):

- How levy money is spent: Scotland's share goes into Scottish Government skills programmes, Wales's into Medr-funded provision, and there is no digital levy account in either.[^s16][^s27]
- Levels and names: SCQF in Scotland; Foundation/Apprenticeship/Higher/Degree in Wales; ApprenticeshipsNI and Higher Level Apprenticeships in Northern Ireland.[^s12][^s27][^s35]
- Eligibility, English and maths rules, and vacancy sites, as set out above.[^s7][^s32][^s36]

England-only, wherever they appear in the rest of the GTOD corpus:

- GOV.UK's Find an apprenticeship vacancy service. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each run a separate service.[^s1][^s28][^s36]
- The Department for Education apprenticeship funding rules, the English levy digital account and co-investment rules; Scotland is funded by SDS and SFC, Wales by Medr, Northern Ireland by the Department for the Economy.[^s5][^s27][^s37]
- Level 7 apprenticeship funding changes: Wales funds degree apprenticeships at Level 6 only, and Scotland and Northern Ireland use their own budgets for master's-level (SCQF 11 / Level 7) apprenticeships.[^s27][^s2][^s35]
- Functional skills / GCSE English and maths exit requirements: Wales uses Essential Skills Wales, and Scotland sets requirements per framework.[^s32][^s7]
