## In one line

The public sector runs some of the biggest apprenticeship programmes in the UK: the Civil Service takes apprentices at Levels 2 to 7 from age 16 across all four nations, the NHS trains nurses through a Level 6 degree apprenticeship and nursing associates at Level 5, police forces in England and Wales run a three year Level 6 Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship, and in England you can now train as a teacher without a degree through the Teacher Degree Apprenticeship.[^s1][^s23][^s24][^s29][^s37]

> [!GAP]
> The Civil Service Jobs website (civilservicejobs.service.gov.uk) returned only a "quick check" bot verification page in this run, so it is referenced here through the Civil Service Careers pages that point to it rather than opened directly.

## Key facts

- Civil Service apprenticeships run at Levels 2 to 7, can be done in England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, are open to anyone aged 16 or over who meets the Civil Service nationality rules, and are advertised on Civil Service Jobs.[^s1]
- As at September 2026, HMRC says its apprentices are paid the full salary for their grade rather than an apprentice wage, and that an Administrative Officer grade apprentice starts on "£28,000 plus".[^s7]
- DWP Digital says its digital apprentices start on a salary of at least £29,500 with full Civil Service benefits and a permanent job, as at September 2026.[^s8]
- The Home Office offers 33 apprenticeship standards across 11 professions, from Level 2 customer service to Level 7 senior leader, and was ranked 16th in the Top 100 Apprenticeship Employers 2024.[^s14]
- The Civil Service nationality rules (GOV.UK, last updated 8 August 2023) say roughly 75% of posts are open to Commonwealth citizens and EEA nationals as well as UK nationals; the rest are reserved for UK nationals.[^s2]
- The Registered Nurse Degree (NMC 2018) apprenticeship standard is Level 6, typically 48 months, with a maximum funding band of £26,000; version 2.0 took effect on 25 March 2026.[^s23]
- The Nursing Associate (NMC 2018) apprenticeship is Level 5, typically 24 months, with a funding band of £15,000; version 2.0 took effect on 25 March 2026.[^s24][^s25]
- Trainee nursing associates in the NHS are paid on Agenda for Change band 3, and registered nursing associates are usually employed at band 4.[^s17]
- The Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship (PCDA) lasts about three years, leads to a Level 6 degree in Professional Policing Practice, and needs A levels or equivalent in at least two subjects.[^s29]
- The Metropolitan Police pays new constables a starting salary of £43,689 including allowances on both the PCEP and PCDA routes, rising to £49,137 after three years on the PCDA route, as at September 2026.[^s31]
- West Midlands Police had its PCDA closed to new applications as at September 2026, with PCEP, detective and graduate routes open.[^s33]
- Police Scotland is not covered by the College of Policing routes; it was recruiting constables nationally with no closing date as at September 2026, and applicants must be at least 17 and a half to apply and 18 to be appointed.[^s26][^s34][^s35]
- The Teacher Degree Apprenticeship (England only) lets you train as a teacher without a degree: you earn a bachelor's degree and Qualified Teacher Status together, spend about 40% of your time (two days a week) at university, pay no tuition fees, and are paid an unqualified teacher's salary in maintained schools; you must be at least 16 and have GCSE grade 4 in English and maths (plus science for primary).[^s37]
- The postgraduate teaching apprenticeship (England only) needs a bachelor's degree, lasts at least nine months, involves teaching in school four days a week with one day of training, has no fees and pays an unqualified teacher's salary.[^s38]
- From 1 September 2025 the unqualified teacher pay range in England is £22,601 to £35,259 outside London, £24,066 to £36,718 in the fringe, £26,789 to £39,450 in outer London and £28,343 to £40,994 in inner London.[^s40]
- The Early Years Educator apprenticeship is Level 3, typically 18 months, and the current version 1.5 (effective 12 June 2025) is in revision with a new version and £7,000 funding band due from 1 November 2026.[^s41][^s42]

## What does the Civil Service's apprenticeship offer involve?

The Civil Service Careers apprenticeships page says apprenticeships are available from Level 2 (GCSE equivalent) up to Level 7 (Master's equivalent), and can be done in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.[^s1] They are open to anyone aged 16 or over who meets the Civil Service nationality rules, with further restrictions possible for some posts.[^s1]

Professions listed on the page include science and engineering, counter fraud, customer services, cyber security, digital, economics, finance, legal, operational delivery and social research.[^s1] The page names the Civil Service Career Launch apprenticeship, a Level 3 Business Administrator route.[^s1] All applications go through the Civil Service Jobs website; the page gives no salary figures or application windows.[^s1]

The Civil Service Early Careers page gives a Level 4 Tax Technician apprentice at HMRC as an example, says apprenticeships are open from age 16, and mentions cyber talent schemes that include apprenticeships within the Government Security Profession.[^s6]

## Does the Civil Service Fast Track apprenticeship still exist?

**GTOD take:** the Civil Service Fast Track apprenticeship no longer appears on the Civil Service Careers apprenticeships or Fast Stream pages, and the only page in this research that mentions it is the MoD guidance last updated on 24 March 2017.[^s1][^s4][^s10] Treat "Fast Track" as a historic scheme name and search Civil Service Jobs for current apprenticeship vacancies instead.

The Fast Stream, which is the Civil Service graduate scheme and not an apprenticeship, does accept applications from degree apprentices as well as final year undergraduates and graduates; its starting salary is £31,554, most schemes accept a 2:2, applications for 2025/26 were closed, and the next cohorts start on 7 or 28 September 2026.[^s4]

## Can I join the Civil Service if I am not a British citizen?

GOV.UK's Civil Service nationality rules (last updated 8 August 2023) say you can work in the Civil Service if you are a UK national or have dual nationality where one part is British.[^s2] About 75% of Civil Service posts are also open to Commonwealth citizens and EEA nationals; the remaining posts, which carry special allegiance requirements, are reserved for UK nationals.[^s2]

HMRC adds its own apprenticeship conditions: you must have the right to live and work in the UK, hold valid ID and a National Insurance number, have lived in the UK or EEA for the three years before starting, be out of full time education (or finishing that academic year), and be available for at least 30 hours a week.[^s7]

## What are Success Profiles and why do they matter for Civil Service apprenticeships?

Success Profiles are the recruitment framework used across the Civil Service (GOV.UK page last updated 29 January 2025).[^s3] A profile has five elements: behaviours, strengths, ability, experience and technical skills, and not every element is used for every job.[^s3] Each job advert says which elements you will be assessed on, so read it before you write your application.[^s3]

**GTOD take:** for apprenticeships, adverts usually lean on behaviours and strengths rather than experience, but check the individual advert on Civil Service Jobs rather than assuming.

## What does HMRC's apprenticeship involve?

HMRC says its apprentices are paid the full salary of their grade rather than a basic apprentice wage, and that an Administrative Officer (AO) grade apprentice starts on "£28,000 plus", as at September 2026.[^s7] Schemes listed on HMRC's apprenticeships page include the Finance Foundation Scheme (Levels 3 and 4, AO grade), Finance Graduate Scheme (Level 7, HO grade), Policy apprenticeship (Level 4), Project Manager apprenticeship (Level 4), Project Management Degree Apprenticeship (Level 6), customer service apprenticeships, Counter Fraud Investigator (Level 4), and graduate and non-graduate solicitor apprenticeships.[^s7] HMRC says it runs apprenticeships across more than 16 professions including digital, data analysis and the Government Economic Service.[^s7]

The Finance Foundation Apprenticeship Scheme lasts three years at AO grade and takes you through a Level 3 AAT apprenticeship (about 18 months) then a Level 4 AAT apprenticeship (about 18 months), ending with Accounting Technician status.[^s5] Entry needs five GCSEs at grades 4 to 9 (A* to C) including maths and English, an eligibility assessment by the training provider, being aged 16 or over by 1 September 2026, no finance or accountancy degree, and no previous government funded course covering the same content.[^s5]

The Finance Graduate Scheme lasts four years at Higher Officer grade, starting with a Level 4 finance apprenticeship (CIMA Certificate level) and moving to a Level 7 CIMA, CIPFA or other accountancy body qualification.[^s5] External applicants need a 2:1 degree in any subject.[^s5] Both schemes use an application form, Civil Service numerical, verbal and judgement tests, a recorded video interview and a virtual assessment centre.[^s5] Neither page gives salary figures, locations or application windows; HMRC points to the job advert for timings.[^s5]

## What does DWP's apprenticeship involve?

DWP Digital offers digital apprenticeships with a starting salary of at least £29,500, full Civil Service benefits and a permanent job, as at September 2026.[^s8] Apprentices are based in one of DWP's digital hubs with hybrid working, and learn through a partner training provider with one to one tutoring and lab sessions.[^s8] Schemes named on the page include software engineering and DevOps engineer; skills covered include APIs, AI, cyber security, cloud and DevOps.[^s8] DWP says it launches more digital apprenticeships through the year and asks people to register interest; no entry requirements or dates are given.[^s8]

DWP also runs a Social Mobility Apprenticeship (SMA), an 18 month role with the chance of a permanent job after 12 months, which has provided over 3,000 places in more than 13 years (GOV.UK blog, 9 February 2026).[^s9] Qualifications on offer are customer service (Levels 2 to 3), business administration (Level 3), credit controller and collector (Level 2) and public sector compliance officer (Level 3).[^s9] General applicants must be 16 or over, registered unemployed and facing barriers such as limited work experience; school leaver applicants must be 16 to 19, not in education, employment or training, and meet criteria such as a low income household.[^s9] Vacancies are not advertised externally: work coaches nominate people from their caseloads and school advisers promote places in schools.[^s9] No salary is stated.[^s9]

## What does the Home Office's apprenticeship involve?

The Home Office says it offers 33 apprenticeship standards across 11 professions.[^s14] These include leadership and management (Levels 3 to 7, from team leader to senior leader), improvement (Levels 3 to 6), associate project manager (Level 4), public service operational delivery officer (Level 3), business administration (Level 3), HR (Levels 3, 5 and 7), customer service (Levels 2 to 3), procurement and supply chain practitioner (Level 4), finance (Levels 3 to 4, including assistant accountant and accountancy or taxation professional) and policy (Level 4).[^s14]

Apprenticeships last more than 12 months with at least six hours a week of off the job training.[^s14] The Home Office says apprenticeships suit people starting out, changing career or upskilling, that previous qualifications including degrees are acceptable, and that it gives extra support to people without GCSE English or maths.[^s14] It was ranked 16th in the Top 100 Apprenticeship Employers 2024.[^s14] No salary, locations or application dates are stated.[^s14]

## What does the Ministry of Defence's civilian apprenticeship involve?

GOV.UK's guidance on MoD apprenticeships (last updated 24 March 2017) lists around 20 civilian schemes across the department and its agencies, from Level 2 (equivalent to five GCSEs) through Level 3 (equivalent to two A levels) up to Level 7 (Master's level).[^s10] Named areas include MoD information apprenticeships in IT, software, telecoms and information security, the Defence Commercial Higher Apprenticeship Programme, Defence Equipment and Support (engineering, finance, commercial), the Defence Science and Technology Laboratory (engineering, lab technician, business administration) and the UK Hydrographic Office (IT, business administration).[^s10]

## Is there a Civil Service economics degree apprenticeship?

GOV.UK announced on 20 September 2018 that the Civil Service was launching the UK's first degree level apprenticeship in economics, delivered with the University of Kent for the Government Economic Service.[^s15] Participating departments at launch were HM Treasury, DWP, the business department, DfE and Defra.[^s15] Entry needed GCSE maths at grade B (6) or above and 96 UCAS points (CCC at A level or equivalent), and the salary was about £22,000 in London and over £20,000 nationally, as at September 2018.[^s15]

## What does an NHS apprenticeship involve?

NHS Health Careers says apprenticeships give routes into many of the more than 350 NHS careers, mixing on the job training with classroom learning, and that entry requirements vary by employer and by level.[^s16] As a guide, advanced level apprenticeships typically ask for four or five GCSEs at grades 9 to 4 (A to C), and higher apprenticeships typically ask for A levels, a Level 3 qualification or relevant work experience.[^s16] Vacancies are advertised on NHS Jobs, and Health Careers warns there is often a lot of competition for places.[^s16] NHS Jobs says around 25,000 posts are advertised on the site every month.[^s21]

## How are NHS apprentices paid?

NHS Health Careers says trainee nursing associates are paid on band 3 of Agenda for Change while training, and registered nursing associates are usually employed at band 4.[^s17] NHS Employers' apprenticeship pay guidance (13 June 2024) says existing NHS staff who take an approved apprenticeship get the higher of the apprenticeship rate or their preserved previous pay step, and sets out Annex 21 percentages for degree apprenticeships of 60 to 70% of the top of the band in year 1, 65 to 70% in year 2, 70% in year 3 and 75% in year 4.[^s18] Pay preservation covers basic pay only, not unsocial hours or overtime.[^s18]

> [!GAP]
> Pay for a school leaver starting a non-degree NHS apprenticeship (for example a Level 2 or 3 healthcare support worker or business administration role) is set by each trust's job advert and could not be stated as a single figure. The NHS Employers page gives worked examples in £ but these are illustrations tied to specific band points, so check the advert on NHS Jobs for the actual rate.

## What does the Registered Nurse Degree Apprenticeship involve?

The Registered Nurse Degree (NMC 2018) apprenticeship standard is Level 6 (an integrated degree), typically 48 months long, with a maximum funding band of £26,000; version 1.1 has been retired and version 2.0 took effect on 25 March 2026.[^s23] It covers adult, mental health, learning disability and children's nursing, and the training provider must be approved by the Nursing and Midwifery Council.[^s23] Apprenticeship standards are England-only.

Individual trusts run the scheme. University Hospitals Dorset's version leads to a BSc (Hons) in Adult Nursing with Bournemouth University over about 3.5 years, starting August/September 2026, with at least 2,300 placement hours and 2,300 theory hours and at least one day a week at university.[^s19] You must be 18 or over at the start and hold GCSE grade C/4 in English and maths or an accepted equivalent; A levels, an Access to HE Diploma or a Level 3 apprenticeship are considered case by case.[^s19] There are no tuition fees and you are employed on an NHS salary; UHD asks people interested in the 2027 intake to register interest.[^s19]

University Hospitals Bristol and Weston runs a full four year RNDA (Levels 4 to 6) and a two year accelerated route for qualified nursing associates or assistant practitioners.[^s20] Full route apprentices are paid at band 3 in years 1 and 2 and band 4 in years 3 and 4; accelerated apprentices are on band 4 throughout, as at September 2026.[^s20] The trust allocates your university, and fees are paid through the apprenticeship levy.[^s20]

## What does the Nursing Associate apprenticeship involve?

The Nursing Associate (NMC 2018) apprenticeship is Level 5, typically 24 months, with a funding band of £15,000, and leads to registration with the Nursing and Midwifery Council; version 2.0 took effect on 25 March 2026.[^s24][^s25] Health Careers says entry usually needs GCSE grades 9 to 4 (A to C) in maths and English or Functional Skills Level 2, and some employers also ask for a Level 3 qualification.[^s17] Trainees work about 37.5 hours a week on shifts including nights and weekends, are paid at band 3, and get 27 days of annual leave plus bank holidays.[^s17] Places are usually advertised on NHS Jobs, though some universities take direct applications where you pay your own training costs.[^s17] A registered nursing associate can later shorten a registered nurse degree apprenticeship to two years.[^s17]

No. The NHS Graduate Management Training Scheme page makes no mention of an apprenticeship route.[^s22] It is a graduate scheme with a starting salary of £31,276 rising to £33,681 after 12 months; applications for 2026 were closed and applications for the 2027 cohort open at midday on 23 September 2026.[^s22]

## What is the Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship (PCDA)?

The College of Policing, the professional body for policing in England and Wales, lists four ways to join as a new constable: the Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship (PCDA), the Degree Holder Entry Programme (DHEP), the Professional Policing Degree (PPD) and the Police Constable Entry Programme (PCEP).[^s26] The PCDA lasts about three years, you are a warranted officer from day one, and you finish with a Level 6 degree in Professional Policing Practice.[^s29] Entry needs an A level or equivalent in at least two subjects and competence in written and spoken English.[^s29] The national Join the Police site says the minimum qualification across routes is a Level 3 qualification, defined as two A levels, and that whether you need a driving licence varies by force.[^s27]

The PCEP is a two year route with no degree study; it is being phased out gradually but some forces still offer it.[^s29] The DHEP and Detective DHEP are two year routes for people who already hold a degree in any subject other than professional policing, leading to a Level 6 graduate diploma; the detective version requires the National Investigators' Examination and PIP2 accreditation.[^s28][^s29] Police Now runs two year graduate programmes needing a 2:1.[^s29] None of the national pages state salary.[^s28][^s29]

## What does the Metropolitan Police's apprenticeship involve?

The Met offers two entry routes for new constables: the two year Police Constable Entry Programme, taught at the Met Training Centre and on the job, and the three year Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship, taught at university and on the job with a fully funded degree.[^s30] For people without the academic requirements, the Met partners with London colleges on an access route into policing, including a Level 3 Access to HE Diploma in Policing.[^s30][^s36]

Starting salary is £43,689 including allowances on both routes, as at September 2026; it rises to £46,416 after two years on PCEP or £49,137 after three years on PCDA once probation is passed, and can reach £62,094 after five years.[^s31] You must be 17 or over to apply and 18 to start, and the usual upper age is 57.[^s32] The PCEP and PCDA need two A levels or a Level 3 qualification, or equivalent work experience; the Detective Constable Entry Programme needs a degree.[^s32] You must have lived in the UK continuously for the last three years, pass a fitness (bleep) test, medical and eyesight checks, have no current illegal drug use, and meet tattoo and financial rules.[^s32] No application dates are stated.[^s30]

## What does West Midlands Police's apprenticeship involve?

West Midlands Police lists the Police Constable Entry Programme (no degree needed, hands on training), the Detective Constable Entry Programme, the Professional Policing Degree route for people who already have that degree, the two year Degree Holder Entry Programme and Detective Constable DHEP (any degree, graduate diploma), and the three year Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship with a fully funded policing degree.[^s33] As at September 2026 the PCEP, DCEP, PPD, DHEP and DCDHEP were open, and the PCDA was closed to new applications with no reopening date given.[^s33] No salary figures are stated.[^s33]

## Does Police Scotland run an apprenticeship?

Police Scotland recruits its own way and is not covered by the College of Policing routes, which apply to England and Wales only.[^s26] As at September 2026 it was recruiting constables nationally with no closing date, promising at least two weeks' notice if a deadline is set.[^s34] You must be at least 17 and a half to apply and 18 to be appointed, be a British, EU/EEA or Commonwealth citizen or have indefinite leave to remain, and have lived in the UK for at least three years before applying.[^s35] Fitness is assessed against the Scottish Police Service National Fitness Standard, and BMI must be between 18 and 30 at the medical.[^s35] The eligibility page does not set an academic qualification requirement.[^s35]

## Can I become a teacher through an apprenticeship without a degree?

Yes, in England, through the Teacher Degree Apprenticeship (TDA). DfE's Get Into Teaching page describes the TDA as a new route to qualified teacher status that does not need an existing degree: you earn a bachelor's degree and QTS at the same time on a multi-year, full time programme, spending about 40% of your time (two days a week) at university.[^s37] You must be at least 16, have lived in the UK for three years or more, hold GCSE grade 4 (C) or above in English and maths (and science for primary), and meet the A level requirements set by the university.[^s37]

There are no tuition fees, but you cannot get student finance.[^s37] In maintained (local authority) schools TDA trainees are paid on the unqualified teacher pay range; academies and independent schools set their own pay, often following the same ranges.[^s37] As at September 2026 the page says the providers confirmed so far offer TDAs in secondary maths, with more subjects being added up to September 2026 and training due to start in autumn 2026; courses are listed on DfE's Find Teacher Training Courses service.[^s37]

> [!GAP]
> The Get Into Teaching TDA page still reads as if written before the autumn 2026 start, so it is not clear from the page which subjects and providers are actually running TDAs for 2026/27 or whether recruitment for 2027 has opened. Check the live course list on Find Teacher Training Courses (filter for teacher degree apprenticeships). The page does not state the number of years the TDA takes.

## What is the postgraduate teaching apprenticeship and what does it pay?

The postgraduate teaching apprenticeship (PGTA) is an England-only route to QTS for people who already have a bachelor's degree.[^s38] It lasts at least nine months, can start at any point in the academic year, and usually means teaching in school four days a week with one day for training.[^s38] Some providers want significant prior school experience, and non-UK citizens must have lived in the UK for three years (with exceptions for Afghan and Ukraine resettlement schemes).[^s38] You pay no training fees and are paid an unqualified teacher's salary, which varies by school; DfE points out this can be worth less than a fee-paying course with a tax-free bursary of up to £31,000, because salary is taxed.[^s38] You apply through Find Teacher Training Courses, and DfE suggests also applying for fee-paying courses to improve your chances.[^s38]

DfE's salaried training page lists three salaried postgraduate routes: School Direct salaried (one academic year), Teach First (two academic years) and the postgraduate teaching apprenticeship (nine months).[^s39] Trainees on salaried routes cannot get bursaries, scholarships or student finance, and PGTA trainees teach no more than 80% of a full timetable.[^s39]

The School Teachers' Pay and Conditions Document 2025 sets the unqualified teacher pay range in England from 1 September 2025 at £22,601 to £35,259 outside London, £24,066 to £36,718 in the fringe area, £26,789 to £39,450 in outer London and £28,343 to £40,994 in inner London; the main pay range for qualified teachers starts at £32,916 outside London.[^s40] The document applies to England only, as Wales has set its own teachers' pay since 2018.[^s40]

The Early Years Educator apprenticeship is a Level 3 standard typically lasting 18 months; entry needs Level 2 English and maths (Entry Level 3 for apprentices with an education, health and care plan) plus a first aid qualification, and version 1.2 carried a £6,000 funding band.[^s41] The current version 1.5 took effect on 12 June 2025 and is "in revision", with a revised version and a £7,000 funding band due from 1 November 2026.[^s42]

## Are there local government and regulator apprenticeships?

The Local Government Association confirms councils recruit apprentices onto the Level 6 Social Worker (Integrated Degree) apprenticeship, and welcomed a government clarification that both undergraduate and postgraduate (Level 7) delivery routes remain fundable within that Level 6 standard for now, while warning that government does not expect to fund Level 7 delivery within Level 6 standards in the long term.[^s43] The LGA gathered evidence from over 40 councils, universities and sector partners before raising the issue.[^s43]

The FCA's apprenticeships page lists ten apprenticeship roles (including accountant, project manager, business administrator, compliance and risk, cyber security technologist, data analyst and paralegal) but states that applications for its 2021 programmes are closed and gives no levels, salary or dates.[^s44]
