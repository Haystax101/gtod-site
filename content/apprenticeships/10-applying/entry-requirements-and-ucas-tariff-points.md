## In one line

There is no single national entry requirement for an apprenticeship: each employer sets its own GCSE, A level or UCAS point bar, but in England the government funding rules add one fixed condition, which is that anyone who starts a Level 3 or higher apprenticeship aged 16 to 18 must hold or achieve Level 2 English and maths (GCSE grade 4 or a Functional Skills Level 2 pass) before they can finish.[^s3][^s8]

## Key facts

- England only: an apprentice aged 16 to 18 on the day they start a Level 3 or higher apprenticeship must hold or achieve an approved Level 2 English and maths qualification before they can complete, under the 2026 to 2027 funding rules (paragraphs 29.2 and 55).[^s3]
- England only: an apprentice aged 16 to 18 on a Level 2 apprenticeship must achieve at least Level 1 English and maths (Functional Skills Level 1 or GCSE grade 3 to 1) and then work towards Level 2, but does not have to sit the Level 2 assessment unless the provider thinks they are ready (paragraph 54.1).[^s3]
- England only: since 11 February 2025, apprentices aged 19 or over at the start do not have to pass English and maths to complete their apprenticeship; they study it only if the employer agrees, and then only need to attempt the assessment.[^s6][^s3]
- GCSE grade 4 is a "standard pass" and grade 5 a "strong pass"; the bottom of grade 4 lines up with the bottom of the old grade C.[^s10]
- For a Level 3 or higher apprenticeship in England, the accepted Level 2 English evidence includes GCSE grade 4 (or C before 2017), a Functional Skills Level 2 pass, Scottish National 5 or Higher at grade C, and British Sign Language Level 2, as at the GOV.UK list updated 19 February 2026.[^s8]
- UCAS tariff points for A levels are A* 56, A 48, B 40, C 32, D 24, E 16; AS levels are worth 40% of an A level.[^s16][^s15]
- A BTEC National Extended Diploma is worth 168 points at D*D*D* down to 48 at PPP; a National Diploma runs 112 (D*D*) to 32 (PP); a National Extended Certificate runs 56 (D*) to 16 (P).[^s16]
- A T Level is worth 168 points at Distinction*, 144 at Distinction, 120 at Merit, 96 at Pass with a C or above in the core, and 72 at Pass with a D or E in the core.[^s18]
- Scottish Highers are worth A 33, B 27, C 21, D 15; Advanced Highers are A 56, B 48, C 40, D 32.[^s16]
- An EPQ is worth 28 points at A* down to 8 at E; Core Maths is 20 at A down to 6 at E.[^s16]
- GCSEs and other Level 2 qualifications carry no tariff points at all; only Level 3 (or SCQF Level 6) qualifications count.[^s14]
- UCAS says not all universities use tariff points, and employers are free to ignore them completely; some, like BAE Systems, quote a points figure (96 points for a degree apprenticeship in England and Wales, as at September 2026) while others, like PwC's entry-level programmes, say they focus on potential rather than academic background.[^s15][^s23][^s27]
- From the 2026 application cycle, a completed Level 3 apprenticeship itself earns UCAS points: 48 for a 12-month apprenticeship, 64 for 18 months, 96 for 24 months and 112 for 36 months.[^s19]
- The 16 to 19 "condition of funding" that forces college students without a grade 4 to keep studying English and maths does not apply to apprentices; apprentices have their own rules.[^s22]

## Do I need GCSE English and maths to start an apprenticeship?

Two different things get mixed up here: what the employer asks for in the job advert, and what the government's funding rules require you to achieve before you can finish. The employer's list is theirs to set. The funding rules are fixed and, in England, they are set out each year by the Department for Education.[^s5][^s3]

Most employers do ask for GCSE English and maths in the advert. BAE Systems, for example, asks for five GCSEs at grades 9 to 4 (A* to C) including maths and English for its advanced (Level 3) apprenticeships in England and Wales, with a science or technical subject added for engineering roles, as at September 2026.[^s24] But that is BAE's choice, not a law.

The funding rules only bite once you are on the apprenticeship. They say what English and maths you must hold or achieve before the end. For a 16 to 18 year old on a Level 3 or above apprenticeship, that is Level 2 in both subjects, which means GCSE grade 4 or above, or an equivalent, before you can complete.[^s3][^s8]

**GTOD take:** in practice, an employer taking on a 16 to 18 year old without grade 4 English and maths for a Level 3 apprenticeship is signing up to fund and timetable extra qualifications, so many simply require the GCSEs upfront. Applying without them is not impossible, but expect fewer doors to be open.

## What are the apprenticeship English and maths rules for 16 to 18 year olds?

These rules are England only. They come from the apprenticeship funding rules for August 2026 to July 2027 (version 3, published 29 July 2026), which apply to apprentices starting from 1 August 2026.[^s1][^s3]

Your age is measured on the day you start your apprenticeship training (paragraph 29.2). If you are 18 on that day, you are treated as 16 to 18 for the whole apprenticeship, even after you turn 19.[^s3]

If you start a Level 3 or higher apprenticeship aged 16 to 18 without Level 2 English and maths, you must achieve an approved Level 2 in both subjects before you can pass through the gateway to your end-point assessment and complete (paragraph 55).[^s3] Level 2 here means a GCSE at grade 4 or above, a Functional Skills Level 2 pass, or one of the other equivalents on the GOV.UK list.[^s8]

If you start a Level 2 apprenticeship aged 16 to 18 without Level 1, you must achieve at least Level 1 (Functional Skills Level 1 or GCSE grade 3 to 1) and then work towards Level 2 where there is time. You are not required to attempt the Level 2 assessment unless your provider is satisfied you are ready (paragraph 54.1).[^s3] If you already hold Level 1, you must start and take the assessments for Level 2 (paragraph 54.2.1).[^s3]

The training for English and maths must be on top of your minimum off-the-job training hours (paragraph 84.2), and it must not be delivered entirely by self-directed distance learning.[^s3][^s2]

The 2025 to 2026 rules (version 3, January 2026) say the same thing for 16 to 18 year olds at paragraphs 50 and 51, so nothing changed for this age group between the two years.[^s4]

## What changed in 2025 for apprentices aged 19 and over?

On 11 February 2025, during National Apprenticeship Week, the Department for Education announced with immediate effect that apprentices aged 19 or over at the start of their apprenticeship would no longer have to pass English and maths to complete.[^s6] Employers and providers had until 1 April 2025 to agree with each existing adult apprentice whether they would carry on studying the subjects.[^s6]

The rule is now written into the funding rules. For apprentices aged 19 or over at the start, English and maths is optional and only happens if the employer agrees to include it in the programme (paragraph 47.2 of the 2026 to 2027 rules).[^s3] Where it is included, the apprentice must attempt the assessment but does not have to pass it to complete the apprenticeship (paragraph 50.5).[^s3] An adult apprentice who wants the qualifications but whose employer says no can still use the separate adult skills funding entitlement (paragraph 57.2).[^s3]

This is England only. The DfE said at the time that the change could let as many as 10,000 more apprentices a year complete, and that the change to the pass rule did not alter the assessment of occupational skills.[^s6]

The same announcement cut the minimum apprenticeship length from 12 months to 8 months, effective from August 2025; the 2025 to 2026 rules confirm the 8-month minimum at paragraph 73.[^s6][^s4]

**GTOD take:** if you are 18 and undecided, the age-at-start rule matters. Start a Level 3 apprenticeship at 18 without grade 4 maths and you must pass it to finish. Start the same apprenticeship at 19 and you need not. That is not a reason to wait, but it is a reason to know which rule applies to you.

## What does GCSE grade 4 versus grade 5 mean?

GCSEs in England are graded 9 to 1, with 9 the highest. The Department for Education calls grade 4 a "standard pass" and grade 5 a "strong pass".[^s10] The bottom of grade 4 is aligned with the bottom of the old grade C, the bottom of grade 7 with the bottom of the old grade A, and the bottom of grade 1 with the bottom of the old grade G.[^s10][^s11]

The DfE's factsheet for employers says a grade 4 or above marks a similar achievement to the old grade C or above, and that employers who used to ask for a C should treat a grade 4 the same way unless they deliberately want to raise their bar.[^s10] Grade 9 is awarded to fewer students than the old A* was.[^s10]

For the apprenticeship funding rules, grade 4 is the line: GCSE grade 4 (awarded from 2017) or grade C (awarded before 2017) counts as Level 2.[^s8] Grade 5 is never required by the funding rules; if an employer asks for a 5 or a 6, that is their own choice.

**GTOD take:** if an advert says "grade 4 or above" you are fine with a 4. If it says "grade 5 or above" or "strong pass", a 4 will not meet the stated bar, but see the section on narrowly missing.

## Does Functional Skills Level 2 count the same as a GCSE grade 4?

For the apprenticeship funding rules in England, yes. A Functional Skills Level 2 pass sits alongside GCSE grade 4 on the GOV.UK list of acceptable Level 2 English qualifications for apprenticeships at Level 3 to 7, updated 19 February 2026.[^s8] Only Ofqual-regulated Functional Skills count.[^s7] GOV.UK's general list of qualification levels also puts Level 2 Functional Skills and GCSE grades 9 to 4 at the same level, Level 2.[^s12]

Other things on the Level 2 list for English include Key Skills Level 2, O level grade C (from 1975), CSE grade 1, British Sign Language Level 2, Essential Skills (Northern Ireland) Level 2, Scottish National 5 grade C, Scottish Higher grade C, an IB Diploma score of 3 and a European Baccalaureate score of 5.00.[^s8] A degree or other Level 4 or higher qualification counts if at least half its content is English or maths.[^s7]

English language tests such as ESOL, IELTS and CELTA do not count as equivalents.[^s7] For apprenticeships at Level 3 and above, a Scottish qualification must be at least SCQF Level 5.[^s7]

For a Level 2 apprenticeship, the Level 1 list includes GCSE grade 1 or above, a Functional Skills Level 1 pass, Scottish National 4, and Essential Skills Level 1.[^s8][^s9] For maths at Level 1, Core Maths at grade E and A level maths at grade E also appear on the list.[^s9]

**GTOD take:** the equivalence holds for the funding rule, not necessarily for an employer's advert. Some employers write "GCSE grade 4 or equivalent" and accept Functional Skills; some write "GCSE" and mean it. Ask if the advert is unclear.

## What if I have a learning difficulty and cannot get Level 2 English or maths?

The 2026 to 2027 funding rules (paragraphs 58 to 62) allow an apprentice with an identified learning difficulty or disability that directly affects their ability to achieve English or maths to work towards Entry Level 3 Functional Skills instead.[^s3] This needs evidence such as an Education, Health and Care plan, a statement of special educational needs, or a formal structured assessment, plus a professional judgement (from a SEN specialist or equivalent) that reasonable adjustments alone would not be enough.[^s3] The provider must still support the apprentice's literacy and numeracy.[^s3]

This is England only.[^s3]

## How are UCAS tariff points calculated?

The UCAS tariff turns Level 3 qualifications and grades into a number so that different qualifications can be compared.[^s13] Each qualification gets a size band from 1 to 4 (based on guided learning hours) and a grade band from 3 to 14; the two are multiplied to give the points.[^s16] An A level is size band 4, so grade A* (grade band 14) is 4 × 14 = 56 points and grade E (grade band 4) is 4 × 4 = 16.[^s16]

The current scale has been in use for courses starting from September 2017; under the previous scale an A* was worth 140 points, so if you see a requirement like "240 points" it is almost certainly the old scale.[^s17] AS levels are set at 40% of the equivalent A level grade.[^s17][^s15]

Only Level 3 (or Scottish SCQF Level 6) qualifications attract points; GCSEs and other Level 2 qualifications never do.[^s14] Where two qualifications overlap (for example an AS and an A level in the same subject), only the higher one is counted.[^s15]

UCAS is clear that the tariff is a comparison tool, not an entitlement: universities and colleges do not have to use it, many make offers in grades rather than points, and even those that use points can decide which qualifications they will count for a given course.[^s15][^s13] UCAS updates the tables each year as qualifications change.[^s14] The official calculator is at ucas.com.[^s15]

## What are the UCAS tariff points for A levels, BTECs, T Levels, Scottish Highers, IB, EPQ and Core Maths?

The values below are from UCAS's published tariff tables (introduced for 2017 entry), which UCAS still uses; the T Level values come from UCAS's T Level page.[^s16][^s18] Leicester College's summary page, checked on 3 September 2026, gives the same A level, BTEC Extended Diploma and T Level figures.[^s20]

A level: A* 56, A 48, B 40, C 32, D 24, E 16.[^s16]

AS level: A 20, B 16, C 12, D 10, E 6.[^s16]

BTEC Level 3 National Extended Diploma (three A level size): D*D*D* 168, D*D*D 160, D*DD 152, DDD 144, DDM 128, DMM 112, MMM 96, MMP 80, MPP 64, PPP 48.[^s16]

BTEC Level 3 National Diploma (two A level size): D*D* 112, D*D 104, DD 96, DM 80, MM 64, MP 48, PP 32.[^s16]

BTEC Level 3 National Extended Certificate or Certificate (one A level size): D* 56, D 48, M 32, P 16.[^s16]

T Level: Distinction* 168, Distinction 144 (which UCAS equates to AAA at A level), Merit 120 (BBB), Pass with a C or above in the core 96 (CCC), Pass with a D or E in the core 72 (DDD).[^s18] A student who completes only part of a T Level can still get some points, split according to the weighting of the core and the occupational specialism.[^s18]

Scottish Higher: A 33, B 27, C 21, D 15.[^s16]

Scottish Advanced Higher: A 56, B 48, C 40, D 32.[^s16]

International Baccalaureate Diploma: no single figure; points are added up per component. Higher Level subjects score 7 = 56, 6 = 48, 5 = 32, 4 = 24, 3 = 12; Standard Level subjects score 7 = 28, 6 = 24, 5 = 16, 4 = 12, 3 = 6; the Extended Essay and Theory of Knowledge each score A 12, B 10, C 8, D 6, E 4.[^s16]

Extended Project Qualification (EPQ): A* 28, A 24, B 20, C 16, D 12, E 8.[^s16]

Core Maths (Level 3 Certificate in Mathematical Studies): A 20, B 16, C 12, D 10, E 6.[^s16]

So three A levels at BBB are 120 points, the same as a T Level Merit or a BTEC Extended Diploma at DMM plus an AS at grade B.[^s16][^s18]

## Do a completed Level 3 apprenticeship or a T Level earn UCAS points?

Yes to both. UCAS added Level 3 apprenticeships to the tariff for the 2026 application cycle, allocating points by length: a 12-month apprenticeship earns 48 points, 18 months earns 64, 24 months earns 96 and 36 months earns 112.[^s19] UCAS describes a two-year apprenticeship as aligned with AA or CCC at A level, or a T Level Pass with A* to C in the core.[^s19] Only the pass counts; merit and distinction grades are not given extra points because not every apprenticeship across the UK has them.[^s19]

T Levels are two-year courses in England of around 1,800 hours, and their tariff points run from 168 for Distinction* to 72 for a Pass with a D or E in the core.[^s18]

**GTOD take:** this matters if you might switch from an apprenticeship to a full-time degree later. A Level 3 apprenticeship no longer leaves you with zero points on a UCAS form.

## Do employers actually use UCAS points for apprenticeships?

Some do, some do not, and UCAS's own apprenticeship tariff page is about universities using points, not employers.[^s19] UCAS says openly that not all universities and colleges use tariff points, and that many make offers in grades instead.[^s15] Employers have even more freedom because nothing obliges them to use the tariff at all.

Examples of published requirements, from employer pages opened for this document (dates in the sources list):

BAE Systems degree apprenticeships (England and Wales): five GCSEs at grade 9 to 4 (A* to C) or equivalent, including maths and English, plus a minimum of "240/96 UCAS points"; some roles also need a science or technical subject.[^s23] The 240 is the old tariff and the 96 is the current one, which is CCC at A level.[^s23][^s16] For Scotland, BAE asks for five National 5s at C or above including English, maths and a science, plus three or four Highers at C or above including English and maths.[^s23] BAE's 2026 window has closed and applications for 2027 open in January 2027.[^s23]

BAE Systems advanced (Level 3) apprenticeships: five GCSEs at 9 to 4 including maths and English, plus a science or technical subject for engineering roles; in Scotland, five National 4s or four National 5s at C plus three Highers at C.[^s24] BAE says requirements vary by role.[^s24] BAE lists its apprenticeships at Level 2, Level 3, Levels 4 to 5, and Level 6 (degree, four to five years).[^s25]

PwC Flying Start degree programmes (Accounting): entry requirements are set by the partner universities (Newcastle, Manchester, Reading and Queen Mary University of London), each with its own admissions policy, so PwC's own page gives no grade figure.[^s26] PwC's entry-level programmes are described as focusing on potential, motivation and transferable skills rather than academic background.[^s27]

Civil Service apprenticeships: open to anyone aged 16 or over who meets the Civil Service nationality rules, from Level 2 up to Level 7; no GCSE or UCAS figure is given on the main page.[^s28]

Accenture, Lloyds Banking Group and KPMG list their apprenticeship programmes and application stages but state no grade or UCAS thresholds on the pages checked; KPMG's process is an application form, online assessments, video interview and an in-person "Launch Pad" day.[^s29][^s30][^s31]

> Rolls-Royce, IBM, EY and Goldman Sachs apprenticeship pages could not be opened in this session (404 errors or old URLs), so their current requirements are not stated here. Individual vacancies on Find an apprenticeship (gov.uk) carry the requirements for that specific role and are the place to check.

**GTOD take:** the pattern across big employers is that engineering and defence firms publish hard numbers (GCSE count plus a points or grade figure), while professional services firms increasingly publish no academic threshold and rely on online tests and interviews. Never assume a firm without a published threshold has a low one; assume its assessments do the filtering instead.

## What does "or equivalent" mean in an entry requirement?

"Or equivalent" means the employer will consider qualifications other than the one named, but it is the employer who decides what counts. There are two formal reference points you can use in the conversation.

For English and maths, GOV.UK publishes the definitive list of what counts as Level 2 for apprenticeship funding, including Functional Skills Level 2, Key Skills Level 2, O levels at C, CSE grade 1, Scottish National 5 and Higher at C, Northern Irish and Welsh Essential Skills Level 2, and British Sign Language Level 2 for English.[^s8] An employer that says "GCSE grade 4 or equivalent" for English or maths would be hard pressed to reject something on that list.

For Level 3 qualifications, the UCAS tariff gives a size-and-grade comparison, so "96 UCAS points or equivalent" can be met by CCC at A level, a BTEC Extended Diploma at MMM, or a T Level Pass with a C or above in the core.[^s16][^s18] Three Scottish Highers at AAA only reach 99 points, so a Scottish applicant chasing a points target usually needs a fourth Higher or an Advanced Higher.[^s16] But UCAS itself says a university (and by extension an employer) can decide not to count a qualification if its content is not relevant to the course.[^s15]

GOV.UK's list of qualification levels is the other useful reference: A levels, T Levels and advanced apprenticeships are all Level 3; HNCs and higher apprenticeships are Level 4; HNDs and foundation degrees are Level 5; honours degrees and degree apprenticeships are Level 6.[^s12]

**GTOD take:** if you hold a BTEC, T Level, Scottish qualification or IB and the advert only names A levels, apply anyway and say in your application exactly what you hold and its tariff value. Most "A levels" wording is shorthand, not a ban.

## What are contextual or adjusted offers, and do apprenticeships use them?

Contextual admissions means a university or employer lowers or adjusts its stated requirement for applicants whose circumstances made it harder to get top grades, using measures such as the applicant's school, postcode or free school meal eligibility.

> [!GAP]
> UCAS's contextual offers page could not be opened in this session (the fetch was rate-limited), so this document does not give the UCAS definition or the list of measures used. No employer page opened in this session stated a contextual policy for apprenticeships either. What would settle it: UCAS's contextual admissions page and any employer's early careers FAQ that mentions contextual recruitment.

**GTOD take:** the closest thing on the employer pages opened here is PwC saying its entry-level programmes look at potential rather than academic background, which is a broad statement rather than a formal adjusted offer.[^s27] If your grades are below an advert's bar and your school or circumstances are relevant, say so in the application; it costs nothing.

## What should I do if I narrowly miss the entry requirements?

First, understand which bar you have missed. If it is the employer's advertised bar (say, 112 points and you have 104), the employer can waive it if they want to; nothing stops them.[^s15] If it is the funding-rules bar (a 16 to 18 year old without Level 2 English or maths on a Level 3 apprenticeship), the employer cannot waive it, but you can still start and achieve the qualification during the apprenticeship.[^s3]

Contact the employer before you assume you are out. Results day is in August and many apprenticeships have already interviewed, so the employer knows you. Ask whether they will still take you, whether they will take you on a lower-level apprenticeship, or whether they will hold your place if you resit. **GTOD take:** this is the single most useful action; a formula did not reject you, a person did, and a person can change their mind.

If it is GCSE English or maths you have missed, you have two routes. On an apprenticeship in England, the provider can fund and deliver GCSE or Functional Skills up to Level 2 as part of the programme; the 2026 to 2027 rules list both as fundable (paragraph 53).[^s3] At college, if you are 16 to 18 on a study programme or T Level without a grade 4, the condition of funding (guidance last updated 24 July 2026) means you must keep studying the subject: with a grade 3 you must study towards the GCSE, and with a grade 2 or below you can study either the GCSE or Functional Skills Level 2.[^s21][^s22] That condition does not apply to apprentices.[^s22]

If you have missed A level or Level 3 grades for a degree apprenticeship, the realistic alternatives are a Level 3 (advanced) apprenticeship, which needs GCSEs rather than A levels, or a Level 4 or 5 (higher) apprenticeship, which sits between A levels and a degree.[^s12] BAE Systems, for instance, runs Level 3, Level 4 to 5 and Level 6 routes side by side.[^s25] A completed Level 3 apprenticeship of 24 months is now worth 96 UCAS points if you later apply to university.[^s19]

Resitting A levels or an equivalent course means applying in the next cycle. **GTOD take:** before resitting, ask the employer whether they would rather you start on a lower-level apprenticeship now and progress internally; many prefer a known apprentice over a stranger with better grades.

> [!GAP]
> GCSE resit exam dates (the November series for English language and maths) and the rules on who can enter them were not verified in this session. What would settle it: the JCQ or exam board timetable pages and the Ofqual conditions on November entries.

## Do these rules apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

The apprenticeship funding rules, the English and maths rules and the 16 to 19 condition of funding in this document are England only; they are published by the Department for Education for apprenticeships funded in England.[^s3][^s22] The UCAS tariff is UK-wide, and UCAS's tables include Scottish Highers, Advanced Highers, and Welsh and Northern Irish qualifications.[^s16]

GOV.UK's list of qualification levels covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland uses the separate SCQF framework.[^s12] For apprenticeships at Level 3 and above in England, a Scottish English or maths qualification must be at least SCQF Level 5 (National 5 at grade C) to count as Level 2.[^s7][^s8] Employers recruiting in Scotland publish separate Scottish requirements; BAE Systems, for example, asks for National 5s and Highers rather than GCSEs and A levels.[^s23]

> The English and maths rules for Scottish Modern and Graduate Apprenticeships, Welsh apprenticeships and Northern Irish apprenticeships were not researched in this session. What would settle it: apprenticeships.scot, Careers Wales and nidirect apprenticeship pages.
