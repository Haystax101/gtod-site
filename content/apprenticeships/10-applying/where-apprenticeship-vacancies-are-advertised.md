## In one line

No single website lists every UK apprenticeship, so use the official vacancy site for your nation (GOV.UK in England, apprenticeships.scot, the Welsh Government service, JobApplyNI) plus the career pages of the employers you actually want, and treat aggregators as a top-up.[^s1][^s3]

## Key facts

- GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship covers England only; the GOV.UK page sends Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish users to apprenticeships.scot, careerswales.gov.wales and nidirect.gov.uk instead.[^s1]
- On 3 September 2026 the Find an apprenticeship search showed 3,678 live vacancies across England, sortable by new, closing soon, wage or start date, and filterable by level (Foundation, Level 2 to Level 7), job category and Disability Confident employers.[^s2]
- To apply on Find an apprenticeship you need an account, created through GOV.UK One Login; the account lets you save vacancies, set up alerts for new roles matching your interests and track applications, but you can search without one.[^s3]
- Employers are not forced to use Find an apprenticeship. DfE guidance says an employer's vacancy is advertised there if the employer creates it through the "recruit an apprentice" tool, and also lists open days, social media and careers events as routes; the 2025 to 2026 funding rules we checked contain no rule requiring vacancies to be advertised on the site.[^s30][^s31]
- UCAS started showing apprenticeship vacancies in October 2023 using a feed from Find an apprenticeship, with employers also able to post directly on UCAS.[^s6][^s9] On 3 September 2026 the UCAS apprenticeship search returned 3,227 results, with filters for England, Scotland and Northern Ireland and for apprenticeship or graduate roles.[^s8]
- Scotland: apprenticeships.scot is run by Skills Development Scotland and lists Foundation, Modern and Graduate Apprenticeship vacancies; no live count is shown on the site.[^s10][^s13]
- Wales: the Welsh Government runs a separate Find an Apprenticeship service at findanapprenticeship.service.gov.wales for people aged 16 or over, with filters by sector (24 options) and level (Foundation Level 2, Level 3, Level 4 to 5, Level 6).[^s16][^s17]
- Northern Ireland: nidirect sends you to JobApplyNI, run by the Department for Communities; on 3 September 2026 JobApplyNI showed 1,687 total opportunities but only 2 tagged in its Apprenticeships sector, and nidirect says to also contact training providers and employers directly.[^s20][^s21]
- Indeed showed 7,940 results for "apprenticeship" on 3 September 2026, but the first page mixed in apprenticeship assessor, coach and trainer jobs from training companies, and Indeed has no dedicated apprenticeship job-type filter.[^s34]
- Schools in England must give pupils in years 8 to 13 at least six encounters with providers of technical education or apprenticeships under section 42B of the Education Act 1997, as amended by the Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022, and must publish a provider access policy statement.[^s32]

## Is there one website that lists every apprenticeship?

No. The Department for Education's own advice is to search Find an apprenticeship, which it says is used by thousands of employers, and separately to visit the website of any employer you already want to work for, because some employers advertise only on their own sites.[^s3][^s4]

Careers Wales gives the same message for Wales: use its apprenticeship search, but also check employer websites, general job sites, employer open days and personal contacts.[^s15] nidirect says the same for Northern Ireland: search JobApplyNI, but also contact training providers, employers, the Careers Service or a Jobs and Benefits office.[^s20]

**GTOD take:** Treat this as a rule. If you only use one site, you will miss vacancies. Pick the official site for your nation, add the career pages of five to ten employers you care about, and add one aggregator for alerts.

## What is GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship and what does it not list?

Find an apprenticeship is the government's free vacancy site for England. GOV.UK says you must be 16 or over and not in full-time or part-time education to start an apprenticeship, and that earlier qualifications, including a degree, do not stop you applying.[^s1] The GOV.UK page was last updated on 2 September 2026.[^s1]

On 3 September 2026 the search showed 3,678 results across England.[^s2] The count changes daily; a second load a few minutes later showed 3,674.[^s2] You can filter by distance from a postcode, by apprenticeship type (foundation, or Level 2 to Level 7), by 15 job categories and by Disability Confident employers, and sort by newest, closing soon, wage or start date.[^s2]

What it does not list:

- Anything in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland. GOV.UK sends those users to apprenticeships.scot, careerswales.gov.wales and nidirect.gov.uk.[^s1]
- Vacancies an employer chose not to post there. DfE's employer guidance says a vacancy is advertised on Find an apprenticeship when the employer (or the training provider managing recruitment for them) creates it through the "recruit an apprentice" tool, and it lists open days, social media and careers events as other recruitment routes.[^s30] We found no rule in the 2025 to 2026 apprenticeship funding rules that forces employers or providers to advertise on the site.[^s31]
- Closed or filled roles. A guide to the site written by Amazing Apprenticeships says only current, live vacancies are displayed, so a role that has closed disappears rather than showing as closed.[^s29]

**GTOD take:** The 3,678 figure on 3 September 2026 is a snapshot on a single day at the start of the autumn recruiting season, so do not read it as the size of the market. Big employers tend to open degree apprenticeship applications in waves through the autumn, and Find an apprenticeship only shows what is live that day. Check it weekly rather than once.

**GTOD take:** Some large employers that run their own application systems list their apprenticeships on Find an apprenticeship with a link out to their own site, and some do not list there at all. We could not find an official statement of how many do either, so treat the employer's own careers page as the source of truth for any named company.

## How do alerts and accounts work on Find an apprenticeship?

You can search Find an apprenticeship without an account, but you need one to apply.[^s3] The account is created through GOV.UK One Login, using a personal email address, and you choose a security code by text message or authenticator app.[^s3][^s29]

Once logged in you can save vacancies, set up alerts for new apprenticeships that match your interests, and manage all your applications in one place.[^s3] Amazing Apprenticeships' guide (dated September 2024) says the site sends feedback if an application is unsuccessful and recommends managing alerts so you hear when new vacancies matching your criteria are added.[^s29]

There is a conflict on age. GOV.UK says you must be 16 or over to start an apprenticeship.[^s1] Amazing Apprenticeships' search-and-apply page says an account on Find an apprenticeship can be created from age 14.[^s28] These can both be true (you can browse and set alerts at 14 but start a job at 16), but the official GOV.UK page does not state an account age, so we would trust GOV.UK on eligibility and treat the 14+ figure as unconfirmed.[^s1][^s28]

## Does UCAS list the same apprenticeships as GOV.UK?

Mostly, but not exactly. In February 2023 DfE confirmed UCAS would carry apprenticeship vacancies, with listings integrated from Find an apprenticeship, and with employers also able to advertise directly on UCAS, whose data would then be shared back to Find an apprenticeship.[^s9] UCAS switched the vacancies on in October 2023; FE Week reported that listings came from the government site with real-time updates showing employer, training provider, level, entry requirements, closing date and salary, and that students clicked through to Find an apprenticeship to apply, with direct applications through UCAS planned for autumn 2024.[^s6]

On 3 September 2026 the UCAS search returned 3,227 results, compared with 3,678 on Find an apprenticeship the same day.[^s8][^s2] UCAS lets you filter by nation of qualification (England, Northern Ireland, Scotland), by level (Intermediate, Advanced, Higher, Degree, plus Scottish Modern and Technical Apprenticeships), by industry, by region, and by vacancy type (apprenticeship or graduate job).[^s8] UCAS also offers job alerts and a shortlist when you sign up.[^s7]

Weaknesses:

- UCAS mixes apprenticeships with graduate jobs in the same search, so you need the vacancy type filter to keep them apart.[^s8]
- The UCAS count on 3 September 2026 was lower than the GOV.UK count, even though UCAS also includes Scottish and Northern Irish roles, so UCAS is not a superset of Find an apprenticeship.[^s8][^s2]
- We could not confirm from UCAS's own pages whether you can now apply inside UCAS or whether every listing still links out; UCAS's apprenticeships hub page does not say.[^s5]

**GTOD take:** Use UCAS if you are also considering university, because it puts both options side by side and its alerts are easy. Do not rely on it alone for apprenticeships; check Find an apprenticeship too.

## Where do I find apprenticeships in Scotland?

The official site is apprenticeships.scot, run by Skills Development Scotland, which covers Foundation Apprenticeships (done while at school), Modern Apprenticeships (paid jobs from age 16) and Graduate Apprenticeships (paid jobs with a degree).[^s10][^s12] mygov.scot points to the same site.[^s11]

apprenticeships.scot says you can search vacancies near you across more than 100 apprenticeship types, that Graduate Apprenticeship vacancies are listed there and each vacancy explains how to apply, and that My World of Work has application advice; a helpline is on 0800 917 8000.[^s12][^s13]

Weaknesses:

- The site did not display a total count of live vacancies on the pages we opened on 3 September 2026, so we cannot say how many roles it carries.[^s10][^s12]
- The pages we opened did not describe email alerts or saved searches for applicants, only an employer dashboard for posting vacancies.[^s10]
- Scottish employers can also appear on UCAS, which has a Scotland filter and Modern Apprenticeship level filter, but UCAS is not the official Scottish route.[^s8]

## Where do I find apprenticeships in Wales?

Wales has two linked official sources. GOV.WALES sends you to the Welsh Government's own Find an Apprenticeship service at findanapprenticeship.service.gov.wales, which is for people aged 16 or over.[^s16] That service is Welsh Government funded and bilingual, and lets you filter by job name, location, one of 24 sectors, level (Foundation Level 2, Level 3, Level 4 to 5, Level 6) and Disability Confident employers.[^s17]

Careers Wales, a Welsh Government service, links to an apprenticeship search and keeps a directory of larger employers who offer apprenticeships in Wales, organised into 14 sectors plus an A to Z and a degree apprenticeship list; its helpline is 0800 028 4844.[^s14][^s18] Careers Wales' own advice is to use the search and also check employer websites, general job sites, employer events and personal contacts.[^s15]

Weaknesses:

- The Welsh service's home page describes accounts for employers to post vacancies but no applicant alert feature.[^s17]
- We could not load the Welsh search results page, so we cannot give a live vacancy count for Wales.[^s17]
- The Welsh service is separate from the England one, so a Welsh applicant looking at GOV.UK will see only English vacancies.[^s1][^s16]

## Where do I find apprenticeships in Northern Ireland?

nidirect is the official starting point. Its apprenticeships campaign page (last updated 3 July 2026) says to search online for apprenticeship opportunities, including Higher Level Apprenticeships, and that you need a job or an employer willing to take you on before you can start.[^s19] The search service page sends you to JobApplyNI and also says you can find apprenticeships by contacting training providers, employers, the Careers Service or a Jobs and Benefits office.[^s20]

JobApplyNI is run by the Department for Communities. On 3 September 2026 it showed 1,687 total opportunities, of which only 2 were in its Apprenticeships sector.[^s21] Registering lets you save search preferences and receive vacancy information by email.[^s21]

For Higher Level Apprenticeships (Level 4 to Level 7), nidirect (updated 20 August 2026) says to contact the relevant further education college or university directly: the six FE colleges, Ulster University, Queen's University Belfast, the Open University and CAFRE.[^s22]

Weaknesses:

- JobApplyNI is a general jobs site, and the count of 2 apprenticeship-tagged roles on 3 September 2026 suggests most Northern Irish apprenticeships are not advertised there.[^s21]
- Higher Level Apprenticeship recruitment runs through colleges and universities rather than one vacancy site.[^s22]

**GTOD take:** In Northern Ireland the vacancy site is the weakest of the four nations. Go to the colleges, universities and employers directly, and use the UCAS Northern Ireland filter as a second check.[^s8]

## Should I check employer career sites directly?

Yes. DfE says that if there is an employer you want to work for, visit their website to see whether they are recruiting apprentices, and its 2024 guidance says some employers advertise vacancies directly on their own websites.[^s3][^s4] UCAS gives the same advice and suggests meeting employers at UCAS exhibitions' apprenticeship zones.[^s7] Careers Wales and nidirect say the same for Wales and Northern Ireland.[^s15][^s20]

DfE's employer guidance also shows why: employers choose whether to create a vacancy through the government tool, can hand recruitment to their training provider, and can recruit through open days, social media and careers events instead.[^s30]

**GTOD take:** For degree apprenticeships at large companies, the employer's careers page is the primary source and everything else is a mirror. Bookmark the early-careers or apprenticeships page of each target employer and check it from September onwards, because those pages also carry the opening dates that vacancy sites do not show until the role is live.

## What about RateMyApprenticeship (now Higherin), GetMyFirstJob and Not Going To Uni?

Higherin is the new name for RateMyApprenticeship and RateMyPlacement; the rebrand happened in August 2025 after 18 years under the old names.[^s25] It lets students search and apply for placements, internships, apprenticeships and graduate jobs, read reviews written by apprentices, and register for free job alerts with tailored recommendations and deadline reminders.[^s23][^s24] It is free for students; employers use a separate employer portal.[^s25] Its apprenticeship guide covers Level 2 (Intermediate), Level 3 (Advanced), Level 4 and 5 (Higher) and Level 6 and 7 (Degree) apprenticeships.[^s24]

GetMyFirstJob is run by GetMyFirstJob Limited and is "powered by TalentPeople".[^s26] It covers apprenticeships, degree apprenticeships, graduate schemes, work experience and bootcamps, says it works with more than 800 employers, and describes more than 625 different apprenticeship roles from Level 2 to Level 7.[^s26][^s27] It has a Career Quiz and asks you to register.[^s27]

Not Going To Uni is named by Amazing Apprenticeships as a specialist job site alongside GetMyFirstJob and UCAS.[^s28] We could not open its site on 3 September 2026 (see gap below).

Weaknesses:

- Higherin's pages did not show a total number of live apprenticeship vacancies; its apprenticeships page displayed six example roles.[^s24] Its featured employers are large firms (Deloitte, EY, PwC, Aldi, GKN Aerospace), which reflects who advertises there.[^s25][^s24]
- GetMyFirstJob's pages did not display a live vacancy count either; the "625 roles" figure describes apprenticeship types, not open jobs.[^s26][^s27]
- These are commercial sites where employers use a paid portal, so they can only show the employers who choose to advertise with them.[^s25]

**GTOD take:** Higherin is most useful for its reviews of specific employers' programmes and for deadline reminders. GetMyFirstJob tends to carry more small and medium employers through training providers. Neither replaces the official site, and both skew towards whichever employers are paying that year.

## Are Indeed and LinkedIn any good for apprenticeships?

Indeed showed 7,940 results for "apprenticeship" in the UK on 3 September 2026.[^s34] But the first page included an Apprenticeship Coach, an Assessor, an Apprenticeship Assessor and a Chef Apprenticeship Trainer, all jobs at training companies, not apprenticeships.[^s34] Indeed's filters are pay, remote, job type, education level, industry and date posted; there is no apprenticeship-specific filter.[^s34]

LinkedIn blocked our fetch, so we cannot describe its apprenticeship listings or counts from its own pages (see gap below).

**GTOD take:** Indeed's headline number overstates apprenticeship vacancies because keyword matching pulls in trainer, assessor and "apprentice-level" jobs with no formal training. If you use Indeed, read each listing for a named training provider and an apprenticeship standard or level; if neither is there, it may not be a real apprenticeship. LinkedIn is worth having for following employers' early-careers accounts, but it is not a vacancy source you can rely on for 16 to 18 year olds.

## What do Amazing Apprenticeships and Bright Network do?

Amazing Apprenticeships does not host vacancies. Its search-and-apply page (last modified 21 April 2026) points to Find an apprenticeship, which it describes as the government portal with free access to a large number of English vacancies at all levels, and to GetMyFirstJob, Not Going To Uni, UCAS, employer websites, social media and its own free monthly newsletter.[^s28] The same page refers to a listing of more than 950 higher and degree apprenticeship vacancies.[^s28]

Bright Network's site could not be opened during this research (see gap below), so we cannot describe its apprenticeship coverage from its own pages.

**GTOD take:** Amazing Apprenticeships' higher and degree listing and its newsletter are a useful way to see big-employer opening dates in one place, but it is a signpost, not a search engine.

## Do training providers advertise apprenticeships themselves?

Yes. DfE's employer guidance says an employer can ask its training provider to manage recruitment on its behalf.[^s30] nidirect tells Northern Irish applicants to contact training providers directly, and for Higher Level Apprenticeships to contact the college or university that delivers the programme.[^s20][^s22] The Welsh service says to contact training providers to check whether an apprenticeship is delivered in Welsh.[^s17]

**GTOD take:** When a provider manages recruitment, the vacancy is usually posted on Find an apprenticeship under the provider's name and often on the provider's own job board too. Searching Find an apprenticeship by the provider's name can show every employer they are recruiting for. We could not open provider job boards in this session, so we have no data on how many vacancies appear only on a provider's site.

## Does my school have to tell me about apprenticeships?

In England, yes. Section 42B of the Education Act 1997, as amended by the Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022, requires maintained schools and academies to give providers of technical education and apprenticeships access to pupils.[^s32] The governing body must make sure pupils in years 8 to 13 get at least six encounters with such providers: two in year 8 or early year 9, two in year 10 or early year 11 (each by 28 February), and two in year 12 or early year 13 which pupils can choose to attend.[^s32]

Schools must also publish a provider access policy statement setting out how providers can get access, on what grounds access can be refused, what premises are available and how the six encounters are met.[^s32] The statutory careers guidance was last updated on 23 June 2026, and its 5 January 2023 update reflected the change to the provider access legislation.[^s33]

The guidance itself refers to Find an apprenticeship as the government service where young people can search and apply, but does not require schools to circulate vacancies.[^s32]

**GTOD take:** The law gives you encounters with providers, not a vacancy list. If your school has not arranged apprenticeship provider sessions, ask the careers leader for the provider access policy statement; it is a public document and the six encounters are a legal duty.

## Are social media channels worth following?

DfE's employer guidance names social media as one of the routes employers use to recruit apprentices, alongside open days and careers events, and Amazing Apprenticeships lists social media among its recommended places to find vacancies.[^s30][^s28]

**GTOD take:** Social media is where you hear that applications have opened, not where you apply. Follow the early-careers accounts of your target employers and the official Find an apprenticeship, UCAS and apprenticeships.scot accounts, then go to the employer's page to apply. Do not treat a post from an unverified account as a vacancy.

## What is the best way to combine these sources?

**GTOD take:** A workable weekly routine for someone in England is: one saved alert on Find an apprenticeship, one alert on UCAS if you are also considering university, one alert on Higherin or GetMyFirstJob, and a direct check of each target employer's careers page.[^s3][^s7][^s23] In Scotland swap GOV.UK for apprenticeships.scot, in Wales use the Welsh Government service plus the Careers Wales employer directory, and in Northern Ireland go to colleges, universities and employers first.[^s12][^s17][^s22]

> [!GAP]
> Live vacancy counts for apprenticeships.scot and the Welsh Government Find an Apprenticeship service. Neither site showed a total on the pages we opened, and the Welsh search results page returned a 403 error on 3 September 2026. Opening the search results pages in a browser would settle it.

> [!GAP]
> Not Going To Uni (notgoingtouni.co.uk) returned a 403 error on 3 September 2026, so we could not confirm who runs it, what it lists or its vacancy count. LinkedIn and Bright Network blocked automated fetches (robots.txt), so their coverage and counts are unverified. Opening these in a browser would settle it.

> [!GAP]
> How many large employers list their degree apprenticeships on Find an apprenticeship rather than only on their own careers pages. We found no official figure; DfE's guidance only confirms that posting on the site depends on the employer or provider creating the vacancy. A sample check of, say, 20 named employers against Find an apprenticeship in October 2026 would settle it.

> [!GAP]
> Whether UCAS now lets you apply for apprenticeships inside UCAS (planned for autumn 2024 according to FE Week in October 2023) or still links out to Find an apprenticeship and employer sites. UCAS's own pages did not say. Opening a live UCAS vacancy and checking its apply button would settle it.

> [!GAP]
> The 950+ higher and degree vacancy figure on the Amazing Apprenticeships search-and-apply page is stated without a date, and we could not confirm which listing it refers to. Training-provider job boards were not opened in this session, so we have no data on vacancies that appear only there.

> [!GAP]
> The Find an apprenticeship count of 3,678 on 3 September 2026 is lower than we expected for the official England site. We report it as displayed; the search page may have applied a default filter we could not see. A repeat check on the results page, with the level and category filters visibly cleared, would settle it.
