## In one line

A rejection from a UK apprenticeship is rarely permanent: most big employers let you apply again in the next intake (PwC, Deloitte, Barclays, HSBC, Fast Stream) or after a short wait (EY: three months for apprentices), feedback is a courtesy not a legal right, and Clearing, Level 3 apprenticeships, T Levels, HTQs and foundation years all keep the degree apprenticeship route open.[^s1][^s6][^s12][^s8][^s10][^s17][^s22]

## Key facts

- PwC UK accepts one Early Careers application per intake year; if you are unsuccessful or withdraw from the 2027 process you must wait for the programmes starting in 2028.[^s1][^s2]
- Deloitte UK accepts one application per programme per academic year (September to August) and says unsuccessful candidates are welcome to reapply the following year.[^s3][^s4]
- EY UK: as at September 2026, unsuccessful apprentice or Career Starters applicants must wait three months from the rejection date before applying again (six months for graduate and undergraduate roles), and must use a new email address the second time.[^s6]
- Barclays accepts one application per candidate per year for its graduate and internship programmes and says unsuccessful candidates can reapply once the next recruitment season begins.[^s12]
- HSBC says you can apply again if you applied in a previous recruitment cycle and year, and promises a final decision plus developmental feedback by email at each stage.[^s8]
- The Civil Service Fast Stream allows one application per recruitment campaign, says success after two or more attempts is not unusual, and gives feedback after the online tests, the work-based scenarios and the assessment centre.[^s10]
- Accenture UK says rejected candidates are welcome to reapply and that each role needs a new application; IBM's careers FAQ says you may apply to as many roles as you like, one at a time.[^s14][^s15]
- Under UK GDPR you can make a subject access request, verbally or in writing, and the employer must reply within one month (extendable by up to two months for complex requests), usually free.[^s16]
- The ICO's recruitment guidance says candidates are entitled to a copy of their information "including any outcomes or results relating to them at all stages of the recruitment process".[^s17]
- UCAS Clearing for 2026 entry runs from 2 July to 19 October 2026 and is open to anyone applying after 30 June, with no offers, who missed their conditions, or who declined their firm place.[^s22][^s23]
- The equal-consideration deadline for most undergraduate courses for 2027 entry is 13 January 2027 at 18:00 UK time.[^s23]
- In England in 2024/25 there were 353,500 apprenticeship starts, of which 74,990 (21.2%) were by under-19s and 51.3% were by people aged 25 and over.[^s25]

## Can I reapply to the same employer after being rejected?

Almost always yes, but the rule is set by each employer, not by law. As at September 2026 the published rules from employers' own early-careers pages are below. If an employer is not listed, its FAQ did not state a rule when GTOD checked, so email its early-careers team and ask.[^s1][^s3][^s6]

**PwC UK.** One application at a time and one per intake year. If you are unsuccessful or withdraw from the 2027 programmes, you must wait for the 2028 intake. Withdrawing before the online assessment still counts: you wait until the next intake year.[^s1][^s2]

**Deloitte UK.** One application per programme per academic year, which Deloitte defines as September to August. Unsuccessful candidates are told they are welcome to reapply the following year.[^s3][^s4]

**EY UK.** If you are unsuccessful at any assessment stage, you wait six months from the rejection date for graduate or undergraduate roles, or three months for apprentice or Career Starters roles. You must declare on your application that it is your only one in a six-month period, and must use a new email address when you reapply.[^s6] Note a conflict: an EY student recruitment adviser answering on EY's own Q&A platform in January 2025 said the wait after failing any stage is six months, with no mention of the shorter apprentice period.[^s30] GTOD trusts the official FAQ, which is more recent and specific, but an apprentice applicant should confirm the three-month rule with EY before relying on it.

**KPMG UK.** Neither the apprentice application-process page nor the FAQ page states a reapplication rule or waiting period.[^s5][^s31]

**Barclays.** One application per candidate per year for graduate and internship programmes; if you were unsuccessful this season you can reapply when the next season opens. Only the first of multiple applications is considered. The page names graduate and internship programmes, not apprenticeships specifically.[^s12]

**HSBC.** You can apply again if your previous application was in a previous recruitment cycle and year. You cannot apply to more than one programme at once.[^s8]

**NatWest Group.** If you apply to more than one programme, only the first application goes forward, and you can apply to only one location. The FAQ does not state a waiting period after rejection.[^s9]

**Lloyds Banking Group.** The apprenticeships FAQ does not state a reapplication rule; it directs other questions to the recruitment team.[^s29]

**Bank of England.** One application per candidate; only your first application is accepted. No stated waiting period after rejection.[^s13]

**Civil Service Fast Stream.** One application per recruitment campaign; a second application in the same campaign gets both withdrawn. You can apply again in a later year, each application is treated separately, and the FAQ says it is not unusual to succeed on a second or later attempt.[^s10]

**Accenture UK.** Rejected candidates are welcome to reapply or look for a better-fitting role. Each role needs a new application. No waiting period is stated.[^s14]

**IBM.** IBM's global careers FAQ says you are free to apply to as many roles as you like, one at a time, and may withdraw and reapply while a position is still open. No UK-specific apprenticeship rule was found.[^s15]

**BAE Systems.** The FAQ says you may sometimes apply for more than one role, depending on the country, the role type, "reapplication wait times" and the timing of recruitment cycles, but does not state what those wait times are.[^s7]

**GCHQ.** The published application-process page does not state a reapplication rule or waiting period.[^s32]

> [!GAP]
> Rolls-Royce, JLR, Airbus and Amazon UK could not be verified. Rolls-Royce's FAQ answers load only in the browser, JLR's careers site blocks automated access, and the Airbus and Amazon UK apprenticeship FAQ pages could not be opened. Their reapplication rules, if any, should be checked by hand on their early-careers pages before Charge states them.

## Does failing the online test mean I am blocked?

It depends on the employer, and most treat a failed test the same as any other rejection. PwC counts an unsuccessful outcome at any stage, including the online assessment, as using up your one application for that intake year.[^s1][^s2]

EY starts its waiting clock (three months for apprentices, six for graduates) from the date you were rejected at any assessment stage, so a failed online test on 1 October would mean an apprentice could apply again from 1 January.[^s6]

The Fast Stream sends feedback whether you pass or fail the online tests, and lets you apply again in the next campaign.[^s10]

**GTOD take:** No employer GTOD checked publishes a separate, longer ban for failing a test. If a recruiter tells you otherwise, ask them to point to the written rule.

## Do employers give feedback after rejecting you?

Some do, at specific stages. Others say nothing. As at September 2026:

- **HSBC**: a final decision and developmental feedback by email at each stage of the process.[^s8]
- **Civil Service Fast Stream**: feedback after the online tests and after the work-based scenarios, whether you pass or fail; written feedback after the assessment centre; no individual feedback at the anonymised "additional information" sift.[^s10]
- **Civil Service (wider)**: the careers site encourages you to ask for feedback whatever the outcome.[^s11]
- **KPMG UK**: a personalised skills feedback report after the online assessment, and the option to request feedback if you are unsuccessful at the final Launch Pad stage.[^s5]
- **Deloitte UK**: a strengths feedback report after the immersive online assessment and after the job simulation (not the outcome itself), and feedback after the final-stage assessment.[^s4]
- **PwC UK**: the careers team contacts you with feedback after the business assessment, and recaps feedback from earlier stages during the role-matching call.[^s2]

GOV.UK's own apprenticeship guidance says you can ask for feedback if you are not selected for interview or for the apprenticeship, and can complain if you think you were discriminated against or treated unfairly.[^s20]

Acas tells employers to say at interview when and how they will give the outcome and what sort of feedback to expect.[^s19]

**GTOD take:** Ask for feedback within a few days of the rejection email, in two or three polite sentences, naming the stage you reached. You are more likely to get a real answer after an interview or assessment centre than after an automated test.

## Do I have a legal right to feedback or my interview notes?

There is no general legal right to feedback from a private employer. Acas guidance treats telling candidates what feedback to expect as good practice, not a legal duty, and GOV.UK says you "can ask" for feedback rather than that you are owed it.[^s19][^s20]

What you do have is a right of access to your personal data under UK GDPR. The ICO says you can ask any organisation whether it holds your personal information and request copies, and this is called a subject access request (SAR).[^s18]

The ICO's recruitment guidance is explicit that candidates are entitled to a copy of their information, including outcomes or results relating to them at all stages of the recruitment process, subject to exemptions such as confidential references.[^s17]

A SAR can be made verbally or in writing, you do not need to quote the law or use a form, the employer must respond within one month, and it can extend by up to two further months only if the request is complex. In most cases no fee can be charged.[^s16]

**GTOD take:** A SAR is a heavy tool. Use it if you suspect something went wrong (a scoring error, a lost application, possible discrimination), not as a routine way to get tips. Ask for feedback first. If you do send a SAR, email the employer's data protection or HR contact, give your full name, the role and the dates, and ask for your assessment scores, interviewer notes and the reasons recorded for the decision.

> [!GAP]
> GTOD did not open the ICO chapter on how long employers may keep recruitment records, so the window in which a SAR will still find your interview notes is unverified. The ICO recruitment guidance refers to a "Keeping recruitment records" chapter that would settle it.

## I have no apprenticeship by results day. What are my options?

You have more routes than it feels like on the day. As at September 2026 the verified options are below.

**Keep applying for apprenticeships.** Vacancies are listed year-round on the GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship service, and you apply by searching, creating an account and submitting an application.[^s20][^s28] Many large employers run one intake a year, but the national service is not tied to a September start.

**UCAS Clearing.** For 2026 entry, Clearing runs from 2 July to 19 October 2026. You can use it if you are applying after 30 June for the first time, got no offers, missed the conditions of your offers, or declined your firm place. A first-time Clearing applicant must register and submit an application before adding a Clearing choice.[^s22][^s23]

**Apply to university next year.** The equal-consideration deadline for most undergraduate courses for 2027 entry is 13 January 2027 at 18:00 UK time; 30 June 2027 is the last date before applications go straight into Clearing, which opens on 2 July 2027 and closes on 18 October 2027.[^s23]

**Gap year.** UCAS describes gap years as a chance to earn money and gain skills through paid employment, and to tailor the year to the subject you plan to study if you have deferred or intend to apply.[^s24]

**GTOD take:** A gap year spent in a relevant paid job, plus a second round of applications in the autumn, is a normal and respectable route into a degree apprenticeship. Employers read "worked a year in a related role" as a plus, not a gap.

## Can I do a Level 3 apprenticeship first and move up later?

Yes. Apprenticeships run at Level 2 (equivalent to GCSE), Level 3 (equivalent to A level) and Levels 4 to 7 (foundation degree and above). To start one you must be 16 or over, live in England and not be in full-time education.[^s21]

Skills for Careers says that when you finish an apprenticeship you could do another at a higher level or in a different industry.[^s28]

**GTOD take:** A Level 3 apprenticeship with the same employer, or in the same sector, is one of the most reliable ways into a Level 6 degree apprenticeship. You arrive with a track record, references and a completed standard, and many employers prefer promoting an existing apprentice to hiring externally. Ask the employer at interview whether Level 3 apprentices can progress to the degree programme.

## What about T Levels, HTQs and foundation years?

**T Levels** are two-year courses taken after GCSEs, broadly equivalent in size to three A levels, with an industry placement of at least 315 hours (about 45 days). The government lists skilled employment, an apprenticeship and higher education as progression options.[^s26]

**Higher Technical Qualifications (HTQs)** are Level 4 and 5 qualifications built on employer-set standards. They take one to two years, can be studied full-time or part-time, are for people aged 18 or over living in England, and lead either straight into work or on to further higher education.[^s27]

**Foundation years.** GTOD could not open a primary page on foundation years in this research session, so no facts are given here.

> [!GAP]
> A UCAS or university-sector page describing foundation years (an extra year before a degree for those without the required grades or subjects) is needed before Charge gives detail on them.

**GTOD take:** For a 17-year-old who missed out on a degree apprenticeship, a T Level in the same sector is worth a look if you are still at school and want a placement on your CV. An HTQ or a Level 4 apprenticeship suits someone who has finished sixth form and wants to stay technical without a three-year degree. Both leave the degree apprenticeship route open next year.

## Can I start an apprenticeship in January or spring instead of September?

Apprenticeship vacancies are published on GOV.UK throughout the year, so start dates are not limited to September.[^s20][^s28]

**GTOD take:** The big graduate-style schemes (Big Four, banks, defence) mostly run one autumn intake, but smaller employers, NHS trusts, councils and many engineering firms recruit whenever a post is funded. Set up alerts on Find an apprenticeship and check the employer's own careers page for "spring intake" or "January start".

> [!GAP]
> GTOD did not find a primary source that quantifies how many apprenticeship starts happen outside August to October, or which named employers run January or spring intakes. The DfE apprenticeship statistics publish starts by month and would settle the first point.

## How competitive are apprenticeships really?

Only sourced numbers are given here. In England in 2024/25 there were 353,500 apprenticeship starts, up 4.1% on the previous year's 339,580.[^s25]

Under-19s accounted for 74,990 starts, or 21.2% of the total, while over half of all starts (51.3%) were by people aged 25 and over.[^s25]

Starts at Level 4 and above were 39.8% of all starts, their highest volume yet and 15.1% up on 2023/24; Level 2 starts were 18.6% of the total; Level 6 and 7 starts were 60,350, or 17.1% of all starts.[^s25]

The Fast Stream's own FAQ says it is not unusual for applicants to succeed at the second or later attempt, and that each application is judged on its own without knowledge of previous ones.[^s10]

> [!GAP]
> No employer among those checked publishes applications-per-place figures for its apprenticeship programmes on the FAQ pages opened, so no ratio is stated. If Charge needs one, it should come from an employer press release or annual report, dated, not from an aggregator.

**GTOD take:** The under-19 share of starts is small and the higher-level share is growing fast, which means school leavers are competing with older, more experienced candidates for Level 4+ places. That is a reason to apply widely and early, not a reason to give up.

## What should I change before I reapply?

**GTOD take:** Three things, in order. First, get the reason: use any feedback the employer gave, and if you failed an online test, practise that test type until you can pass it comfortably. Second, add evidence: a part-time job, a Level 3 course, a T Level placement or a volunteering role in the same sector gives you new material for the application form. Third, widen the net: apply to several employers in different recruitment cycles rather than one dream firm.

Two sourced points shape the timing. PwC and Deloitte will not look at a second application in the same intake year, so a rejection in October means the next chance is the following autumn.[^s1][^s3] EY's three-month wait for apprentices means a rejection in October could allow a new application in January, using a new email address.[^s6]
