## In one line

An apprenticeship assessment centre is usually a half day of two to four exercises (some mix of interview, group task, presentation, case study, written or practical task) in which trained assessors score you against the employer's published behaviours or values using written indicators and a numeric scale.[^s27][^s10][^s37]

## Key facts

- The Civil Service's Success Profiles framework assesses candidates on up to five elements: Behaviours, Strengths, Ability, Experience and Technical. Not every role uses every element; the job advert says which apply.[^s1]
- The Civil Service defines nine Behaviours (for example Working Together, Delivering at Pace, Communicating and Influencing) and tells candidates to evidence them with STAR-style examples from work, volunteering, hobbies or work experience.[^s3]
- The Civil Service defines strengths as the things you do regularly, do well and that motivate you, and tells candidates not to rehearse strengths answers because assessors want your first response.[^s1][^s2]
- The NHS Values Based Recruitment framework (dated March 2016, still the published framework as at September 2026) requires a structured, face-to-face interview that assesses candidates against the six NHS Constitution values, and requires feedback to unsuccessful candidates on request.[^s10]
- Rolls-Royce publishes its apprenticeship assessment centre scoring scale: assessors rate each behaviour indicator from 1 to 6, where 6 means every positive indicator was seen.[^s27]
- A competency interview asks what you have done ("tell me about a time when"); a strengths interview asks what you enjoy and do naturally well, and assessors watch for energy and spontaneity rather than a polished example.[^s8][^s25]
- STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result, and the National Careers Service recommends it for CVs, application forms and interviews as well as for answering competency questions.[^s26]
- Typical final-stage lengths stated by employers as at September 2026: KPMG Launch Pad half a day in person; HSBC assessment centre about 3 hours, mostly virtual; Rolls-Royce apprenticeship assessment centre half a day in person; Civil Service Fast Stream assessment centre half a day online; Deloitte final stage 70 to 90 minutes in person.[^s12][^s39][^s27][^s23][^s15]
- EY says it reimburses travel to in-person Experience Days up to a set maximum; Lloyds Banking Group says travel expenses are covered for in-person graduate assessment centres; PwC says overseas travel is only reimbursed with prior agreement.[^s33][^s17][^s11]

## What happens at an apprenticeship assessment centre?

An assessment centre (some employers call it an assessment day, Launch Pad or Experience Day) is the final stage of most large-employer apprenticeship recruitment. Prospects describes it as a combination of tasks that test suitability for a job, lasting anything from an afternoon to two days, usually with 6 to 8 candidates in a group.[^s29]

The exercises that come up most often, as described by university careers services and employers, are: a group exercise or discussion, a presentation, a case study, a written exercise, an in-tray or e-tray exercise (prioritising a pile of emails and tasks), a practical or technical task, psychometric tests, and a one-to-one interview.[^s28][^s29][^s37]

Employers' own descriptions of their final stage, as at September 2026:

- **Rolls-Royce (UK apprenticeships):** a half day in person at a Rolls-Royce site. Four parts: an interview, a prepared presentation (5 minutes for Level 3 apprentices, 7 minutes for degree and higher apprentices), a technical exercise based on a case study, and a practical activity that tests how you follow instructions and learn.[^s27]
- **KPMG (apprentices):** Launch Pad, a half-day in-person event mixing assessed and non-assessed activities, with the chance to meet KPMG staff. Offers go out within 2 working days. KPMG's page does not list the individual exercises.[^s12][^s34]
- **HSBC (early careers):** an assessment centre of about 3 hours, mostly virtual, made up of interviews and exercises plus meeting HSBC people.[^s39]
- **Lloyds Banking Group (apprentices):** a final interview or assessment day, online or face to face, which may include a skills-based interview, a team exercise, a group activity with a presentation, and pre-work.[^s18][^s32]
- **Lloyds Banking Group (graduates):** a one-to-one interview, a group exercise and individual assessments based on pre-work, in person or virtual.[^s17]
- **Deloitte (early careers):** a final stage of about 70 to 90 minutes in person at your matched office, made up of a 30 to 40 minute topic discussion based on set preparation and a 50 minute skills and motivation interview.[^s15]
- **EY (apprentices):** an in-person Experience Day of activities and exercises assessing strengths and fit, followed by a separate final interview with senior staff. No phones allowed; bring a calculator and notepad.[^s16]
- **PwC (early careers):** a virtual assessment centre, then a business assessment (an in-person interview with senior staff at your matched office).[^s11]
- **Civil Service Fast Stream (graduate):** a half-day online assessment centre with three exercises: a written advice exercise, a one-to-one stakeholder communication role-play, and a 30 minute personal development conversation.[^s19][^s23]
- **Civil Service Fast Track apprenticeship (2018 guide, historic):** a half day of a 40 minute group exercise with 4 to 6 candidates, a 40 minute written exercise with two admin tasks, and a 45 minute competency interview.[^s31]
- **BAE Systems:** the FAQ says the process varies by role but typically includes an online application, assessments, interviews and sometimes an assessment centre.[^s13]

KPMG says most of its Launch Pad days for a given intake run between October and December, and its advice for every stage is to be yourself, because the assessments are designed to find skills and potential rather than rehearsed answers.[^s14] The National Careers Service says assessment centre tasks are there to show how you work with other people, deal with problems, and communicate and contribute.[^s22]

> [!GAP]
> The Civil Service Fast Track apprenticeship guide is dated 2018 and GTOD could not find a current equivalent for Civil Service apprenticeships; the 2018 exercises are included as an illustration of a public-sector apprenticeship assessment centre, not as the current process. Individual department candidate packs on Civil Service Jobs would settle what a 2026 vacancy uses.

**GTOD take:** the pattern across these employers is stable: expect one interview plus one or two group or individual tasks, in about half a day. The Big Four now front-load most testing into online "job simulation" stages, so the in-person day is shorter than it was a few years ago.

## What do assessors actually score?

Assessors do not score "how you came across" in general. They score named behaviours, competencies or values, one exercise at a time, using written descriptions of what good and poor evidence looks like.[^s8][^s27]

The NHS structured interview guidance explains the mechanism: each criterion (for example Collaboration) has positive indicators such as considering others' perspectives, and negative indicators such as being dismissive of others' views. Interviewers write down what you said and classify it against those indicators.[^s8]

Rolls-Royce publishes the same mechanism for its apprentice assessment centre. Its four behaviours are Put Safety First, Do the Right Thing, Keep It Simple and Make a Difference. For each, assessors rate the evidence on a 1 to 6 scale: 1 to 2 means mostly negative indicators, 3 slightly more positive than negative, 4 a good standard, 5 high quality and 6 very strong evidence with every positive indicator seen. The ratings are combined into an overall score per behaviour.[^s27]

The Civil Service says every Fast Stream exercise is marked by a different assessor, so no single person decides your result, and assessors are trained to mark consistently.[^s20] The NHS framework says assessment centres must use a systematic procedure to record and rate specific behaviours as they occur.[^s10]

Published rating scales are rare. The Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (Scotland's prosecution service, which uses the Civil Service framework) publishes two: application answers are scored 0 to 3 (0 fails, 2 is "effective", 3 "highly effective"), and interview answers are scored 1 to 7 for its Band B roles or 0 to 3 for Band C and above.[^s6]

> [!GAP]
> The GOV.UK Success Profiles pages and the Civil Service Careers site do not publish the numeric scale used to score behaviour answers at interview. The 1 to 7 scale is documented by COPFS for its own roles, but GTOD could not find it on a GOV.UK page. A department's candidate pack would settle it for a specific vacancy.

**GTOD take:** because scoring is indicator-based, the highest-value preparation is reading the employer's published behaviours or values and making sure each example you give hits several positive indicators explicitly, rather than trying to be impressive in general.

## What are Civil Service Success Profiles?

Success Profiles is the recruitment framework used across the UK Civil Service. It assesses candidates on a mix of five elements: Behaviours (the actions that lead to effective performance), Strengths (what you do regularly, do well and find motivating), Ability (aptitude for the role), Experience, and Technical (specific professional skills or qualifications). Each vacancy states which elements it tests.[^s1][^s4]

The nine Civil Service Behaviours are: Seeing the Big Picture, Changing and Improving, Making Effective Decisions, Leadership, Communicating and Influencing, Working Together, Developing Self and Others, Managing a Quality Service, and Delivering at Pace. Each is described at different levels for different grades, from Administrative Assistant up to Director General.[^s3]

Behaviours can be assessed by written examples on the application form, situational judgement tests, personality questionnaires, interview questions, and assessment centre exercises such as presentations, in-tray exercises, role-play and group discussions.[^s3][^s5] GOV.UK explicitly tells candidates to use the STAR method (set the scene, show what you did and how, describe the outcome) and says examples can come from work, volunteering, hobbies or work experience.[^s3][^s4]

Civil Service interviews usually last 45 to 60 minutes and are conducted by a panel, often of three people, who ask about the behaviours and strengths listed in the advert. Assessment centres for Civil Service roles can combine interviews, presentations, written exercises and role-play over a half or full day.[^s37] The GOV.UK candidate overview gives interview length as 30 to 60 minutes; the two official pages differ slightly, and GTOD would treat 30 to 60 minutes as the safe range.[^s4][^s37]

Civil Service apprenticeships are recruited through the same framework: the Civil Service Careers apprenticeships page directs applicants to guidance on Success Profiles, behaviours, CVs, personal statements and assessments, and says apprenticeships run from Level 2 to Level 7.[^s42]

## What is the NHS Values Based Recruitment framework?

Values Based Recruitment (VBR) is the NHS approach to selecting staff and students whose personal values match the six NHS Constitution values: working together for patients, respect and dignity, commitment to quality of care, compassion, improving lives, and everyone counts.[^s7][^s40] The framework was developed after the Francis Inquiry into Mid Staffordshire.[^s7]

The framework document (March 2016) sets six core requirements. The one that matters most for candidates is CR3: every applicant must have an individual, face-to-face structured interview that includes an assessment of values. That interview can be delivered as a Multiple Mini Interview (a circuit of short stations, each with one scenario and one assessor), as part of a wider selection centre, or combined with a situational judgement test.[^s10]

Core requirement CR4 says unsuccessful candidates must be given feedback if they ask for it.[^s10] CR6 says that from 1 April 2015 every university delivering NHS-funded training must assess applicants for NHS values using these requirements, which is why nursing, midwifery and allied health degree apprenticeships routinely include a values interview.[^s10]

The framework rates selection methods on evidence: structured interviews and situational judgement tests are rated high for reliability and validity; assessment centres are rated high for validity because assessors directly observe job-relevant behaviour, but are costly to run; personality tests are rated moderate and are flagged as easy to coach.[^s10]

NHS Employers publishes a behaviour framework giving examples of behaviours that do and do not match each NHS value, which trusts can use as interview scoring criteria, plus a values mapping tool for trusts with their own local values.[^s9][^s38]

> [!GAP]
> GTOD could not open NHS candidate-facing interview guidance (the Health Careers interview tips page blocked automated access), so the candidate advice above is drawn from the employer-facing framework. Individual trusts' apprenticeship candidate packs would give the exact interview format for a given vacancy.

## What is a competency-based interview?

A competency-based (also called behavioural) interview asks you to describe a real past situation in which you showed a named skill or behaviour. The question form is "tell me about a time when". The logic, stated in the NHS structured interview guidance, is that past behaviour is the best available predictor of future behaviour in similar situations.[^s8]

The Civil Service uses the same approach for its Behaviours element: you are asked for specific occasions when you demonstrated a behaviour, and you should prepare examples in advance by reading the job description and the behaviours it lists.[^s37][^s5]

Answers are scored against indicators for that competency. The old Civil Service Fast Track apprenticeship interview, for example, asked around a dozen questions, two or three per competency, with 1 to 2 minutes expected per answer.[^s31] The Rolls-Royce apprentice interview covers motivation, self-management, learning agility, collaboration and safety focus, with questions such as describing a recent thing you learned or how you would respond to seeing a safety rule broken.[^s27]

## What is a strengths-based interview and how is it different?

A strengths-based interview asks what you enjoy, what energises you and what you do naturally well, rather than for a rehearsed example of a skill. The CIPD summarises the difference as: competency interviews assess what you can do; strengths interviews assess what you enjoy doing and have a natural aptitude for.[^s24]

The Civil Service defines a strength as something you perform well (performance), feel motivated by (engagement) and do often (use). Its published list has 33 strengths, including Adaptable, Analytical, Authentic and Catalyst.[^s2] Strengths are assessed through interviews, recorded video interviews, situational strengths tests, simulations and personality questionnaires.[^s2]

The single most important sourced point: the Civil Service tells candidates not to rehearse strengths answers, because assessors are looking for your initial response, and there are no right or wrong answers.[^s2] Its careers site adds that strengths questions want your genuine first response, while behaviour questions want well-structured, considered examples.[^s5]

Example strengths questions from university careers services include "What energises you?", "What makes a good day for you?", "What does good communication look like to you?" and "Talk us through an activity you really enjoy."[^s30][^s25] Assessors watch facial expression, tone and body language, and are looking for spontaneous, active language and visible enthusiasm.[^s25][^s30]

Employers that say they use strengths: EY states that its core strengths framework "plays a significant role during the recruitment process" and that Experience Days assess strengths.[^s16] Deloitte says its assessments look at strengths and behavioural competencies, and sends candidates a strengths-based feedback report.[^s15] Lloyds Banking Group's apprenticeship FAQ lists a skills-based interview and gives a contact address at cappfinity.com, a strengths assessment provider.[^s32] A University of Sheffield guide names Aviva, Standard Chartered, EY, Royal Mail, BAE Systems, Unilever, Cisco and Reckitt as strengths-based employers, but that list is undated and secondary.[^s30]

> [!GAP]
> GTOD could not open a Cappfinity page giving the publisher's own definition of a strength (the site returned only header content). The Civil Service and CIPD definitions above are used instead.

**GTOD take:** most large apprenticeship employers now blend the two. Prepare STAR examples for the behaviours in the advert, but for strengths questions answer quickly and honestly. Saying you dislike something is fine; assessors are checking fit, and a forced answer usually shows.

## What is the STAR method?

STAR is a way of structuring an example so an assessor can score it. The National Careers Service defines it as Situation (the situation you had to deal with), Task (what you were asked to do), Action (what you did) and Result (what happened and what you learned). It recommends STAR for CVs, cover letters, application forms and interviews, with examples drawn from work, study, volunteering or personal life, kept short and told naturally rather than read from a script.[^s26]

GOV.UK's Success Profiles guidance uses the same structure for behaviour examples: set the scene, show what you did and how, and give the outcome.[^s3][^s4] The JobHelp site (DWP) describes it as giving real examples of times you did something well.[^s21]

The Government Digital and Data careers site publishes two variants: STARR, which adds Reflect (what you learned and would do differently), and CAR (Challenge, Action, Result), a shorter version for questions about problems you have faced.[^s35]

**GTOD take:** the Action section is where marks are won because indicators describe actions. Keep Situation to two sentences, say "I" not "we", and always finish with a result, even if the outcome was mixed.

## How do video interviews work?

There are two formats. In a live video interview you talk to an interviewer or panel in real time over Teams or Zoom. In a pre-recorded (one-way) interview, questions appear on screen and you record a timed answer to each, at any time before a deadline.[^s36] The Civil Service uses both formats and describes them the same way.[^s37]

Employer examples as at September 2026:

- **KPMG:** a recorded interview of 6 questions presented by an AI avatar called Leah; you get preparation time before each recording; answers are scored by a human recruiter; it takes about 40 minutes.[^s12][^s34]
- **PwC:** a recorded video interview on the SHL platform in which you answer on-screen questions and assessors review the recordings later.[^s11]
- **EY:** recorded video answers form part of the online assessment stage, with a practice round and a time limit per question, done in a quiet place with camera and microphone on.[^s16][^s33]
- **Deloitte and Lloyds Banking Group:** video responses form part of a "job simulation" or "job insight" stage alongside written and ranking questions.[^s15][^s17]
- **HSBC:** the online assessment includes short video responses to workplace scenarios and takes about 40 minutes.[^s39]

Practical guidance from Prospects: check whether you can restart after a technical problem, put the camera at eye level, use a quiet tidy background, dress as you would in person, and test the technology days in advance.[^s36] Network Rail's talent acquisition specialist, quoted by targetjobs, adds: use reliable equipment, have your CV to hand, and allow yourself thinking time.[^s41]

Both PwC and KPMG state that using AI tools or third-party help in assessments without permission is prohibited and can lead to withdrawal of an application.[^s11][^s12]

## What should I wear and what should I bring?

Employers differ, so check your invitation. Rolls-Royce tells apprentice candidates to wear business casual and says school uniform or a college suit is fine; no need to buy anything.[^s27] EY recommends smart casual for Experience Days.[^s16] The 2018 Civil Service Fast Track guide asked for formal dress, as for any interview.[^s31] Prospects says business professional is the safest choice for corporate, finance, law and government employers.[^s29]

Bring what the exercises need. EY asks for a calculator and a notepad and bans phones during the day.[^s16] Lloyds says some online assessments need pen, paper and a calculator.[^s18] Rolls-Royce says to bring notes or handouts for your presentation, because there are no projectors, and props are allowed.[^s27]

For virtual days: reliable wifi, a charged device, a quiet room, and looking at the camera rather than the screen.[^s27][^s28]

**GTOD take:** if the invitation says nothing, smart casual (shirt, trousers or skirt, clean shoes) covers almost every apprenticeship employer. Nobody is scoring your clothes; they are scoring the exercises.

## Will my travel expenses be paid?

Only where the employer says so. As at September 2026:

- **EY:** reimburses travel to in-person Experience Days up to a set maximum; do not book until instructed.[^s33]
- **Lloyds Banking Group:** travel expenses are covered for in-person graduate assessment centres; the apprenticeship FAQ does not mention expenses.[^s17][^s32]
- **KPMG:** says travel expense guidance is sent with Launch Pad instructions, without stating amounts.[^s12]
- **PwC:** travel from outside the UK is reimbursed only with prior agreement and only at economy fares.[^s11]
- **HSBC:** reimbursement varies by location; ask the local recruitment team.[^s39]
- **Civil Service Fast Track (2018 guide, historic):** standard class rail, 26p per mile by car, hotel up to £130 a night in London or £90 in Newcastle, no tube, bus, taxi or parking, claims within 30 days with receipts.[^s31]

> [!GAP]
> Rolls-Royce, BAE Systems, Deloitte and the Civil Service Fast Stream pages opened for this document do not state an expenses policy for candidates. Ask the recruiter before booking travel.

## Will I get feedback if I am unsuccessful?

Often yes, but usually only if you ask. The Civil Service encourages every candidate to ask for feedback whatever the outcome.[^s37] The NHS framework makes feedback on request a core requirement.[^s10] KPMG says unsuccessful Launch Pad candidates may request feedback.[^s12] PwC gives feedback after the business assessment stage.[^s11]

Some employers give automatic feedback. HSBC emails developmental feedback at each stage, with the online assessment result within a couple of days.[^s39] Deloitte sends a strengths feedback report within four weeks of the online assessment and gives unsuccessful candidates guidance for a future attempt.[^s15] EY says it gives personalised strengths feedback throughout.[^s16] KPMG sends a skills feedback report after its online assessment.[^s12]

Reapplication rules matter too. PwC allows one application per year, so an unsuccessful 2027 application means waiting until 2028.[^s11] EY makes rejected apprenticeship applicants wait three months.[^s33]

**GTOD take:** always request feedback in writing within a week. Indicator-based scoring means the feedback can tell you exactly which behaviour scored low, which is the most useful preparation for the next attempt.

## Can I ask for adjustments?

Yes. The Civil Service says reasonable adjustments can be made for any element of assessment, including extra time, large print or support at an assessment centre, requested when you apply.[^s37][^s4] Deloitte lists extended time, alternative formats, BSL interpreters, quiet spaces and assistive technology, and is Disability Confident accredited.[^s15] PwC, KPMG, EY, HSBC and Lloyds all say to flag needs at application or contact the recruitment team, and Lloyds names support for autistic, ADHD and dyslexic candidates.[^s11][^s12][^s16][^s39][^s17]
