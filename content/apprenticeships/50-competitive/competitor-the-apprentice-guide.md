## In one line

The Apprentice Guide is a subscription web app for UK apprenticeship applicants, run by a company incorporated in August 2025 by two directors born in 2006, which sells a video course, AI-marked mock video interviews, CV and cover letter generators, mock assessments and employer fact files behind a 3-day no-card trial whose price is not published on the site.[^s1][^s2][^s6][^s7]

## Key facts

- Operating company: The Apprentice Guide Limited, company number 16631670, private limited company, incorporated 6 August 2025, registered office 85 Great Portland Street, First Floor, London W1W 7LT, status active as at 3 September 2026.[^s6]
- Directors: Ruby Beatrice Lowery (born June 2006) and Samuel Mark Walsh (born February 2006), both appointed 6 August 2025; no other officers and no resignations.[^s7]
- Origin story on the site: a TikTok account started in September 2024, reaching a claimed 20,000 followers within 4 months, with the platform built by January 2025.[^s3]
- Product bundle as at September 2026: a 6-stage video course, AI-marked mock video interviews, an ATS-focused CV builder, a cover letter writer, mock online assessments (numerical, job simulation, personality), corporate fact files and an apprenticeship finder.[^s1]
- Trial: 3 days, advertised as no credit card required and cancel anytime.[^s1][^s2]
- Price: no £ figure, plan name or billing period appears on the pricing, home, register or login pages as fetched on 3 September 2026.[^s2][^s11]
- Terms (last updated 4 September 2025): the paid plan begins automatically if the trial is not cancelled; cancellation takes effect at the end of the billing cycle; all purchases are final with no refunds or partial refunds.[^s4]
- Privacy policy (last updated 5 September 2025): recorded video interviews are collected as personal data; the service is not intended for under-16s; no named processors, retention periods or ICO number.[^s5]
- Marketing stats: 4.9/5 rating, "2,500+ success stories", "92% success rate", "trusted by 20,000+ students"; none is sourced or explained on the site.[^s2][^s3]
- Trustpilot as at 3 September 2026: unclaimed profile, 3 reviews, TrustScore 3.2, two of them 1-star.[^s12]
- The site footer says "Powered by TapLab", a London creator-management agency that lists @theapprenticeguide as one of its educational creators and shares the same registered address.[^s1][^s13]

## Who runs The Apprentice Guide and when was it founded?

The Apprentice Guide Limited (company number 16631670) was incorporated at Companies House on 6 August 2025 as a private limited company. Its SIC codes cover software development, IT consultancy, first-degree higher education and educational support services. First accounts are due by 6 May 2027 and the first confirmation statement was due by 19 August 2026.[^s6]

The two directors are Ruby Beatrice Lowery, born June 2006, and Samuel Mark Walsh, born February 2006. Both are British, UK-resident, and were appointed on incorporation.[^s7] The site's About page introduces them as co-founders: Sam Walsh on an audit apprenticeship at Smith & Williamson and Ruby Lowery in data analytics at The Walt Disney Company. Both did A-levels at grammar school before choosing apprenticeships.[^s3]

The About page dates the TikTok account to September 2024 and says the web platform was built by January 2025, which is seven months before the company was incorporated.[^s3][^s6]

The registered office, 85 Great Portland Street, is also the registered office of Revishaan Limited (company number 14204550), trading as TapLab. TapLab describes itself as an educational talent management agency for creators and lists @theapprenticeguide among its creators. The Apprentice Guide's footer reads "Powered by TapLab".[^s1][^s13]

**GTOD take:** 85 Great Portland Street is a widely used virtual office address, so the shared address on its own proves nothing about ownership, but the "Powered by TapLab" credit suggests TapLab either built or markets the product. Worth knowing because it means the founders are creators first and the software may be white-labelled or agency-built.

Two other domains are unrelated. theapprenticeguide.co.uk names a different team (CEO Desirae Dias) and has placeholder Lorem Ipsum text in its story section.[^s14] theapprenticeguide.com is a US ClickFunnels page selling a $9.95 a month mentorship for apprentice electricians.[^s15]

## What does The Apprentice Guide product include?

As at 3 September 2026 the home page lists seven components.[^s1]

- Video course: described in one place as a "30-video course" and in another as over 40 videos in total, split into 6 stages covering the application process.[^s1]
- AI-marked mock video interviews (see the AI section below).[^s1]
- CV builder aimed at applicant tracking systems.[^s1]
- Cover letter writer.[^s1]
- Mock online assessments: numerical tests, job simulations and personality tests.[^s1]
- Corporate fact files: company profiles with apprenticeship details.[^s1][^s9]
- Apprenticeship finder: described as thousands of live UK apprenticeships, browsable by sector, level and location, with no data source named.[^s10]

The pricing page adds a "Personal Career Coach" line, described as one-to-one sessions with apprenticeship specialists, and "Expert Interview Coaching". Neither appears on the home page and no detail is given on who the coaches are or how sessions are booked.[^s2]

The Events page lists two past online events, both marked free for "Pro members": a panel with three degree-apprenticeship offer holders on 31 March and a mock assessment centre on 1 July, with no year shown. No upcoming events were listed on 3 September 2026.[^s8]

The Resources page lists only the corporate fact files as a concrete resource, with the other categories (guides, interview tips, industry insights) named but not populated.[^s9]

**GTOD take:** The "Pro members" wording on the events page is the only hint at a plan name anywhere on the public site. The mismatch between the home page (7 tools) and the pricing page (adds human coaching) suggests marketing copy that has drifted from the product.

## What does The Apprentice Guide cost?

No price is published. On 3 September 2026 the pricing page, home page, register page and login page contained no £ amount, no plan name other than a generic "Land Your Dream Apprenticeship" card, no billing period and no payment-provider reference in the static HTML.[^s1][^s2][^s11] The terms of service also do not state a price.[^s4]

> [!GAP]
> The subscription price, whether there are monthly and annual options, and what "Pro" includes were not visible without creating an account. The pricing page is a Next.js app and the plan cards appear to be populated after login or client-side. Settling this requires someone to register with a throwaway email and screenshot the plan selector, ideally on the day the trial expires, to see the charged amount.

## How does the free trial work?

The site advertises a 3-day free trial, no credit card required, cancel anytime.[^s1][^s2] Sign-up is by email one-time code or Google account.[^s11]

The terms (last updated 4 September 2025) say the company may offer a free trial with limited access, and that if the user does not cancel before the trial expires, the chosen subscription plan begins automatically. Cancellation stops future charges and takes effect at the end of the current billing cycle.[^s4]

**GTOD take:** "No credit card required" and "plan begins automatically" cannot both be literally true unless card details are taken at some point before day 3. Either the card is collected during onboarding after the marketing page, or the trial simply lapses. A Trustpilot reviewer in March 2026 alleged being charged after cancelling, which is consistent with a card being on file.[^s12] This is the single most useful thing to test with a throwaway account.

## What is the refund policy?

The terms state that all subscription purchases are final and that no refunds or partial refunds are given regardless of usage or when the user cancels. Governing law is stated as UK law with UK courts.[^s4]

**GTOD take:** A blanket no-refund clause on a consumer digital subscription sold to 16 to 19 year olds is unusual and may sit badly with UK consumer cancellation rights for distance contracts, but that is a legal question we have not checked. For GTOD's purposes it is a trust weakness to be aware of, not a claim to repeat to users.

## How does the AI-marked interview practice work?

The home page describes the tool in two sentences: employers use AI software to mark video interviews and The Apprentice Guide has mirrored it, and users get instant personalised feedback on video interview technique.[^s1] One testimonial on the home page references an HSBC video interview.[^s1]

Nothing on the fetched pages states what the user records (number of questions, time limits, whether questions are employer-specific), what is scored (content, structure, delivery, eye contact, speech pace), how the score is presented, or which AI model or vendor is used. The pricing page lists "How does the AI interview simulator work?" as an FAQ heading but the answer text was not present in the fetched HTML.[^s2]

The privacy policy confirms that recorded video interviews are collected as personal data but does not name any third-party AI or video processor, give a retention period or state an ICO registration number.[^s5] The terms include a disclaimer that AI-generated feedback should not be relied on alone and that users must verify information themselves.[^s4]

> [!GAP]
> The scoring rubric, question bank size, vendor and model behind the AI interview marking are not disclosed. Only an account walkthrough would settle it. Note for that test: record which employer question sets are offered and whether feedback is text, a numeric score, or both.

## Are the user numbers and success stats substantiated?

No. The pricing page shows "2,500+ success stories" and a "92% success rate"; the About page says "trusted by 20,000+ students" and separately says the TikTok account reached 20,000 followers within 4 months. The site also shows a 4.9/5 star rating.[^s2][^s3] None of these figures is linked to a review platform, a methodology, or a definition of "success".[^s2]

The "20,000+ students" figure matches the claimed TikTok follower count, and the About page says it took between 3 and 24 applications for the founders to secure apprenticeships.[^s3]

The six home-page testimonials carry generic names and roles (for example "Recent Graduate @ApprenticeGuide", "Career Changer @ApprenticeGuide"), and one is labelled "Premium User".[^s1]

Trustpilot as at 3 September 2026 shows an unclaimed profile with 3 reviews and a TrustScore of 3.2. A 5-star review (2 July 2026) from a Year 12 applicant praised the tools but noted there was no assessment centre practice on the site. A 1-star review (15 July 2026) said fact files, reasoning tests and course content did not load and that support denied any problem. A 1-star review (7 March 2026) said the subscription lacked the advertised tools and that money was taken after cancellation.[^s12]

**GTOD take:** The 4.9/5 on the site and 3.2 on Trustpilot are not the same thing; the site figure has no stated source. A 92% success rate with no denominator is a marketing number, not evidence. Charge should never repeat these figures as fact.

## What is The Apprentice Guide's social following?

The About page claims the TikTok account reached 20,000 followers within 4 months of its September 2024 launch.[^s3] TapLab lists @theapprenticeguide as one of its educational creators.[^s13] No social profile links appear on the site itself.[^s1]

> [!GAP]
> Current follower counts on TikTok, Instagram and LinkedIn as at 3 September 2026 could not be read: those platforms block automated fetching and the site does not link to its profiles. A founder should open the @theapprenticeguide TikTok profile manually and record the follower and likes counts with the date.

## Where do The Apprentice Guide's vacancy listings come from?

The Apprenticeship Finder page says it offers thousands of live UK apprenticeships filterable by sector, level and location, but names no data source and does not say whether listings are pulled from the GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship service or entered manually.[^s10]

> [!GAP]
> Whether the finder uses the GOV.UK vacancy API, a scrape, or hand-entered listings, and how often it refreshes, is unknown. Compare a handful of listings against GOV.UK Find an apprenticeship on the same day to test.

## GTOD take: strengths and weaknesses relative to Charge and the playbook

**GTOD take:** Strengths. The Apprentice Guide has a founder-creator story that lands with 16 to 19 year olds, a claimed TikTok audience built in months, and a bundle that covers the whole funnel (course, CV, cover letter, assessments, video interview, employer files) in one login. The AI video interview tool targets a real pain point (HireVue-style employer screening) that GTOD's Charge chatbot does not address as a product. Events with offer-holder panels and a mock assessment centre mirror GTOD's own community format.

**GTOD take:** Weaknesses. Pricing is hidden, the terms give no refunds and auto-start a paid plan after a 3-day trial, and the only independent reviews include two 1-star complaints about broken features and post-cancellation charges. The company is 13 months old with a first set of accounts not yet filed, the legal documents are Google Docs, and the privacy policy names no processors despite storing video of minors' interviews (the policy excludes under-16s, but the product is marketed at sixth formers). Marketing statistics contradict each other (30 versus 40 videos; 2,500 success stories versus 20,000 students) and are unsourced. Content depth is unverifiable from outside the paywall.

**GTOD take:** Positioning for GTOD. Charge is free, cites sources, and gives dated facts on wages, deadlines and entry requirements, which The Apprentice Guide does not attempt. GTOD's gap is practice tooling: if members ask for interview practice, GTOD's playbook could either point to free alternatives (employer practice tests, university careers services) or partner rather than build. Do not badmouth The Apprentice Guide in Charge; state only the verifiable facts above and let users judge.
