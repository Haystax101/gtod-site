## In one line

As at September 2026, the apprentice rate of the National Minimum Wage is £8.00 an hour (in force since 1 April 2026); it applies to apprentices aged under 19, and to apprentices aged 19 or over who are still in the first 12 months of their apprenticeship, after which 19+ apprentices must get the full rate for their age.[^s1][^s2][^s7]

## Key facts

- As at September 2026, the apprentice rate is £8.00 per hour, effective 1 April 2026, up from £7.55 which applied from 1 April 2025.[^s1][^s6]
- Other rates in force from 1 April 2026: £12.71 (National Living Wage, 21 and over), £10.85 (18 to 20), £8.00 (under 18), and the apprentice rate £8.00.[^s1]
- The apprentice rate applies to an apprentice who is either under 19, or 19 and over but within the first 12 months of the apprenticeship.[^s4][^s7]
- An apprentice moves to the standard age rate only when they are both 19 or over and have completed the first year.[^s2][^s7]
- Employers must pay apprentices for training and study time, including college days and study away from the workplace.[^s2][^s7]
- The apprentice rate is a legal minimum, a floor, and employers can and often do pay more.[^s2][^s7]
- The minimum wage applies across the whole UK: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.[^s4][^s15]
- If underpaid: raise it with the employer, call the Acas helpline on 0300 123 1100, or complain to HMRC, which can be done anonymously.[^s9][^s10]

## How much is the apprentice minimum wage right now?

As at September 2026, the apprentice rate of the National Minimum Wage is £8.00 per hour. This rate took effect on 1 April 2026, following recommendations the Low Pay Commission submitted to government in October 2025, which the government accepted in full and announced at the Autumn Budget in November 2025.[^s1] The previous apprentice rate was £7.55 per hour, which applied from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.[^s6][^s15] The apprentice rate rose from £6.40 to £7.55 on 1 April 2025, and from £7.55 to £8.00 on 1 April 2026, a 45p increase of 6.0%.[^s1][^s6]

The apprentice rate is the same cash figure as the under-18 rate (£8.00) from April 2026, but they are two legally separate rates.[^s1]

## What are the other minimum wage rates for comparison?

From 1 April 2026, the four statutory rates are:[^s1]

- National Living Wage (21 and over): £12.71 per hour, a 50p rise of 4.1%
- 18 to 20 rate: £10.85 per hour, an 85p rise of 8.5%
- Under 18 rate: £8.00 per hour, a 45p rise of 6.0%
- Apprentice rate: £8.00 per hour, a 45p rise of 6.0%

The accommodation offset, the only benefit-in-kind that can count towards minimum wage pay, is £11.10 per day from 1 April 2026, a 44p increase of 4.1%.[^s1] These rates change every year on 1 April.[^s2]

## Who gets the apprentice rate?

You get the apprentice rate of the National Minimum Wage if you are an apprentice and either aged under 19 in any year of the apprenticeship, or aged 19 or over and within the first 12 months of the apprenticeship.[^s4][^s7]

In law, regulation 5 of the National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 says the apprenticeship rate applies to a worker employed under a contract of apprenticeship, an apprenticeship agreement or an approved English apprenticeship agreement, or treated as employed under a contract of apprenticeship, who is within the first 12 months after the commencement of that employment or under 19 years of age.[^s4] The regulation covers apprenticeships across the UK: in England including Trailblazer apprenticeships and apprenticeship standards, Modern Apprenticeships in Scotland, ApprenticeshipsNI in Northern Ireland, and Foundation and Higher Apprenticeships in Wales.[^s4] The first 12 months is measured from the start of the apprenticeship employment.[^s11]

## I'm 18 and start my apprenticeship this month, what happens when I turn 19?

If you start an apprenticeship aged 18, you get the apprentice rate (£8.00 as at September 2026). When you turn 19 partway through your first year, you stay on the apprentice rate, because turning 19 alone does not move you up. HMRC's own guidance gives this exact example: a worker who starts at 18 and a half and turns 19 during the year is still entitled to the apprentice rate for the remainder of the 12 months from the start of the contract of apprenticeship.[^s11]

You only move off the apprentice rate once you are both 19 or over and have completed the first year. At that point you must get at least the rate for your age, £10.85 for 18 to 20 year olds as at September 2026.[^s2][^s11]

Someone who starts at 19 or older gets the apprentice rate for the first 12 months only; after that they move to the rate for their age.[^s11]

## What happens to my pay in year two of my apprenticeship?

It depends on your age at the start of year two:[^s2][^s7]

- If you are still under 19 in year two, the apprentice rate (£8.00) can legally continue for as long as you are under 19, even into year two or beyond.[^s2][^s7]
- If you are 19 or over and have completed your first year, your employer must pay you at least the standard minimum wage for your age: £10.85 if you are 18 to 20, or £12.71 if you are 21 or over, as at September 2026.[^s7]

Acas gives a worked example: "Ali" is 19 on a two-year apprenticeship, paid at least £8.00 in year one; in year two, aged 20, the employer must pay at least £10.85.[^s7] The pay rise to the age rate takes effect from the first pay reference period on or after you complete the first year, so it may not land on the exact anniversary day, but from the next pay period after it.[^s8][^s12] The same timing applies when you have a birthday that moves you into a higher age band, and when the government raises the rates on 1 April: the higher rate starts from the next pay reference period after the change.[^s8][^s12]

Note two traps that catch apprentices out: starting the first year again after changing employer, even within the same apprenticeship, or starting a new apprenticeship agreement with the same employer, resets the "first year" clock, so the apprentice rate can apply again for that first year.[^s7]

## Do I get paid for my college and training days?

Yes. GOV.UK is explicit that an employer must pay an apprentice for time spent training or studying for their apprenticeship.[^s2] Acas adds that this includes learning or training outside normal working hours or away from the usual workplace, for example online learning at home.[^s7] If you also study for English or maths qualifications required by the apprenticeship, you are entitled to paid study time during your normal working hours.[^s2] You are paid at your applicable minimum wage rate for this training time; it is not unpaid.[^s2][^s7]

## Can my employer pay me more than the apprentice rate?

Yes. The apprentice rate is a legal minimum, not a fixed wage. GOV.UK and Acas both state employers can choose to pay more.[^s2][^s7] Many do, especially for higher-level and degree apprenticeships. UCAS notes that apprenticeships pay from minimum wage up to £300 per week, and lists major employers such as Deloitte, BMW and the BBC.[^s17] For context, the Low Pay Commission reports that median pay across all apprenticeships is around £10.86 an hour, well above the £8.00 apprentice-rate floor, because most employers pay more than the legal minimum.[^s16]

**GTOD take:** degree and higher apprenticeships often advertise annual salaries well above the apprentice-rate floor, so always check the actual advertised salary on the vacancy rather than assuming you will be paid £8.00 an hour. Treat any specific salary figure as employer-specific, not a rule.

## What if I'm being paid less than the apprentice minimum wage?

First, check your pay using the National Minimum Wage and Living Wage calculator on GOV.UK.[^s7] If it looks wrong:[^s9][^s10]

1. Raise it with your employer first. You can raise a formal grievance.[^s9]
2. Contact Acas for free, confidential advice on 0300 123 1100, Monday to Friday.[^s9]
3. Complain to HMRC using the online "complain about pay and work rights" form. Complaints can be anonymous, can be made by a third party, and can be made even after you have left the job.[^s9][^s10]

HMRC enforces the minimum wage and can order your employer to pay arrears and a penalty.[^s10] Your employer cannot legally contract out of the minimum wage, even if you signed something agreeing to less.[^s9]

## Is the apprentice rate the same in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

Yes. The National Minimum Wage, including the apprentice rate, is UK-wide statute and not a devolved matter, so the same rates apply in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.[^s15] The National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 explicitly cover apprenticeships in each nation: Trailblazer and English apprenticeships, Modern Apprenticeships in Scotland, ApprenticeshipsNI in Northern Ireland, and Foundation and Higher Apprenticeships in Wales.[^s4] The 2026 amendment regulations state that they extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.[^s6]

## Is the apprentice rate going to change?

Two things are worth knowing, as at September 2026.

April 2027 rates are not yet confirmed. The Low Pay Commission was asked to recommend the rates to apply from April 2027 and to report to government by the end of October 2026; the government then confirms the figures, normally at the autumn Budget, as the April 2026 rates were confirmed at the Autumn Budget in November 2025.[^s1] The LPC has published an indicative estimate that the National Living Wage would need to be in the range £13.02 to £13.34, an increase of 2.4% to 5.0%, with a central estimate of £13.18 (3.7%), to stay at two-thirds of median earnings. The LPC stresses these projected figures are indicative only and could change with the economic data.[^s1] No indicative figure has been given for the April 2027 apprentice rate.[^s1]

Government policy on removing age bands applies to the 18 to 20 rate, not directly to the apprentice rate. The government has committed to removing the discriminatory age bands for adults and moving towards a single adult rate, with the LPC setting out a possible path to extend the National Living Wage to 20 year olds in 2027 and to 18 and 19 year olds in 2028 or 2029, subject to economic conditions.[^s13][^s14] That commitment is about the 18 to 20 rate aligning with the National Living Wage, not the apprentice rate.[^s13][^s14] On apprentices specifically, the LPC has in recent years recommended bringing the apprentice rate into line with the 16 to 17 year old rate, and has examined but not adopted ending the different treatment of apprentices altogether.[^s16]

**GTOD take:** the direction of travel is upward and the gap between the apprentice rate and older-age rates is narrowing, but nothing about the apprentice rate for April 2027 is confirmed yet. Check the GOV.UK minimum wage rates page after the autumn Budget.

> [!GAP]
> The exact April 2027 apprentice rate is not confirmed as at September 2026. There is no confirmed government commitment to abolish or single-rate the apprentice rate specifically; the "single adult rate" policy currently targets the 18 to 20 age band.
