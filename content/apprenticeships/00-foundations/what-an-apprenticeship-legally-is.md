## In one line

In England an apprenticeship is legally a paid job with training: you are an employee on an "apprenticeship agreement", it must last at least 8 months, each standard sets its own minimum training hours, 16 to 18 year olds must still achieve English and maths, and you finish with an independent end-point assessment.

## Key facts

- As at September 2026, an apprentice in England is an employee on a contract of employment called an apprenticeship agreement, not a student. This is set by the Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009.[^s4]
- You must be paid and get the same core employment rights as other staff, including at least 28 days paid holiday a year (5.6 weeks, including bank holidays).[^s11]
- The apprentice minimum wage is £8.00 an hour from 1 April 2026, up from £7.55, set out in the National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Regulations 2026 following the Autumn Budget 2025. It applies if you are under 19, or 19 and over and in the first year of your apprenticeship.[^s9][^s10]
- Once you are 19 or over and past your first year, you must be paid the normal minimum wage for your age: £10.85 an hour for 18 to 20, and £12.71 an hour for 21 and over, from 1 April 2026.[^s10]
- The legal minimum length is 8 months for apprenticeships starting on or after 1 August 2025. It was 12 months before.[^s2]
- Off-the-job training is no longer a flat 20% or 6 hours a week for new starts. Since 1 August 2025 each apprenticeship standard has its own published minimum number of training hours, and no apprenticeship can require fewer than 187 hours.[^s1]
- You normally work at least 30 hours a week. Part-time is allowed but usually takes longer.[^s1][^s7]
- If you start aged 16 to 18, you must still achieve English and maths to finish: at least level 1 on a level 2 apprenticeship, and level 2 on a level 3 or above, unless you already hold an accepted equivalent such as a GCSE grade 4.[^s6][^s7]
- Since 11 February 2025, apprentices aged 19 and over at the start no longer have to achieve English and maths to complete, but this change does not apply to 16 to 18 year olds.[^s6][^s7]
- Every apprenticeship ends with an end-point assessment, an independent test of your skills. You cannot pass the apprenticeship without it.[^s8]
- IfATE closed on 1 June 2025 and Skills England now owns apprenticeship standards, under the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (Transfer of Functions etc) Act 2025.[^s5]
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different systems. Everything here is England only.

## Am I an employee or a student if I do an apprenticeship in England?

You are an employee. In England an apprenticeship is a real, paid job with training built in. You sign an apprenticeship agreement with your employer. Under section A5 of the Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009, an approved English apprenticeship agreement is treated as a contract of service, an ordinary employment contract, and specifically not as a traditional contract of apprenticeship.[^s4]

Why the distinction matters: an old-style contract of apprenticeship gave apprentices extra protection against dismissal and redundancy. A modern apprenticeship agreement under the 2009 Act does not, so you have the same statutory rights as any other employee, no more and no less.[^s4]

Practically, being an employee means you have a job role, a manager, a payslip, and you can be managed and dismissed like any employee if a fair process is followed. It also means the apprenticeship and its funding cannot run without a valid apprenticeship agreement in place.[^s1]

## How much do I get paid and what rights do I have as an apprentice in England?

You must be paid. As at September 2026 the apprentice National Minimum Wage rate is £8.00 an hour, effective from 1 April 2026, up from £7.55. The rise was confirmed at the Autumn Budget 2025 on Low Pay Commission recommendations and made law by the National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Regulations 2026.[^s9][^s10]

The apprentice rate only applies if you are under 19, or 19 or over and in the first year of your apprenticeship. Once you are 19 or over and have completed your first year, you must be paid the standard minimum wage for your age band: from 1 April 2026 that is £10.85 an hour for ages 18 to 20 and £12.71 an hour for 21 and over.[^s10]

As an employee you also get core employment rights, including at least 28 days (5.6 weeks) paid holiday a year including bank holidays, rest breaks, and health and safety protection under the Working Time Regulations 1998.[^s11] Your off-the-job training happens during paid working hours; you should not have to do it in your own time.[^s16]

**GTOD take:** many employers pay well above the £8.00 legal floor, especially for degree and higher apprenticeships. £8.00 is the legal minimum, not the going rate. Always check the actual advertised wage before you apply.

## How long does an apprenticeship in England have to last?

For apprenticeships starting on or after 1 August 2025, the legal minimum is 8 months. This was cut from the previous 12-month minimum by the Apprenticeships (Miscellaneous Provisions) (England) (Amendment) Regulations 2025, which changed the minimum duration of the "practical period" from 12 months to 8 months.[^s2]

The 12-month minimum still applies to anyone who started before 1 August 2025; the change was not backdated.[^s1]

Important: 8 months is a floor, not a target. GOV.UK states apprenticeships can take from 8 months to 6 years to complete, depending on the type and level.[^s17] The 8-month minimum mainly helps people who already have relevant prior learning or experience finish faster.[^s12]

The practical period is the training part of the apprenticeship, from your learning start date to your learning end date, and it excludes the end-point assessment that comes afterwards.[^s1]

## How many hours a week do I work, and how much of my week has to be training?

You will normally work at least 30 hours a week; that is what full-time apprenticeship funding is based on. Part-time is allowed, but the apprenticeship then usually takes longer.[^s1][^s7]

Off-the-job training, the actual learning of new skills, has changed, and this is the part most out-of-date websites get wrong:

- Old rule, for starts before 1 August 2022: at least 20% of your normal working hours had to be off-the-job training.[^s1]
- Middle rule, for starts from 1 August 2022 to 31 July 2025: at least 6 hours a week on average, which is 20% of a 30-hour week, calculated over the practical period.[^s1]
- Current rule, for starts from 1 August 2025: the 20% and 6-hours formula is gone. Each apprenticeship standard now has its own published minimum number of off-the-job training hours on the front of the standard. These published minimums vary widely by standard, and no apprenticeship can require fewer than 187 hours. That figure is 20% of 8 months, and after any reduction for prior learning it is the absolute floor.[^s1]

Off-the-job training must happen during your normal paid working hours, and must teach new knowledge and skills relevant to your standard. Things that do not count include your normal productive job, general company inductions, progress reviews, exams, and standalone English and maths qualifications.[^s1]

## Do I have to pass English and maths on an apprenticeship in England?

It depends on your age when you start.

If you start aged 16 to 18: yes, you must achieve English and maths to complete, unless you already hold an accepted equivalent such as GCSE grade 4 or C and above.[^s6] The exact rule in the 2025-26 funding rules is:

- On a level 2 apprenticeship, if you hold neither level 1 nor level 2, you must achieve at least level 1 (Functional Skills level 1 or GCSE grade 3 to 1), then work towards level 2 where there is time to make meaningful progress. You are not forced to sit the level 2 exam unless your provider judges you ready.[^s7]
- On a level 3 or higher apprenticeship, you must hold or achieve an approved level 2 in both English and maths before you can complete.[^s7]
- For 16 to 18 year olds, achieving the required English and maths must be fulfilled in order to pass gateway, the checkpoint before your end-point assessment.[^s7]

If you start aged 19 or over: since 11 February 2025 you no longer have to achieve English and maths to complete your apprenticeship. It became optional, and funding is still available if you and your employer agree to include it in your training plan.[^s6][^s7]

This 2025 change only affected adults aged 19 and over at the start. The rules for 16 to 18 year olds were not relaxed.[^s7]

## What is end-point assessment (EPA) on an apprenticeship in England?

End-point assessment is the final, independent test at the end of your apprenticeship. It checks that you can actually do the job to the level set out in your apprenticeship standard, and you cannot achieve your apprenticeship without passing it.[^s8]

Key points about EPA:

- Before you can take it you must pass "gateway", the point where your employer confirms you are ready and you have met any requirements. For 16 to 18 year olds that includes achieving English and maths.[^s7][^s8]
- It is delivered by an independent assessment organisation, historically called an end-point assessment organisation, separate from your employer and training provider. These are regulated by Ofqual, and your EPA counts as a regulated qualification.[^s8]
- It is usually graded, commonly fail, pass, merit or distinction, though grading varies by standard.[^s8]
- If you fail part of it, you can normally take a resit or retake, but you cannot resit just to push a pass up to a higher grade, and resits are often capped at a pass.[^s8]

Reform is under way, so verify against your own standard. In February 2025 the government published new Apprenticeship Assessment Principles. These allow assessment to happen on-programme, during the apprenticeship rather than only at the end, for some standards, and let training providers deliver and mark some elements of the assessment, with assessment organisations still assuring the results.[^s13] Ofqual published its decisions on the new regulatory framework in December 2025.[^s14] These changes are being rolled out standard by standard, so your current EPA rules stay in place until your specific standard's revised plan goes live.[^s13]

## Who runs apprenticeship standards in England now, IfATE or Skills England?

Skills England. The Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (IfATE) closed on 1 June 2025, and its functions transferred to the Secretary of State under the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (Transfer of Functions etc) Act 2025, which received Royal Assent on 15 May 2025.[^s5] Skills England now owns and maintains apprenticeship standards.[^s5]

One more moving part: responsibility for the apprenticeship funding rules has moved to the Department for Work and Pensions for the 2026-27 rules.[^s15]

> [!NOTE]
> Skills England was set up as an executive agency of the Department for Education, but it moved to the Department for Work and Pensions on 16 September 2025, taking apprenticeships, adult further education, skills, training and careers with it. See
> `apprenticeship-levy-and-skills-england.md` in this folder, which sources that move to the House of Commons Library briefing. Any description of Skills England as a DfE agency describes the position before that date.

## What's different in the 2026 to 2027 funding rules?

The 2026-27 funding rules apply to apprenticeships starting between 1 August 2026 and 31 July 2027.[^s15] The core rules covered here carry over: the 8-month minimum duration, standard-specific off-the-job training hours, the 187-hour floor, and the 16 to 18 requirement to achieve English and maths all still apply.[^s7][^s15] The main changes for 2026-27 are administrative. For example, the minimum off-the-job hours are now hosted on the Skills England website rather than in an annex, and there are tighter compliance and data rules for providers.[^s15]

> [!GAP]
> The apprentice minimum wage rate for April 2027 onwards is not yet confirmed as at September 2026; the £8.00 an hour figure is correct from 1 April 2026 until the next uprating.

> [!GAP]
> Because the EPA reforms roll out standard by standard through 2026, the exact assessment method, grading, and whether any assessment happens on-programme depends on your specific standard's current assessment plan. Check the Skills England page for your standard.

## What about Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

Everything above is England only. Apprenticeships are a devolved matter, so the other UK nations run separate systems with different rules, funding and terminology. Scotland uses Modern Apprenticeships, plus Foundation and Graduate Apprenticeships, administered by Skills Development Scotland. Wales and Northern Ireland use apprenticeship "frameworks" rather than England's "standards". If you are applying in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, do not rely on the England rules here; check your own nation's system.
