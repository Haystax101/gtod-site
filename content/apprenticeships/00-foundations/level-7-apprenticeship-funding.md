## In one line

From 1 January 2026, England only funds a Level 7 (master's-level) apprenticeship if you are aged 16 to 21 at the start of training, or aged 22 to 24 at the start with an EHC plan and/or care experience (funded to 25), and the Level 7 Solicitor apprenticeship is not specially exempt; it survives only because most solicitor apprentices are school leavers who fall inside that age rule.[^s1][^s3][^s2]

## Key facts

- The Department for Education announced the change on 27 May 2025; the funding rule took effect on 1 January 2026.[^s1][^s3]
- Public and levy funding for a new Level 7 apprenticeship now only applies if, at the start of training, the apprentice is aged between 16 and 21 years old, which is the same as saying under 22.[^s3]
- Extra exception: apprentices aged 22 to 24 at the start who have an EHC plan and/or have been or are in local-authority care are funded up to age 25.[^s3]
- Already started? Anyone who started a Level 7 apprenticeship before 1 January 2026 keeps funding through to completion, whatever their age.[^s3][^s5]
- No standard-specific exemption exists. The Level 7 Solicitor apprenticeship (ST0246) is protected only by the 16 to 21 age rule, not by a carve-out.[^s2][^s6][^s7]
- Employers can still commercially self-fund Level 7 for over-21s; the apprentice still pays no tuition fees and must still meet all apprenticeship rules.[^s13][^s14][^s17]
- A separate, later change defunded 16 lower-level standards (for example Team Leader Level 3 and Operations Manager Level 5) from September 2026. Do not confuse this with the Level 7 change.[^s12]

## What exactly changed for Level 7 apprenticeships, and from when?

On 27 May 2025 the Department for Education announced it was refocusing funding away from Level 7 (master's-level) apprenticeships from January 2026, while maintaining support for those aged 16 to 21 and for existing apprentices.[^s1] The rule took legal effect from 1 January 2026 through the apprenticeship funding rules.[^s3] Before this, a Level 7 apprenticeship (equivalent to a master's degree) could be levy-funded at any age. A Level 7 apprenticeship is the highest apprenticeship level in England.[^s11]

**GTOD take:** the date to remember is 1 January 2026. The 27 May 2025 date is only when it was announced; the funding switch happened on 1 January 2026.

## Who can still get funding for a Level 7 apprenticeship in England?

The DfE funding rules for 2025-26 (version 3) state that from 1 January 2026 an apprentice starting a Level 7 standard is only eligible for funding if, at the start of training, they are aged between 16 and 21 years old (paragraph 28.1), or aged between 22 and 24 years old with an EHC plan and/or care experience (paragraph 28.2).[^s3] The 2026-27 rules carry the same restriction (paragraphs 32.1 and 32.2) but drop the "from 1 January 2026" wording, because those rules only cover starts from 1 August 2026 anyway.[^s4]

What counts is your age on the day training starts, not your age later in the programme.[^s5][^s8]

Note on wording: the operative, numbered rule says "aged between 16 and 21 years old", while a summary note in the same 2025-26 document phrases it as "under the age of 22".[^s3] These mean the same thing, because 16 to 21 is the same as under 22. Some university and provider pages say funding is for "those aged 21 and under", or that over-21s lose funding. Again this is the same boundary, worded differently.[^s8]

## What about care leavers and those with an EHCP?

There is one extra carve-out. If you are aged 22 to 24 at the start of training and you have an Education, Health and Care (EHC) plan, and/or you have been or are in the care of your local authority, funding continues up to age 25.[^s3] The apprenticeship service confirms Level 7 is funded for young people aged 16 to 21, and under 25 for care leavers and those with an EHCP at the start of their apprenticeship.[^s5]

There is no additional carve-out for other groups. Campaigners, for example for people with criminal convictions, asked for one, but it was not granted.[^s15]

## If I already started a Level 7 apprenticeship, do I lose funding?

No. The 2025-26 funding rules state that any individual who started a Level 7 apprenticeship before 1 January 2026 will continue to be funded through to completion.[^s3] This protection is confirmed on the DfE apprenticeship service help page.[^s5] It applies regardless of age: someone aged 30 who started in, say, November 2025 keeps funding to the end.

## Is the Level 7 Solicitor apprenticeship (ST0246) exempt?

No specific exemption exists. The Skills England page for the Solicitor standard (ST0246) states the same rule as every other Level 7: from 1 January 2026 it is only government-funded for apprentices who at the start are aged 16 to 21, or aged under 25 with an EHC plan and/or care experience, and anyone who started before 1 January 2026 is funded to completion.[^s2] The SRA confirms the same restriction for solicitor apprenticeships.[^s9]

What actually happened: law firms, the City of London Law Society and its City Century initiative, and the Law Society lobbied hard against defunding. The government kept funding for 16 to 21 year olds, which the sector treated as a win; Colin Passmore, chair of the City of London Law Society, called it "a sensible, principled outcome and we are grateful that government has listened to the evidence, seen the impact, and acted."[^s10] The Law Society is still campaigning for the age limit to be lifted, with president Richard Atkinson urging the government to maintain apprenticeship funding for people over 21 and noting that the solicitor apprenticeship continues to be the only route outside university to qualify as a solicitor.[^s8] The Law Society reports that in 2023/24 more than 1,300 people started solicitor apprenticeships, 45% of them aged 20 to 24.[^s8]

**GTOD take:** be careful how you read "exemption". The solicitor route was not scrapped, but it did not get a special exemption from the rule either. It keeps funding only for people who start young, aged 16 to 21. Because most solicitor apprentices are 18-year-old school leavers, the route is largely intact, but a 22-year-old graduate hoping to use it now falls outside funding unless the employer pays.

## Did any other Level 7 standards get an exemption or transitional deal?

No. The government considered exempting a small group of Level 7 standards, including the advanced clinical practitioner used in the NHS and the chartered town planner used by councils, but decided against it. Skills England did not find a strong enough economic rationale to exempt a small group of Level 7 apprenticeship standards from defunding.[^s6][^s15] So senior leader, accountancy and taxation professional (ST0001), chartered town planner (ST0536), advanced clinical practitioner (ST0564) and the rest are all subject to the same 16 to 21 age rule.[^s6][^s16]

The only transitional protection is the standard one: existing apprentices who started before 1 January 2026 are funded to completion.[^s3] The RTPI notes government estimates that only around 17% of those currently on the Chartered Town Planner Level 7 apprenticeship would still be eligible under the new rules.[^s16]

Separate change, do not confuse it: in March 2026 the government announced that 16 mostly lower-level standards would be defunded no earlier than 1 September 2026, including Team Leader (Level 3, 12,670 starts in 2024-25 across 450 providers, only 80 of them aged under 19), Operations Manager (Level 5, 12,530 starts), Lead Practitioner in Adult Care (Level 4), Coaching Professional (Level 5) and the Chartered Manager Degree (Level 6).[^s12] That is a different policy from the Level 7 age cap.

## Can employers still pay for a Level 7 apprenticeship themselves?

Yes. Employers can commercially, or privately, self-fund a Level 7 apprenticeship for someone who no longer qualifies for government funding.[^s13][^s14] Two things stay true for the apprentice:

- The apprentice still pays no tuition fees, because apprenticeship rules forbid charging the apprentice for training or assessment.[^s14][^s17]
- The programme still has to meet all apprenticeship rules: a genuine job, off-the-job training, minimum duration, and a Skills England-approved standard.[^s14]

Employers also keep the Employer's National Insurance exemption for apprentices under 25, which BPP reports applies to commercially funded apprentices aged under 25 following an announcement from HMRC and the DfE.[^s13]

**GTOD take:** "self-funded" means the employer pays, not you. If someone over 21 wants a Level 7 apprenticeship after the change, the practical question is whether an employer is willing to fund it. Many will decide not to, because the levy no longer covers it.

## I'm 16 to 19, how does this affect me?

A school leaver usually cannot start a Level 7 apprenticeship straight out of school in most sectors, because Level 7 sits above Level 6 (degree level). But the Solicitor apprenticeship is the big exception you can start at 18, as a six-year Level 7 route that includes a law degree (LLB), the SQE and qualification as a solicitor, with no tuition fees.[^s2][^s9]

What the age rule means for you in plain terms:

- If you start a solicitor apprenticeship at 18 to 21, you are inside the 16 to 21 rule and it is government-funded.[^s2][^s3]
- If you take a long gap, do another degree first, or otherwise start at 22 or older, you fall outside funding unless you have an EHC plan or care background (funded to 25) or the employer self-funds.[^s3][^s13]
- Your age at the start is what is locked in. Starting at 20 and finishing at 26 is fine, because funding continues to completion once you have started.[^s5][^s8]

> [!GAP]
> The exact number of solicitor-apprentice starts by single year of age is not published by government; DfE only publishes apprenticeship starts in three age bands (under 19, 19 to 24, 25 and over).[^s11] So the precise share of solicitor apprentices who start at exactly 22 or older, and would now lose funding, is not stated in a primary source.
