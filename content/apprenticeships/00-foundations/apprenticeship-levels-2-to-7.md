## In one line

In England as at September 2026, apprenticeships run from Level 2 (GCSE standard) up to Level 7 (master's degree standard); you finish with a real occupational qualification, a full degree at Levels 6 and 7, and your training is paid for by government and employers for almost everyone under 25, the one big catch being that Level 7 government funding stopped for people aged 22 and over who start on or after 1 January 2026.[^s1][^s2][^s3]

## Key facts

- Apprenticeship levels map onto academic levels: Level 2 is GCSE standard, Level 3 is A level standard, Levels 4 to 5 are foundation degree and higher education, and Levels 6 to 7 are bachelor's and master's degrees.[^s1]
- To start any apprenticeship in England you must be 16 or over, live in England, and not be in full-time education.[^s1]
- As an apprentice you are an employee: you earn a wage, get holiday pay, and get paid time for training and study.[^s1]
- As at September 2026 the apprentice minimum wage is £8.00 per hour, up from £7.55, from 1 April 2026. It applies if you are under 19, or 19 and over and in the first year of your apprenticeship.[^s8][^s9]
- From 1 January 2026, government funding for Level 7 apprenticeships is only for those aged 16 to 21 at the start, and under 25 for care leavers or those with an EHCP. Those aged 22 and over get no government funding.[^s3]
- Anyone who started a Level 7 apprenticeship before 1 January 2026 keeps their funding to the end.[^s3]
- Foundation apprenticeships, new from August 2025, are Level 2 jobs with training lasting a minimum of 8 months, for young people aged 16 to 21, or up to 24 if a care leaver, prison leaver, or with an EHCP.[^s5][^s7]
- Since 11 February 2025, adult apprentices aged 19 and over at the start no longer have to pass English and maths qualifications to complete. Those aged 16 to 18 still must.[^s6]
- Since 1 August 2025 the minimum apprenticeship duration is 8 months, down from 12 months.[^s2][^s7]
- Skills England replaced the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (IfATE), which closed on 1 June 2025.[^s7][^s11]
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own separate apprenticeship systems with different names, levels and rules.[^s1][^s13][^s14][^s15]

## What are apprenticeship levels?

An apprenticeship level tells you how advanced the training is, using the same ladder as school and university qualifications. In England, GOV.UK groups apprenticeships like this: Foundation and Intermediate apprenticeships are Level 2 (GCSE standard), Advanced apprenticeships are Level 3 (A level standard), Higher apprenticeships are Levels 4, 5, 6 and 7 (foundation degree and above), and Degree apprenticeships are Levels 6 and 7 (bachelor's or master's degree).[^s1]

An apprenticeship is a real job. You are an employee earning a wage with holiday pay, working alongside experienced staff, with paid time for training and study.[^s1] Apprenticeships take from 8 months to 6 years depending on the type and level.[^s1]

> [!NOTE]
> GOV.UK's public "Become an apprentice" page describes training time as at least 20% of normal working hours. That formula was replaced for apprenticeships starting on or after 1 August 2025: each standard now publishes its own minimum number of off-the-job training hours, with an absolute floor of 187 hours. See `what-an-apprenticeship-legally-is.md` in this folder, which sources the current rule to the DfE off-the-job training guidance. The 20% figure remains a reasonable rule of thumb for what a week looks like, but it is no longer the legal test.

## What is a Level 2 apprenticeship equivalent to?

A Level 2 apprenticeship in England is academically equivalent to GCSEs, per GOV.UK's official levels table.[^s1] It is the entry rung of the ladder.

You finish with a Level 2 occupational qualification, the apprenticeship standard for your job role. Some also give an extra qualification such as a diploma.[^s1]

## What is a Level 3 apprenticeship equivalent to?

A Level 3, or Advanced, apprenticeship in England is academically equivalent to A levels, per GOV.UK.[^s1] This is the level many school leavers with GCSEs start at.

You finish with a Level 3 occupational qualification for your role.[^s1]

## What are Levels 4 and 5 apprenticeships?

Levels 4 and 5 are Higher apprenticeships. GOV.UK places Levels 4 to 7 at foundation degree and above.[^s1] Level 4 is broadly the standard of the first year of a degree or a Higher National Certificate; Level 5 is broadly a foundation degree or Higher National Diploma standard.

You finish with a Level 4 or Level 5 qualification, which can include higher education qualifications such as an HNC, HND or foundation degree.[^s1]

## What is a Level 6 (degree) apprenticeship?

A Level 6 degree apprenticeship in England is academically equivalent to a bachelor's degree, per GOV.UK's levels table.[^s1] You work for an employer and study part-time towards a full degree.

You finish with a full bachelor's degree, for example a BA (Hons) or BSc (Hons), and you pay no tuition fees because the training is funded.[^s1] Level 6 and Level 7 degree apprenticeships are the fastest-growing part of the system: degree apprenticeship starts rose from 50,110 in 2023/24 to 60,350 in 2024/25, according to Department for Education statistics published in November 2025.[^s18]

## What is a Level 7 (master's) apprenticeship?

A Level 7 apprenticeship in England is academically equivalent to a master's degree, per GOV.UK's levels table.[^s1] It is the highest apprenticeship level. Examples include accountancy, law (solicitor) and senior healthcare roles.

You finish with a master's-level qualification, and often a professional qualification too.

The big change: from 1 January 2026, government funding for Level 7 apprenticeships only goes to people aged 16 to 21 at the start, plus under-25 care leavers or those with an EHCP. Those aged 22 and over get no government funding and must be funded by their employer commercially, or self-fund.[^s3] This hits the current Level 7 population hard: DfE data reported by FE Week and cited by HEPI on 21 October 2025 shows 89% of Level 7 apprentices are currently aged over 22.[^s19] Anyone who started before 1 January 2026 keeps funding to completion.[^s3]

## What are foundation apprenticeships?

Foundation apprenticeships are new Level 2 jobs with training in England, first available from 1 August 2025.[^s5][^s7] They are a paid, employed stepping stone into a Level 3 or higher apprenticeship or further training.

Key facts as at September 2026: they are for young people aged 16 to 21, or up to 24 if you are a care leaver, prison leaver, or have an EHCP; they have a minimum duration of 8 months; and there are no specific qualifications needed to apply.[^s5][^s16] Employers can receive up to £2,000 for taking one on, paid in three instalments tied to the apprentice progressing.[^s5] The first sectors were construction and the built environment, engineering and manufacturing, health and social care, and digital; catering and hospitality and retail were added from April 2026.[^s2]

Foundation apprenticeships in England are different from the Scottish and Welsh programmes also called "foundation apprenticeships". See the devolved nations section.

## What are the typical entry requirements for each level?

There are no single national entry requirements. Each employer sets its own for each vacancy. To start any apprenticeship in England you must be 16 or over, live in England, and not be in full-time education.[^s1] If you already have relevant qualifications or experience you may be able to train for less time.[^s1]

As a rough guide based on what employers commonly ask for:

- Foundation apprenticeship (Level 2): no specific qualifications required.[^s16]
- Level 2 (Intermediate): usually no formal qualifications; open to those able to complete the course.
- Level 3 (Advanced): employers commonly ask for around five GCSEs at grade 4 or C and above, or a completed Level 2 apprenticeship.
- Levels 4 to 5 (Higher): employers commonly ask for Level 3 qualifications such as A levels, a BTEC National, or a completed Advanced apprenticeship.
- Levels 6 to 7 (Degree): employers commonly ask for A levels, often three, plus around five GCSEs including maths and English, or a relevant Level 3 or higher apprenticeship, plus any specific grades the university or employer sets.

> [!GAP]
> The specific GCSE and A level thresholds above, such as "five GCSEs at grade 4 and above" or "three A levels", are drawn from careers-guidance and provider websites, not a single GOV.UK rule, because entry requirements are set per vacancy by employers. Always check the individual vacancy on the Find an Apprenticeship service for the real requirements.

## What qualification do I finish with at each level?

You finish an apprenticeship with a nationally recognised occupational qualification for your job role at that level, and some apprenticeships add an extra qualification such as a diploma.[^s1] Mapping to academic outcomes, per GOV.UK: Level 2 is GCSE standard; Level 3 is A level standard; Levels 4 to 5 are foundation degree and higher education standard; Level 6 is a bachelor's degree; Level 7 is a master's degree.[^s1] At Levels 6 and 7 the degree is a full university degree, identical to one earned by a full-time student.[^s1]

## Do I have to do English and maths?

It depends on your age at the start. Since 11 February 2025, if you are aged 19 or over when you start, you no longer have to hold or pass separate English and maths qualifications to complete your apprenticeship; you instead show these skills through your work.[^s6] If you are aged 16 to 18 at the start, you still have to work towards and achieve English and maths qualifications.[^s6] Funding for English and maths continues for 16 to 18 year olds, and for adults where the employer agrees it is needed.[^s6] For foundation apprenticeships specifically, 16 to 18 year olds who do not already hold a Level 1 or Level 2 English and maths qualification must study towards it.[^s5]

## Who pays for my apprenticeship, and does that differ by level or age?

You do not pay for your apprenticeship training. There are no tuition fees and no student loan.[^s1] Training and assessment are paid for by a mix of employers and government. Large employers, with an annual pay bill over £3 million, pay the apprenticeship levy at 0.5% of their pay bill, and that money helps fund training across the system, including for smaller employers.[^s2] The levy becomes the Growth and Skills Levy, which can also fund shorter apprenticeship units and foundation apprenticeships.[^s2]

By age: from 1 August 2026 the government fully funds apprenticeship training for all eligible 16 to 24 year olds at both levy-paying and non-levy-paying employers.[^s2] This drive is backed by an apprenticeship budget the GOV.UK press release of 27 May 2025 describes as a record-breaking £3 billion; FE Week reported the figure as £3.075 billion for 2025-26, up 13% from £2.729 billion in 2024-25.[^s4]

By level: each apprenticeship is placed in one of 30 funding bands ranging from £1,500 to £27,000, which cap how much government or levy money can go towards its training. The one level where your age can now block funding is Level 7.[^s2][^s3]

You always earn a wage on top: at least the apprentice minimum wage of £8.00 per hour as at September 2026, rising to your full age-related minimum wage once you are 19 or over and past your first year.[^s8][^s9]

## What changed with Level 7 funding, and does it affect me if I'm under 22?

The change, confirmed by the Department for Education and effective from 1 January 2026: Level 7 apprenticeships stay government-funded only for people aged 16 to 21 at the start, plus under-25s who are care leavers or have an EHCP. People aged 22 and over at the start get no government funding.[^s3] It was announced on 27 May 2025 as part of a package promising 120,000 new training opportunities, including an additional 30,000 apprenticeship starts across this Parliament, by rebalancing spending towards younger and lower-level learners.[^s4]

If you are 16 to 21, this does not stop you: you remain eligible for Level 7 government funding.[^s3] If you started a Level 7 before 1 January 2026 at any age, your funding continues to completion.[^s3]

**GTOD take:** if you are aiming for a Level 7 route such as the solicitor or accountancy apprenticeship and you are a teenager now, watch the clock. If your path runs Level 3, then Level 6, then Level 7, plan the timing so you start the Level 7 before you turn 22, or you could lose government funding. A four-year degree apprenticeship or a gap year can push you over 22 by the time you would start Level 7.

For employers who commercially fund a Level 7 apprentice aged under 25, there is a long-standing separate benefit: employers do not pay employer (secondary Class 1) National Insurance on apprentices under 25 earning up to the upper secondary threshold, a rule in force since April 2016, based on the apprentice's age and status rather than on whether the training is government-funded.[^s12]

## How long does each level take?

Overall, apprenticeships take from 8 months to 6 years depending on type and level.[^s1] Since 1 August 2025, the minimum duration for any apprenticeship is 8 months, where it used to be 12 months.[^s2][^s7] Foundation apprenticeships have a minimum duration of 8 months.[^s5]

As a rough guide, and durations are set per standard rather than by law: Level 2 and Level 3 apprenticeships commonly run 1 to 2 years; Higher apprenticeships at Levels 4 to 5 commonly 2 years or more; degree apprenticeships at Levels 6 to 7 commonly 3 to 6 years.

> [!GAP]
> The per-level duration ranges above are typical figures from careers-guidance sources, not a single GOV.UK rule. The exact typical duration is published on each individual apprenticeship standard on the Skills England website. Only the 8-month legal minimum is a hard rule.[^s2]

## Do apprenticeship levels work the same in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

No. Apprenticeships are devolved, so each nation runs its own system. This document's rules, meaning foundation apprenticeships, the Level 7 funding change, the English and maths change, and the Growth and Skills Levy, are England rules. The exception is the apprentice minimum wage of £8.00, which is UK-wide.[^s1][^s8] GOV.UK directs you to separate bodies for each nation.[^s1]

Scotland: three types. Foundation Apprenticeships are done in school in S5 or S6, at SCQF Level 6, equivalent to a Higher; Modern Apprenticeships are the main earn-while-you-learn route; and Graduate Apprenticeships go up to master's level, SCQF Level 11.[^s14][^s17] Note that "Foundation Apprenticeship" means something completely different in Scotland, a school subject, than in England, where it is a Level 2 job.[^s14]

Wales: four types. Foundation Apprenticeship at Level 2, Apprenticeship at Level 3, Higher Apprenticeship at Levels 4 to 5, and Degree Apprenticeship at Level 6.[^s13] Note that "Foundation Apprenticeship" in Wales is a Level 2 apprenticeship, like England's Intermediate, and is not the same as either the English or Scottish version.[^s13]

Northern Ireland: delivered through ApprenticeshipsNI at Levels 2 and 3, and Higher Level Apprenticeships at Levels 4 to 7, most at Level 5, which are equivalent to an HNC, foundation degree or beyond.[^s15] You generally must be employed or about to be employed in Northern Ireland and work a minimum of 21 hours per week.[^s15]
