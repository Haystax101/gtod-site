One subject. Every league table. One ranking.

Nobody should pick a university off a single league table. They disagree with each other constantly, they measure different things, and the one that put your course 4th put someone else's 40th for reasons it never explains. So we put them side by side, blend them into one ranking, and let you change what counts. Drag the weights, and watch the order move.

How these are built

Each ranking takes every major league table that covers the subject — typically the Complete University Guide, the Guardian, QS and THE — and rescales each input to a 0–100 score within the pool of universities being compared. Those scores are then combined as a weighted average, which is what the sliders change.

Nothing here is a verdict. A ranking is a starting point for a shortlist, not a reason to choose or reject a place. Go to the open day, read the actual module list, and talk to someone doing the course. Then use this to argue with yourself.

Why not just use one table?

Because they measure different things and don't agree. Entry standards reward selectivity, not teaching. Research scores measure what staff publish, not what you'll be taught. International tables are reputation surveys, which favour big old institutions. Each is defensible on its own terms and useless on its own. Seeing all four at once is the only honest way to read them.
